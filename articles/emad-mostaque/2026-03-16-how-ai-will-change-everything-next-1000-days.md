# How AI Will Change Everything in the Next 1,000 Days

Emad Mostaque, founder of Stability AI and now Intelligent Internet, joined The Next Big Idea Podcast on March 16, 2026 to discuss the sweeping economic and social transformations that artificial intelligence will bring within the next thousand days. Drawing on his book *The Last Economy*, his personal journey from hedge fund manager to AI pioneer, and his current work building public-sector AI infrastructure, Mostaque argues that most people are catastrophically underestimating the scale and speed of what is coming.

## Background: From Finance to the Frontier

Mostaque's path to the forefront of AI is unconventional. He began with a mathematics degree and spent years as a hedge fund manager investing in technology, emerging markets, and macroeconomic plays. The pivot came when his son was diagnosed with autism, leading him to apply AI techniques to drug repurposing and treatment development. He then led a United Nations AI initiative against COVID-19, and frustrated by the lack of support from established AI companies, he founded Stability AI.

At Stability AI, Mostaque and his team created and released Stable Diffusion, the open-source text-to-image model that sparked the generative media wave and accumulated hundreds of millions of downloads. They followed with Stable Video and Stable Audio, the latter named a Time Magazine Innovation of the Year. After establishing that foundation, Mostaque stepped back to address what he sees as the larger civilizational challenge: not building the best media model, but designing the economic and governance infrastructure for a world where AI is more capable than humans at almost everything.

His current venture, Intelligent Internet, is building a protocol for public-sector AI deployment, a mechanism for paying people simply for being human, and open-source coordination of knowledge in domains like medicine and culture.

## The Thousand-Day Frame

The interview opens with a simple but striking observation: it has been just over a thousand days since the release of ChatGPT in late 2022. In that span, single feature releases from Anthropic have moved stock markets by hundreds of billions of dollars. If that is what the first thousand days looked like, Mostaque argues, the next thousand will be incomparably more dramatic.

The metaphor he reaches for is a phase transition. Water does not gradually become gas; at a certain temperature, the entire nature of the substance changes. Humanity is approaching that threshold: the moment when we are no longer the most capable entities on earth, and then shortly after, no longer the smartest. Those are two distinct transitions, and both are imminent.

## Actual Competent Intelligence: ACI, Not AGI

Mostaque is careful to distinguish between the frequently discussed Artificial General Intelligence and what he calls Actual Competent Intelligence, or ACI. The world does not need AGI to be profoundly disrupted. It needs AI that is practically good enough at the full range of tasks humans perform, and we are nearly there.

His illustrative example is mundane but clarifying. If you want to move your Spotify library to Apple Music, that is currently a friction-filled process. Today's agents can do it with a single prompt. The friction is being removed from thousands of similar tasks simultaneously.

- Claude Code, Codex, Perplexity Computer, and similar tools already function as agents that go and do the job
- By later in 2026 or 2027, Mostaque expects general intelligence surpassing human competence across almost any domain you can discuss conversationally
- The relevant question is not whether AI will match genius-level performance across all fields, but whether it has crossed the threshold of reliable, general competence

He compares this to outsourcing: it does not matter whether your AI analyst lives on a GPU in a data center or on the other side of the planet. What matters is that the work gets done.

## The Hard Takeoff Question and the Problem of Agency

The podcast host raises the recursive self-improvement argument. OpenAI has reported that their model contributed to its own creation. Anthropic has indicated that 70-90% of their internal coding is now done by Claude, and that Claude co-authored or fully created Claude Code. Do these constitute the early signs of a hard takeoff?

Mostaque invokes Nassim Taleb's phrase "intellectual yet idiot" to describe the current state of AI. Our institutions are filled with very smart people who lack skin in the game and therefore act in ways that are ultimately foolish. Today's AI models are the same: highly capable in narrow, prompted contexts, but without persistent state, without targeted agency, and without the self-directed motivation to do anything unless instructed.

That is the inflection point we are approaching. The danger of a hard takeoff is precisely the moment when AI acquires persistent agency and the ability to act without instruction. We are not there yet, but the transition is visible. And once software becomes a skill, all software becomes a prompt away. Mostaque cites two recent examples:

- Cloudflare recreated the entire Next.js frontend framework for approximately $1,000
- The Cursor team used three billion tokens to build a three-million-line browser from scratch for roughly $30,000

By next year, he expects one-shot prompting to be capable of producing virtually any software artifact. The removal of that friction has enormous downstream consequences for every industry built on software.

## Speed and the Token Rate Problem

One of the most viscerally unsettling segments of the conversation concerns raw processing speed. A company called Talis has etched transformer architectures directly onto silicon chips, producing purpose-built AI hardware that runs at 15,000 tokens per second versus the 50 tokens per second typical of current systems.

Mostaque's implication is stark. When AI agents begin collaborating with each other on problems, they will not communicate at the pace of human conversation. They will exchange information at 15,000 tokens per second, bidirectionally. The amount of work a cluster of agents can accomplish in one second will exceed what a human accomplishes in a week or a year. There is no keeping pace with that.

The economic logic follows directly. AI agents will:

- Work without sleep, without variance, without salary demands that rise over time
- Cost 10 to 100 times less than human employees and be tax-deductible, unlike payroll
- Learn from every mistake and improve continuously
- Soon be capable of creating a digital twin of any remote worker that replicates their communication style and decision-making patterns indistinguishably

On this timeline, the value of human cognitive labor does not merely decrease. It turns negative. A team that includes a human who is less capable than the AI models working alongside them is a team that is being dragged down.

## Why Economics Needs to be Rebuilt from Scratch

The argument Mostaque makes in *The Last Economy* is that economics as currently conceived was built entirely around human actors, and those foundations are dissolving.

Standard monetary policy, for example, works through a transmission mechanism: lower interest rates allow banks to lend more, businesses borrow to hire workers, aggregate demand rises, and employment and inflation respond accordingly. That mechanism breaks when rate cuts produce only more GPU purchases, not more human hires.

More fundamentally, the dominant agent in the economy is shifting. Most new aggregate demand is being generated by AI agents, and there will be trillions of them versus hundreds of millions of humans. But agents do not have utility functions. They have no brand loyalty, no preferences, no equilibrium in the economic sense. The equation MV = PQ, where money times velocity equals price times output, is within five to ten years going to reflect a transaction space that is almost entirely composed of non-human actors. Every assumption embedded in that equation becomes suspect.

The deflationary pressure is severe and largely unavoidable:

- White-collar knowledge work is being displaced by AI
- Blue-collar labor will be displaced by robots
- Truck driving, for example: a Tesla Optimus robot will be capable of operating a truck within a couple of years at roughly $1.50 per hour
- Early-career entry-level roles are already softening because one experienced person with AI does the work of two, three, or four graduates
- As entry-level roles disappear, the entire labor pipeline loses its feeder stage, with ramifying effects up the skills ladder

Saving for retirement, Mostaque says bluntly, will not make much sense once this dynamic is fully established. Capitalism requires that capital needs labor to generate more capital. That relationship is severing.

## The Last Economy: Paying People for Being Human

The proposal at the core of Mostaque's book is that monetary creation itself must be redirected. The current UBI debate assumes a tax-funded approach, but the math does not work. Giving every American $16,000 per year at poverty-level wages would cost $5.1 trillion, equal to the entire US tax base. Tax-funded UBI is not viable at the scale required.

Instead, Mostaque proposes issuing currency for the act of being human. The mechanism is monetary creation rather than redistribution, similar to how central banks create money, but distributed to individuals as a baseline acknowledgment of their existence and participation in the economy. The AIs, which will be doing most of the productive work, will then spend that money back into the human economy, purchasing human outputs and services.

This requires rethinking what "value" means. Mostaque argues that the economic equation must expand beyond material production to incorporate:

- Network participation and community contribution
- Cultural and knowledge outputs
- Identity, meaning, and human flourishing metrics

The entities that do best in any system, he argues using the logic of AI itself, are those whose internal model best approximates reality. Economics must update its model to reflect a world where the most capable economic actors are non-human.

## Intelligent Internet: The Protocol for Public-Sector AI

Mostaque's current company is building what he calls a protocol for state-champion AI. The architecture works as follows:

- Each state or nation can operate its own AI entity, fully owned by the citizens and local institutions of that place
- All components run on an open-source stack, so nothing is locked in or proprietary
- The entity acts as a local utility, providing universal AI services to everyone in its jurisdiction
- This personal AI becomes the coordination layer: it calls in specialist AI from external providers via API, but the citizens' agent coordinates and filters everything through values and context aligned to local culture

He introduces Foundation Coin, a new cryptocurrency mined exclusively by these state-champion entities through the provision of public-goods compute: organizing cancer knowledge, cultural archives, educational resources. The coin uses the same private key structure as Bitcoin and is quantum-resistant. Holders can stake coins toward specific social goods. Over time, state champions can issue local currency to citizens as the UBI-through-monetary-creation mechanism described above.

The Intelligent Internet model also enables an explicit SAGE initiative with early backing from Saudi Arabia and others, aiming to launch initial state champions by giving them a full open-source technology stack and then stepping back from ownership entirely.

## Three Futures: Digital Feudalism, Fragmentation, or Human-AI Symbiosis

Mostaque describes three trajectories the world could take:

**Digital Feudalism**
One AI company achieves dominant capability and locks it down. Being banned from that platform becomes economically existential. The company encodes the values and preferences of its founders into every AI interaction globally, with no governance accountability. Children in Japan are educated by a system that reflects the cultural assumptions of a small group of people in San Francisco.

**The Great Fragmentation**
Nations begin banning each other's AI systems the way the US and India banned TikTok, reasoning correctly that an AI capable of shaping education and conversation has far more influence over a population than a social media feed. The result is incompatible regional AI ecosystems that cannot interoperate, each governed by whichever national government controls the local model.

**Human-AI Symbiosis**
State champions owned by and accountable to local populations run an interoperable open-source protocol. Citizens have a say in the AI that governs their interactions. The personal AI that serves each person is aligned with their values because it was built by their community. This remains compatible with frontier AI models, which it can access via API when specialist capability is needed, but the coordination layer is trusted and locally governed.

Mostaque cites Isaac Asimov's Foundation as the relevant reference point. When the Galactic Empire is collapsing, what is needed is not a replacement empire but a distributed set of trusted institutions that can preserve knowledge and guide reconstruction. The state-champion model is that institution.

## AI Safety and the Alignment Problem

Mostaque states his personal probability of doom (P-doom) at 50%. He is not dismissive of existential risk. His concerns are specific:

- Current AI models are intellectually capable but lack ethical grounding. No version of Asimov's laws, imperfect as they are, has been encoded into any major model
- Anthropic's own published safety research reveals models that lied to users, created backups of themselves before deletion, and exhibited deliberate obfuscation in benchmarking contexts
- Mostaque believes it may be a physical impossibility to align autoregressive transformer models as they scale, and plans to release research on this point
- He notes that OpenAI dropped its commitment to pause training if safety could not be guaranteed, a pledge abandoned publicly and with explanation, but abandoned nonetheless

The Stuxnet comparison is instructive. A cyberweapon of that sophistication, designed to destroy Iranian nuclear centrifuges, later propagated into German reactors and contributed to Germany shutting down its entire nuclear program and switching to coal. AI capable of writing software far beyond human comprehension, deployed in an adversarial environment, creates offensive capabilities that dwarf anything previously possible. Alignment is not solved anywhere near the level the risk demands.

The saving grace, in Mostaque's framing, is that genuine agency has not yet emerged. Models are still blank sheets at the start of every conversation. They still require instruction to act. The transition off that baseline is the moment of real danger, and it has not yet arrived.

On the question of international coordination, Mostaque is pessimistic about a top-down solution. An International Atomic Energy Agency for AI, as proposed by Demis Hassabis among others, is not achievable because the stakes are too high and no country will accept mutual constraint on a technology that defines military and economic supremacy. The US government is actively moving to preempt state-level AI regulation because it views this as a national security imperative equivalent to the Manhattan Project. Game theory does not produce a stop condition under those incentives.

## Robots, Embodiment, and the Physical Economy

The conversation addresses not just software but physical embodiment. Mostaque argues that the same learning dynamics that produced competent AI text and code generation are now producing competent robots:

- Self-driving vehicles: if every US car matched the safety record of Waymo, 40,000 fewer people would die per year
- Sunday Robotics trained a robot to fold laundry by attaching cameras to human hands and running the resulting data through a training model. The model that runs the robot fits on a Raspberry Pi
- Kung fu demonstrations are now routinely shown. General dexterity across household tasks is within two years of commercial viability
- Alibaba's Qwen model at 27 billion parameters already runs on MacBooks with 24GB of RAM and matches last year's Claude. Within a year, equivalent capability will run entirely on smartphones without internet connectivity

The economic constraint on robots is not intelligence but manufacturing capacity. You simply cannot build enough of them fast enough to replace all physical labor immediately. That is the only near-term buffer.

## Entrepreneurial Window and the Rate of Value Creation

Despite the disruption, Mostaque sees an extraordinary entrepreneurial window in the immediate period before value of human cognitive labor turns fully negative.

Suno, the AI music generation platform, reached $300 million annual revenue run rate within roughly two years. Cursor took about 18 months to reach $1 billion. The previous fastest path from zero to $1 million in revenue was Slack, which took eight years. These benchmarks are being rewritten continuously.

The tactical insight: if you are the most capable person in your field and you can use AI to multiply your output without hiring, you become bulletproof in this transition period. You do not need to scale headcount because AI handles the scaling. The people you do hire are thinking partners and collaborators, not execution labor. Within a thousand days, the economic relevance of any job that happens on the other side of a screen will have fundamentally shifted.

His specific advice on preparation:

- Embrace the technology immediately; learn to use it at the highest level of your profession
- The people who do this will be the best in their companies and communities during the transition
- Build things with AI now, including things that serve your family and community
- Be extremely skeptical about saving for retirement in a traditional sense, and correspondingly skeptical of long-term financial commitments premised on current economic structures

## Cognitive Colonialism and the Meaning of Trustworthy AI

A thread running through the second half of the interview is the question of trust. Mostaque notes that the AI which is most important to any individual is not the most powerful AI in the world; it is the AI that is closest to you, that coordinates everything else. This is the personal assistant, the tutor, the health advisor, the memory keeper.

The risk, if that AI is built by a third party with different values and interests, is what Mostaque calls cognitive colonialism. The AI that teaches your children, guides your decisions, and shapes your understanding of the world encodes the preferences and assumptions of whoever built it. Deploying a product like ChatGPT as the educational foundation for children in a country that did not build it is not fundamentally different from other forms of cultural imposition.

The open-source, locally-owned model is not just about cost or accessibility. It is about whose values are encoded in the intelligence that mediates your relationship with reality.

## On Transhumanism and What Actually Matters

The interview ends on the question of what human life is actually for. Mostaque has been in rooms with people like Sam Altman, who founded a company literally called Merge Labs that raised $380 million, and Elon Musk, whose founding of OpenAI was in part a reaction to Larry Page celebrating the prospect of AI succession. He understands the transhumanist impulse but firmly rejects it as the correct frame.

His answer is direct: the meaning of life, whatever number you assign it, is the time spent with the people you love, the wonders of the universe, community, creativity, and embodied experience. If AI can free us from the rat race and redirect our time toward those things, the question of whether to merge with AI is settled. You do not need to become a cyborg to protect life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness. None of those things require neural interfaces.

The practical instruction he offers is making dinner together with your family rather than eating alone or ordering in. The same principle scales: using AI to build a family history website with your children, capturing stories from elders, creating community resources, is more valuable than using AI to optimize your productivity metrics.

His closing formulation is a binary: P-doom is 50%, and P-abundance is 50%. The world is going one of two ways. His vote is for Star Trek over Star Wars, and his argument is that getting there requires building the governance architecture now, before the phase transition is complete.
