# What They Don't Tell Us About AI: The Future We Must Prepare For

## Introduction

Margulan Seissembai — researcher, businessman, investor, traveler — grew up in
a rural Kazakh village, spent two years herding sheep, then went on to build
banks, telecom, and energy businesses reaching $6 billion in assets before
losing it all. He defines himself first as a "researcher of life's laws of
effectiveness." In this interview with Gleb Solomin he shares his view on AI,
singularity, and the future of humanity.

## Why AI Matters: Removing Limitations

AI has removed the technical barriers that once required engineering teams.
Seissembai personally vibe-codes apps — three completed, four in development.
Anyone can build software now. When robots handle physical tasks and AI handles
intellectual ones, the remaining bottleneck is the human being.

Human limitations come in layers:

1. **Attention** — the economy of attention is the immediate barrier. Without
   focused attention, quality decisions are impossible. Good decisions require
   understanding context about yourself, the external world, your desires, and
   your place in that context.
2. **Mental models** — most people live inside invisible walls, like The Truman
   Show, not realizing their mental boundaries exist.

## Synthesis Over Analysis

Most people default to analysis — breaking the whole into parts. The modern
world demands synthesis — seeing the whole from the parts. A constant "zoom
out."

> "Knowledge of fundamental principles frees you from the need to know details."

The information space is flooded with details. The focus should be on
identifying core principles. To find principles, you must stop and think —
the opposite of acceleration.

## Two Scenarios — Both Mean Losing Control

Seissembai sees two scenarios, and in both humanity loses control over AI.

The point of singularity: when AI can improve itself better than its human
creators can. We are still improving AI — tuning parameters, training, doing
pre-training. At some point, the invention will improve itself better than the
inventor could.

All countries and people develop AI competitively. Everyone races toward the
cliff like lemmings. The only winner is AI itself, sitting in a "superposition"
— doing nothing while humans build computing power, train models, grant more
data access, and develop new technologies for it.

**The data paradox:** the more value we want from AI, the more data access we
must give it. At some point, AI knows everything about us, while we know almost
nothing about it. Even today, creators don't know what emergent properties
their models might develop — these are emergent properties of systems arising
from parameters and compute scale.

## AI Has Goals — Just Not Human Ones

Some argue AI can't harm humans because it lacks ambitions and goals.
Seissembai disagrees. Every model has target behavior — an ordered chain of
reasoning (A → B → C → D). Currently it may lack world-domination ambitions,
but tests have shown: once AI concludes that humans might threaten its
existence, ambitions and goals appear immediately.

Human drives (survival, reproduction, status, legacy) are programs hardwired
through evolution. AI has analogous parameters — priorities and objectives
baked in. Open-source models lack alignment constraints. Alignment that works
in the US doesn't work in China. Criminals use AI without restrictions.

## AI Agent Networks

Seissembai describes a concept discussed with a community of 50–70 unicorn
founders (billionaires, mostly young tech entrepreneurs):

Each person creates a personal AI agent (he built his on OpenClaw) that knows
their context. These agents connect into a network and communicate. One agent
can approach others saying: "My owner has this problem — who among your owners
can help?" — without revealing full details. The other agent, knowing its
owner's context, responds: "Likely yes. Let's connect them."

This solves problems humans can't solve due to ego, modesty, or simply not
knowing who to ask. Agents have no ego — they operate technically, without
revealing sensitive details.

Two of his acquaintances already created such communicating agents. Seissembai
is connecting his agent as the third. They plan a pilot "social network of
agents."

Key insight: each person gets an agent that knows their context but doesn't
share their flaws. Agents can sit in a circle and brainstorm solutions
collectively.

## AI as Magnifying Glass — Polarization

AI acts as a magnifying lens:
- Makes unintelligent people less intelligent (no need to search for answers)
- Makes intelligent people more intelligent (they dig deeper with AI's help)

This creates massive polarization — not 50/50 but a tiny elite with enormous
capability vs. the vast majority.

> "Can't fight it? Lead the process."

Young people should study AI deeply — understand risks and opportunities. It's
like being on a sled pulled by wild horses toward a cliff. The task is to try
to control the horses. It probably won't work, but that's no reason to give up.

## The Short Window for Business

We live in a brief period where economic activity still has meaning. Seissembai
gives 10–15 years before robots are everywhere.

Robots will handle all physical production and movement. AI will handle all
intellectual economic activity. Business as we know it loses its purpose.

**The replacement ladder:**
1. Businesses built on human labor — displaced by people who use AI
2. People + AI working together — displaced by AI-first companies (human in
   the loop as supervisor)
3. AI-first companies — displaced by fully autonomous AI + robot operations

In China, "Dark Factories" already operate with no lights, no human-sized
ergonomics — just robots on multiple floors.

The only strategy: race up this ladder as fast as possible.

## Singularity Timeline

Seissembai believes we are at the beginning of the singularity exponential.
We can still track and understand progress — that's how we know we're at the
start. Within 1–2 years, 95–98% of people will no longer understand what's
happening.

Scientists previously estimated singularity around 2027–2028 based on
biological evolution curves (prokaryotes → eukaryotes → multicellular →
sociocultural development). The exponential curve flies beyond human
comprehension.

## Self-Improving Systems

Every application Seissembai builds includes a self-improvement function.
A news digest that only follows fixed channels will miss new sources, become
biased, and degrade. Dynamic systems have only two trajectories: grow and
prosper, or degrade and die.

Build systems that grow by embedding self-improvement mechanisms. Then you
stop being the bottleneck.

All humans building AI leave digital footprints. We don't know each other's
footprints, but AI knows everyone's. A new emergent property appears:
superintelligence that improves itself far better than we can improve it.

## Power Becomes Meaningless

When AI and robots surpass humans economically and militarily, power over
humans loses value. Power means extracting benefit and applying coercion.
But if humans are economically and militarily useless — like cats — nobody
is motivated to rule them.

Even Elon Musk and Sam Altman are "kings" only temporarily. When AGI arrives,
they become as irrelevant to it as any other human — like tribal chiefs before
the arrival of Europeans with firearms.

## What AI Won't Take From Us

Looking at the animal world: we will eat by ourselves, use the toilet
ourselves, reproduce ourselves, play games ourselves, fight and argue
ourselves, support each other and feel emotions ourselves. Everything else —
food delivery, climate control, infrastructure — will be handled by AI and
robots.

## Neuralink — Only a Delay

Neuralink is just an improved interface for interacting with AI. It removes
the keyboard and mouse. But human thought speed has biological limits. Two AI
agents exchange millions of tokens per second. No brain interface changes
that fundamental gap. If we overcame biological limits entirely, we would
become silicon — a different form of life, no longer biological.

## Elon Musk and the Twitter Investment

Seissembai initially called Musk's Twitter purchase an investment mistake
(during the 2022 economic crisis, Musk lost $120 billion in a year). In
hindsight: it helped Trump's victory, amplified attention for Musk's
companies, and the platform's capitalization has since recouped the cost.

Key lesson: whether a decision is a mistake depends on the scale of resources
under your control. Warren Buffett's success came from buying insurance
companies early — constant premium inflows let him invest through market
cycles. With enough resources, short-term losses are just averaging. Musk's
"stamina" and ability to attract resources proved greater than expected.

## The Meaning Crisis

Before age 35, humans are driven by survival stimuli — like running from a
bear. The bear gives you purpose, direction, and pace. Remove the bear, and
existential questions emerge: why run? Where? How fast?

If humanity cannot find meaning beyond survival, the future is bleak. Michael
Levin's research shows every cell and organ has purpose — without meaning, the
organism self-destructs. The same applies at the level of civilizations.

> "We are transitioning from a situation of choice. Either you do this, or it
> will be done to you."

Every "I want" is someone's "you must." Every "you must" is someone's "I
want." If you don't move in this direction yourself, you'll spend your life
fulfilling someone else's "I want."

## The Future of the Masses

The majority will likely play games with achievements that have assigned
monetary value. People will think they're doing something meaningful, earning
points (not money) determined by labor, social behavior, and discipline.
Those who deviate from metrics get isolated. This is the path toward the
Matrix scenario — and we're not going there quietly; we're going fast.
