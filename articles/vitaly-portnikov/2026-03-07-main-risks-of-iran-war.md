# Main Risks of the Iran War

[summary](../../summary/vitaly-portnikov/2026-03-07-main-risks-of-iran-war.md)

Vitaly Portnikov joins Peter Zalmayev on the "Devick" program (Pryamiy
channel, March 7, 2026) to discuss the US-Israeli war against Iran, the
Russia-Ukraine war, and Hungary's pre-election tensions with Ukraine.

## Hungary: Orban exploits Ukraine for electoral gain

Hungarian Foreign Minister Peter Szijjarto visited Moscow, where Putin
promised continued oil and gas deliveries at stable prices and released
two Ukrainian POWs with Hungarian citizenship at Orban's request. Orban
continues demanding restoration of Druzhba pipeline deliveries (halted by
Russian strikes), while Zelensky jokingly threatened to give Orban's
address to the Ukrainian armed forces — a remark that angered even
Hungarian opposition leader Peter Magyar.

Hungarian anti-terrorism units detained two Ukrainian armored cash
transport vehicles with seven crew members and $40 million, which Orban
characterized as "corruption money."

Portnikov argues Orban is deliberately injecting the Ukraine factor into
Hungary's pre-election campaign because Hungary's economic situation is
unfavorable for the government. This mirrors Orban's strategy from
previous parliamentary elections: presenting himself as a "prime minister
of peace" to deflect questions about economic performance.

### The Druzhba dilemma

Former Ukrainian Foreign Minister Dmytro Kuleba described the situation
as a dead end: restoring oil flow means playing into Orban's hands, but
not restoring it also plays into his hands. Portnikov suggests the global
energy crisis may resolve this — the European Commission could eventually
demand Ukraine restore Druzhba deliveries to prevent Europe-wide energy
problems, making the current standoff irrelevant compared to what lies
ahead.

### Orban's protection network

Orban benefits from protection by both Washington and Beijing. After
Munich, Secretary of State Marco Rubio visited only Slovakia and Hungary.
Chinese President Xi Jinping also visited Hungary during his European
tour. Portnikov notes the overlap between Trump's and the CCP's spheres
of influence in Europe — a question worth examining for those who see
China and the US as opponents.

However, American protection does not determine electoral outcomes.
Hungary is one of few European countries where Trump's policies enjoy
broad public support, but pro-Trump voters no longer constitute a
majority of Hungarian society.

## Iran war: miscalculation and unexpected escalation

When the operation began, both Trump and Netanyahu believed that initial
strikes would trigger an Iranian uprising similar to the one suppressed
weeks earlier. Portnikov had warned at the time that this was illusory —
if an uprising lacks sufficient force and the regime's security apparatus
remains effective, no amount of aerial bombardment will help.

### Regimes cannot be toppled from the air

This is a proven military truth. The question becomes: what will Trump do
when he realizes this? White House Press Secretary Leavitt provided the
formula — when Trump decides capitulation has occurred, the operation
ends. He does not need Iran's acknowledgment; the capitulation needs to
happen "in Trump's own head." Netanyahu's assessment of whether
objectives were achieved will not influence Trump's decision.

### The economic trap

Trump likely will declare victory when he perceives a balance between
destruction of Iran's missile and nuclear capabilities and the economic
risks of continuing. The economic risks are already very high. Continued
war could push the US past a point of no return, creating a domestic
economic crisis that would:

- End Trump's political career prematurely
- Hand Democrats a stunning victory in midterm elections
- Make impeachment realistic if Democrats control both chambers

The operation was conceived as triumphant; now Trump must ensure it does
not become his defeat. Even now, the war does not enjoy majority support
among Americans. Gas prices are rising, the Strait of Hormuz remains
closed, and job growth has stalled.

### Netanyahu's influence and the antisemitism paradox

The New York Times published a detailed reconstruction showing Trump
initially hesitated but was persuaded by Netanyahu, with Senator Lindsey
Graham playing a role at least as significant. Anti-Israel sentiment is
growing within the MAGA coalition — Tucker Carlson was effectively
expelled from Trump's circle, and Marjorie Taylor Greene makes similar
statements.

Trump leads a coalition where antisemitic forces play a leading role —
an ultra-right, post-fascist political alliance. Yet Trump personally
believes Israel's security is a US priority, continuing the line of
previous presidents against the views of many supporters.

This mirrors Biden's trap: his unwavering Israel support alienated
ultra-left Democratic voters who sympathized with Hamas propaganda after
October 7, 2023. Many abstained in 2024, helping Trump win.

### Israel's "last chance"

For Israel, Trump's presidency may be the last opportunity for active
American support. Historical polling shows a majority of Americans now
favor the Palestinian perspective over the Israeli one. Future presidents
may not act to prevent threats to Israel's existence, viewing it as
disconnected from US national interests. Trump can act against public
opinion due to his psychological disposition, but where will Israel find
another such president?

## Ukraine's emerging leverage

US military officials are consulting Ukrainian forces on countering
Iranian drones. Americans proved completely unprepared for modern
warfare — despite four years of observing the Russia-Ukraine war, they
assumed their superior firepower would deliver victory in days. Instead,
they are spending millions of dollars per interceptor on cheap drones
that Iran can mass-produce, paralyzing not just US military targets but
the entire Persian Gulf region.

Ukraine's army is the only force with real experience fighting drones
and surviving modern war. This creates leverage not just with the US but
with Europe in the future.

## Russia: the real beneficiary

Russia is gaining significant economic advantages from the prolonged
conflict:

- **Sanctions erosion** — US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced
  partial lifting of Russian oil sanctions to prevent energy market
  chaos
- **India returning to Russian oil** — New Delhi always considered
  Russian oil premium but avoided it to stay on good terms with the
  West; that constraint is lifting
- **European dilemma** — the EU faces the question of whether it can
  afford to refuse petroleum products refined from Russian oil in India
  while its own citizens face energy crisis

### Geopolitical losses vs. economic gains

Portnikov challenges the common view that Russia is a geopolitical
player. Russia is a regional power — its sphere of real influence is the
post-Soviet space. The loss of Syria, Venezuela, and Iran as clients
means little because Russia was never truly a global actor. Even its
African presence (via Wagner) could be displaced overnight if Washington
decided to act.

What matters is how much money Russia has to continue its war against
Ukraine in the 2020s and 2030s. Every day the Iran war continues and
disrupts global energy markets gives Putin additional resources and
capabilities for prosecuting the war against Ukraine and converting
neighboring states into absolute Moscow satellites.

German intelligence reports Russia's budget deficit is more than a third
higher than official figures, linked to Western sanctions. Oil price
increases are still insufficient to balance the Russian budget — but the
trajectory favors Moscow if the Iran conflict drags on.

## Russia helping Iran, America looking away

Radio Liberty and the Washington Post reported that Russia is helping
Iran with targeting against American objectives. When asked about this,
Trump refused to comment in harsh terms; his spokesperson called it
irrelevant. Meanwhile, the US abstained from an IAEA vote condemning
Russian attacks on Ukrainian civilian targets.

Portnikov sees this through one lens: Trump refuses to abandon his myth
of a good relationship with Putin. The president lives in his own world.
Accepting Russian aid to Iran would either validate intelligence he finds
inconvenient or elevate Russia to a peer competitor — both unacceptable
to Trump's worldview.

At the latest UN resolution, virtually all US allies — European nations,
Canada, Australia, New Zealand, even Israel — voted in support of
Ukraine. The US abstained. Only Hungary sided with Washington.

## European reactions: three approaches

**Pedro Sanchez (Spain)** — leads the anti-American front in Europe,
but this reflects domestic politics. As a left-party leader competing
with ultra-left allies, his stance is driven by anti-American voter
sentiment rather than geopolitical calculation.

**Giorgia Meloni (Italy)** — represents the pro-Trump right but not the
pro-Putin wing. She linked the Middle East war to Russia's invasion of
Ukraine: "When a UN Security Council member deliberately attacked its
neighbor, a period of chaos was inevitable." Her message to Trump: act
toward Russia as you act toward Iran.

**Friedrich Merz (Germany)** — offered political and logistical support
for the Iran operation in exchange for resolving Ukraine's situation in a
way that serves European security. His message: Europe is an ally, not
an instrument.

## Third World War?

Asked whether we are approaching World War III, Portnikov noted that in
history, people always learned a world war had begun only after it was
over. He expressed hope that a true third world war simply will not
start.
