# Gas Apocalypse: The Energy Crisis Trump Didn't Plan For

## The Triggering Events

Global energy prices are spiking sharply following two strikes:

- *Israeli strike* on the South Pars field — the joint Iranian-Qatari gas reservoir
- *Iranian retaliatory strike* on Ras Laffan, Qatar's largest industrial complex tied to its
  LNG export infrastructure

European gas prices *doubled within 24 hours*. The Strait of Hormuz remains blocked. Qatar's
gas supplies are effectively off the market, which could fundamentally reshape global LNG trade.

---

## Trump's Reaction: Denial and Blame-Shifting

Trump publicly condemned the Israeli strikes on South Pars, claiming he *knew nothing* about
them — that Israel acted "out of anger" and should not do so again. He warned Iran not to
retaliate against Qatar's energy infrastructure, threatening to destroy *all* of Iran's energy
assets if it did.

Defense Secretary Hegseth echoed the same denial.

*Both claims are false.* The US and Israel are operating as a coalition. Israeli officials have
explicitly confirmed that the United States *helped coordinate* the strikes on Iranian energy
infrastructure. This is Trump's decision — he apparently hoped to intimidate Iran, then panicked
when he realized Iran could strike back with consequences far more damaging to the global economy.

Neither Iran nor Arab states believe the US denials. Israeli officials aren't even bothering to
contradict them — they're openly attributing responsibility to Trump and his administration.

---

## Bessent's Self-Defeating Moves

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is responding to the crisis with moves Portnikov describes as
shooting themselves in the foot:

*Russia sanctions lifted:*
Trump and Bessent have already unblocked energy sanctions against Russia. This gives Putin more
budget revenue, more ability to ignore US pressure to end the war in Ukraine, and more leverage
over any future US-Russia economic normalization — the very goal Trump's inner circle (Steve
Witkoff, Jared Kushner) has been negotiating toward.

*Iran sanctions proposed to be lifted:*
Bessent is now proposing to allow Iranian oil — currently stranded at sea — back onto the market,
to push commodity prices down. The unasked question: *who receives the money?* If it flows to the
Islamic Republic, Iran replenishes its budget, builds more missiles and drones, and extends its
capacity to attack US military assets, Israel, and Gulf states. A temporary oil price dip buys a
permanent expansion of Iranian military capability.

---

## The Core Problem: No Plan

Portnikov's central argument: Trump decided to destroy the Iranian ayatollah regime without any
coherent understanding of how events would unfold. He relied on incompetent forecasts — rapid
Iranian capitulation, willingness to negotiate after Khamenei's elimination. Reality has diverged
completely from those expectations.

The result is a daily cycle of reactive decisions that compound the original errors. Each new
move — threatening Iran, blaming Israel, lifting Russia sanctions, proposing Iran sanctions relief
— creates a new problem while failing to solve the previous one. Portnikov's image: someone trying
to extinguish a fire with a canister, running to each new blaze only to find they're pouring fuel.

*The 20-day benchmark:* Twenty days into the campaign, the pattern is clear. If the next 20 days
follow the same logic, the US risks entering a multi-month or multi-year Middle East war
accompanied by a global economic, energy, and security crisis with unpredictable consequences for
the US itself, Ukraine, and Europe.

---

## Who Benefits

The crisis serves interests well beyond Iran:

- *Russia:* exploits the energy spike to fund its war against Ukraine; energy sanctions lifted
  as a bonus
- *China:* watching with interest as US power and credibility erode; eyes Taiwan
- *Iran:* wants to drag the entire Middle East into medievalism to preserve its own regime;
  willing to burn everything around it

The real test, Portnikov concludes, is whether this doesn't end in a third world war — with the
possible use of nuclear weapons by adversaries who benefit from US weakness and the absence of
strategic thinking in Washington at the most serious moment in its recent history.
