# "Portnikov: US Has Lost the War in Iran — Catastrophe With Massive

[summary](../../summary/vitaly-portnikov/2026-03-26-us-lost-iran-war-trump-crisis.md)

Vitaly Portnikov, interviewed by Vadym Radionov on the Seichas channel,
analyzes Trump's failing Iran strategy, draws parallels with the Vietnam
War, and discusses the implications for Ukraine and the broader
geopolitical order.

## Trump's Iran ultimatum: no good options

Trump issued an ultimatum to Iran but then backed off, giving Tehran
five more days. Portnikov identifies three possible paths, all of them
bad for the US:

- *Negotiate and coexist* — Iran's regime survives and looks like a
  winner to Gulf states. If the US cannot even defend its own assets,
  allies lose faith in American security guarantees.
- *Continue the air campaign* — risks a major energy catastrophe. Iran
  can strike Gulf state infrastructure with unpredictable consequences
  for the global economy, which is already entering a serious crisis.
- *Launch a ground operation* — would require hundreds of thousands of
  US troops and could produce tens of thousands of casualties. Current
  force levels in the Gulf are insufficient, but Trump may be bluffing
  to buy time, much like Putin did before invading Ukraine in 2022.

## The Vietnam parallel

If the US is forced to withdraw without toppling the Iranian regime,
the consequences would mirror the Vietnam War defeat. After Vietnam,
US global leadership was severely diminished. The only reason America
recovered was that the Soviet Union and China had dysfunctional planned
economies incapable of competing despite their political victory.
Today the situation is different — China understood economics where the
Soviets did not, and Russia now has a market economy under a security
service regime. The US may lose a decisive battle this time.

## Trump's inner circle: three groups

Portnikov breaks Trump's entourage into three groups:

- *Political careerists* — Vice President JD Vance and Secretary of
  State Marco Rubio, who think about succession and future elections
  but are constrained by the requirement of obsequious loyalty to an
  aging president.
- *Appointees without independent standing* — people like Defense
  Secretary Pete Hegseth, who owe their position entirely to Trump's
  favor, have no political future without him, and are mainly
  accumulating material for future memoirs.
- *The inner financial circle* — Jared Kushner, Steve Witkoff, and
  Trump family members. This group drives key decisions through the
  logic of speculative enrichment and family capital creation.
  Political consequences do not concern them because they do not think
  in political categories at all.

## Simulated madness as business strategy

Trump has openly said he wants adversaries like Putin and Xi Jinping
to think he is insane. Portnikov argues that this "simulated madness"
is a core business technique Trump has used his entire life — creating
market chaos to enrich his circle. Every time Trump makes dramatic
announcements (like the five-day ultimatum), oil prices swing, and
those with advance knowledge profit. However, Trump's advancing age
and deteriorating psychological state make it increasingly difficult
to separate strategy from genuine instability.

## Putin benefits temporarily

Russia has profited from rising oil prices, abandoning planned budget
austerity after the Siluanov meeting. But Portnikov notes this is
temporary — Russia, like everyone else, is reacting to American
actions rather than architecting events.

## Iran holds the initiative

If Iran refuses Trump's terms, Trump is trapped. Iran is not
interested in ending the war. The only person who can give orders to
all of Iran's armed formations is the Supreme Leader (Rahbar), whom
Trump refuses to engage. Unlike Venezuela, where Maduro's removal led
to a constitutional succession, Iran has no such mechanism that would
satisfy American demands.

## The Ukraine-Iran parallel

Portnikov compares Iran's situation to Ukraine's resistance against
Russia: in both cases, the invading power expected quick capitulation
and got bogged down. The key difference is that Ukraine, as a
democratic state, follows the rules of war and avoids dragging others
into the conflict. Iran behaves like Lukashenko once advised
Zelenskyy — strike oil refineries, and your enemy will negotiate.
This makes Iran far more dangerous for the US.

Each day of Iranian resistance deepens the global economic and energy
crisis, which inevitably rebounds on the United States.

## Netanyahu's catastrophic miscalculation

Portnikov questions how Netanyahu could have believed that air strikes
alone, without a ground operation, could destroy the Iranian regime.
He sees this as a staggering loss of competence for a leader of a
country fighting for its survival. Either Netanyahu genuinely believed
in air power alone, or he deliberately sought to draw the US into a
ground war, indifferent to American casualties, because Israel's
existence takes priority over Trump's political career.

## Israeli political elite erosion

Portnikov reflects on conversations he had in Israel in the late
1990s about what would happen when the generation of legendary
leaders — Ariel Sharon, Shimon Peres — departed after clearing the
political field around them. The answer, he says, is now visible:
an eroded political class that does not match the existential
challenges Israel faces.

## Zelenskyy and personalist governance

Comparing Zelenskyy to Netanyahu, Portnikov notes both run
personalist regimes, but of different kinds:

- Netanyahu is a lifelong professional politician who selects
  subordinates for loyalty.
- Zelenskyy has been in politics only six years and surrounds himself
  with close personal friends rather than political operators.
- Both share a weakness: governing without coalition constraints leads
  to fatal errors that cannot be corrected.

Portnikov argues that Ukraine needed a national unity government from
the start. Without checks and balances, degradation of state
governance began in 2019 when parliamentary-presidential mechanisms
were hollowed out — well before the 2022 invasion.

## Orban as a real problem for Ukraine

Hungary's Orban is a genuine problem for Ukraine — not because of
blocked EU accession talks (which are theoretical until the war ends),
but because:

- Orban is blocking 90 billion euros in promised EU funds
- Hungary's foreign minister reportedly leaked EU consultations on
  Ukraine to Russia's foreign minister
- Orban pressures Ukraine to continue Russian oil transit across its
  territory
