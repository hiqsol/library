Vitaly Portnikov joins Mark Feigin on March 24, 2026 to discuss the evolving
situation with Trump's simultaneous handling of the Iran war and the Ukraine
conflict.

## Iran war: surprise and miscalculation

The US and Israel entered the Iran conflict with a simple plan: strike Iran's
nuclear and missile capabilities, and the population would rise up and change
the regime. The worst-case scenario was that Iran would retaliate against
Israel while the US destroyed its missile potential through negotiations.

Neither scenario materialized. Instead, Iran escalated in unexpected ways:

- **Attacks on Gulf states** — Iran struck the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait,
  Qatar, and Oman, which neither Washington nor Jerusalem anticipated
- **Hormuz Strait blockade** — approaching a global energy and food crisis,
  with fertilizer shipments disrupted ahead of planting season
- **Hezbollah reactivation** — despite the killing of Hassan Nasrallah,
  Hezbollah proved capable of renewed operations
- **Surprise capabilities** — maneuverable missiles and 4,000km range
  rockets reaching Diego Garcia, unknown to US/Israeli intelligence
- **Decentralized command** — Iranian military operations continue
  regardless of leadership eliminations because the decision-making is
  distributed

## The antimissile crisis

A critical underappreciated problem: the world is running out of
anti-missile interceptors. The US has expended enormous quantities of
expensive interceptors against Iranian missiles. New production capacity
won't come online until 2027. If the war continues, not only Ukraine but
Israel and Saudi Arabia will have nothing to intercept missiles with.

Ukraine is actually the one country that could help the US save armaments —
yet Trump says he doesn't need Ukrainian help.

## Trump's pattern: same playbook for Iran and Ukraine

Trump's behavior in the Iran conflict mirrors his Ukraine approach:
threats, declarations of total victory, ultimatums — then zigzagging
toward peace talks. He told journalists that "Zelensky and Putin are close
to a deal, they just need to sit down and negotiate."

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said "we negotiate with bombs, not
conversations," to which Trump responded that Hegseth "is the one who
doesn't want this resolved."

## Global consequences ahead

Portnikov warns of cascading effects in 2026-2027:
- Gas stations closing in Bangladesh is "just the flowers" — bread stores
  will close next
- Regime changes and uprisings in Global South countries
- More radical Islamist regimes may emerge, worse than Iran's
- All of this is on the agenda and Trump doesn't even suspect it

## The Ukraine angle

Trump continues to treat Ukraine and Russia as a side issue to be "solved"
quickly. But the Iran war has consumed American resources and attention,
making the Ukraine situation worse. The interconnection of these conflicts
means that failure in one amplifies problems in the other.

Iran is not interested in peace talks — it wants to lure Trump into a
ground operation that produces "coffins of American soldiers." The real
question is whether Trump will choose ground operations or find an exit
that leaves the Iranian regime intact but weakened — and what that means
for Gulf state alliances with America.
