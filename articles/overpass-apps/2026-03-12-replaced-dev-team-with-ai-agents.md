# I Replaced My Dev Team with AI Agents (7 Months Later)

## Seven Months of Change

After a seven-month hiatus from making videos, Eric returns to share how
profoundly his workflow has changed. He relocated from Oxfordshire to
Birmingham, UK, and his entire professional process shifted to AI-driven
development. Where he previously relied on offshore teams in the
Philippines, India, and Vietnam for Overpass projects, now everything is
done by AI agents.

## The Speed of Obsolescence

The pace of change in developer tooling has accelerated dramatically. New
frameworks used to arrive every few years; now they become obsolete within
months. Eric traces the progression he personally experienced:

1. **ChatGPT** (late 2022) — could write poems and produce code, but often
   wrong and lacking context.
2. **Crew AI** — agentic workflows, exciting at the time, now obsolete.
3. **Copilot** — sat inside VS Code, helped with suggestions.
4. **Cursor / Windsurf** — could actually generate entire codebases, not
   just assist.
5. **Claude Code, Gemini, OpenClaw** — full agentic frameworks where agents
   run independently.
6. **Opus 4.6** — the current game-changer with parallel agent teams.

Many developer friends still copy-paste from ChatGPT and feel it's
"cheating" — reminiscent of how looking things up on Google once felt like
cheating.

## Daily Workflow: Task Lists for Agents

Eric now wakes up and creates a task list for his agents rather than for
human team members:

- A **research agent** investigates feasibility for new requests.
- A **dev agent** examines technical requirements.
- A **design agent** generates mockups in Figma.
- **Sub-agents** handle speculative projects with specified tech stacks.
- A **foreman agent** manages all the others.

The agents work independently, only asking for permissions, API keys, or
provisioning when needed. They test their own work. It resembles working
with offshore teams, except faster and with built-in self-testing.

Additional tooling in the stack: Claude Code with MCP servers, Nano Banana
for icon generation, and Fastlane for app store deployment — all running
independently while Eric manages the overall process.

## The Babysitting Reality

AI agents still require significant oversight. They get things wrong. They
sometimes return work that is completely off-target. This isn't different
from offshore teams delivering incorrect results — but the iteration cycle
is dramatically faster. You can say "undo everything and try again" and get
results in minutes rather than days.

## Practical Impact: Client Quotations

A concrete example: producing a client quotation used to take hours of
work, causing delays of one to two weeks. Now Eric dispatches multiple
agents in parallel — research, development assessment, design mockups —
and delivers a complete quotation to the client the next day.

## The Exponential Curve

What distinguishes AI agents from previous tools and from offshore teams
is the exponential improvement curve. The tools get better day by day.
Eric's primary concern isn't that AI will replace him — that will happen
regardless — but rather keeping up with the pace of advancement.

He commits to avoiding hyperbole-driven content ("OpenClaw changed my
life" two days after release) and instead sharing tools he has actually
used, what worked, what's overrated, and what's genuinely useful.
