Every one of my clients right now is asking me about the same thing. It's not about
a new model. It's not about a better prompt. There's a feature built into Claude and
ChatGPT that most people have never touched — and three things changed recently that
made it impossible to ignore.

## Three forces driving the shift to agents

**1. Better UI.** Claude created Claude Co-Work, a wrapper around Claude Code that's
friendly to non-technical people. ChatGPT followed with Codex. Both look like normal
chat apps, removing the terminal barrier.

**2. Better models.** Opus 4.6 from Claude and GPT 5.4 from OpenAI are extremely good
at long-running tasks without making mistakes. You can give the AI more complex tasks
that deliver more value, and it achieves them consistently.

**3. Politics.** After the Department of War situation with Anthropic, many people left
GPT for Claude, getting exposed to Claude Co-Work in the process.

## Four agent unlocks

### 1. Files

In chat, you're the bottleneck — uploading files one by one, limited to maybe 10 files.
With agents, you bring the AI to the files. You can give it 10, 50, 100, 200 files. The
AI doesn't hold everything in its head; it searches across files, reading only the
relevant parts.

### 2. Memory

Chat memory is shallow and static — your name, role, industry. With agents, you create
an instructions file (claude.md for Claude, agents.md for Codex). The key difference:
you can tell the AI to update its own memory and instructions as it learns new patterns
and insights. Between session 1 and session 20, the value dramatically increases because
it understands your work nuances.

### 3. Systems

Chat has limited, read-only data connectors. Agents can connect to many systems and both
read and write to them — even create tools on the fly.

Example: Drop a meeting transcript into your agent. It goes to your calendar, grabs
attendee emails, reads the transcript, drafts action items per person, updates the CRM,
and drafts a Gmail with everything linked. All you do is review and press send.

### 4. Skills

In Claude, skills are available in chat, but with agents you can string skills together
for complex long-running tasks. You can also run parallel sub-agents — each with isolated
context and memory, focused on a specific sub-task. The parent AI orchestrates, and out
comes the final result, faster and higher quality.

## When to use chat vs agents

**Stay in chat when:**
- Doing conversational back-and-forth iteration
- Everything fits in the context window (roughly 70% of tasks)
- It's a one-off task
- Team collaboration — sharing is easier in chat for non-technical folks

**Switch to agents when:**
- You have more than 10 files
- You need the AI to connect to and write to multiple systems
- You want compounding memory across sessions and topics
- You have repetitive recurring tasks

It's not either/or — it's a spectrum. You might start in chat and move to agents, or
use both in the same workflow.

## Getting started today

1. Download the desktop app (Claude Co-Work or Codex)
2. Pick or create a sandbox folder
3. Copy some files in
4. Give the AI a task in plain English
5. Watch what happens

**Three starter use cases:**
- **File organization** — dump your messy downloads folder, have the AI sort, rename,
  and summarize
- **Expenses** — drop receipts into a folder, have the AI extract dates, vendors,
  amounts into a spreadsheet
- **Consolidation** — drop 50 research or client files, ask specific questions, get a
  master document with answers
