# The Truth About Developer Productivity in the AI Age (It's a Trap)

[summary](../../summary/steve-smith/2026-03-11-developer-productivity-ai-trap-activity-metrics.md)

## The Core Warning

Gen AI is simultaneously a stock market bubble, a technology hype train, and a genuine innovation.
This combination triggers the worst organisational impulses: tripling down on Taylorism, measuring
every line of code, and treating activity counts as productivity progress. The industry is sliding
backwards — from outcome metrics back to activity and output metrics — because they are easier to
implement and easier to game.

---

## The Metric Hierarchy

| Type | What it measures | Ease | Value |
|------|-----------------|------|-------|
| Activity | Behaviours | Easy | Low |
| Output | Deliverables | Medium | Some |
| Outcome | System changes | Hard | High |

The DORA / Accelerate metrics (deployment frequency, lead time, change failure rate, time to
restore) are outcome metrics. AI usage metrics are activity metrics.

---

## Goodhart's Law

> "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."

People change behaviour based on how they are measured:
- Measure AI prompt count → prompt count rises
- Measure % of AI-generated code → number of classes and methods rises
- Measure AI costs → prompt count falls, but prompt size grows

---

## A Brief History of Software Metrics

- **2003** — Martin Fowler: "We can't measure productivity." Rang true for a decade.
- **2010s** — Dr Nicole Forsgren and the State of DevOps Reports: looked at *team sociotechnical
  behaviours* and business success, not individual productivity.
- **Accelerate (book)** — applied statistical validation, cluster analysis, causal modelling to
  years of survey data. Built and validated a predictive model of organisational performance.
  Martin Fowler called it "rare rigorous scientific evidence."

## Critique: DX "AI Assisted Engineering Q4 2025" Report

DX sells a developer intelligence platform. Smith's target is not DX specifically but "a consistent
pattern in industry over the past year: vendors selling AI usage as nirvana as if the last 20 years
of hard lessons had never happened."

*Sample:* 135,000 developers, ~400 companies, 3.5 months. Good sample size, individual-level data,
documented methodology — but no information on whether sampling is random, self-selected,
stratified, or weight-adjusted.

### Headline 1 — "Industry-wide AI adoption is now over 90%"
*Type:* Activity metric. Low value.

Two problems:
1. 90% of developers showing any AI tool usage over 3.5 months ≠ 90% of companies adopting AI.
2. Developer tool interaction ≠ engineering strategy. Strategy is policy, governance, SDLC
   composition.

### Headline 2 — "Developers save an average of 3.6 hours/week with AI tools"
*Type:* Activity metric. Measurement method: self-reported time savings.

Weak construct — depends on memory, counterfactual reasoning, and subjective attribution. Easily
gamed. Without linking to an outcome like lead time improvement, this is perception, not system
performance.

### Headline 3 — "22% of code is AI-authored"
*Type:* Activity metric. Low value.

Definition: "code generated by AI that was merged without major human rewrites." Subjective — two
developers with identical behaviours could report very different percentages. Self-reported.
Goodhart strikes again.

### Headline 4 — "Daily AI users ship 60% more pull requests than non-users"
*Type:* Output metric. Medium value.

The best headline — and the most problematic.

- No mention of statistical significance of the correlation.
- **Pull request throughput is not a unit of delivery.** A developer could merge 100 PRs/day with
  one deploy/month (blocked by a central testing team). Another could merge zero PRs while the
  team does 10 deploys/day via trunk-based development.
- More PRs could simply mean developers going too fast because they are measured on PRs —
  abandoning unit testing, creating more defects, more security patches, more rework.
- The Accelerate book measured throughput *and reliability together* for exactly this reason.
- DX themselves: "It's still possible this doesn't lead to better business results." Correct —
  because it's an output metric, not an outcome.

Pull request throughput tells you nothing about:
- Deployment throughput
- Service reliability
- Causality, system-level effects, or business performance

## What to Do Instead

1. **Recognise the trap.** The AI age is pushing companies toward Taylorism and individual
   productivity measurement.
2. **Remember: the team is the unit of delivery**, not the individual.
3. **Read Accelerate and Modern Software Engineering.** Implement them.
4. **Look at your DORA/Accelerate metrics.** If deployment throughput, service reliability, and
   technical quality are trending right — your technology outcomes are on track.
5. **Ladder up:** Technology outcomes → time-to-value and total cost of ownership → contextual
   business outcomes (e.g. customer lifetime value, profitability).
6. **Then and only then** measure AI usage as an activity metric. Look for *correlation* between
   AI usage and capability improvement — but be explicit that correlation ≠ causation.

Leading indicator capabilities to track (from Accelerate):
- Is trunk-based development increasing?
- Are architectures becoming more loosely coupled?
- Is continuous integration becoming more stable?
