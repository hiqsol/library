## Will the Ayatollah Regime Fall?

Trump spoke of finding "someone who knows what they're doing" to rebuild Iran. Chernyk's assessment:
regime change via air strikes alone is **practically impossible**. Historical parallel: NATO's 1999 *Allied
Force* campaign in Serbia did not topple Slobodan Milošević immediately — he lost power only in 2003,
despite no ground operation. Air strikes can prepare the ground for a political transition over time, but
Chernyk believes the US ultimately wants to **keep the Ayatollah regime weakened, not eliminated** — to
maintain pressure on Gulf Arab monarchies and contain Turkish imperialism.

Breaking news on the day of broadcast: Khamenei's son reportedly suffered injuries and had a leg amputated
— suggesting targeted pressure on the leadership is active, but full regime removal is not the goal.

## US–India Sanctions Waiver (30 Days)

The US Treasury issued a special licence allowing India to buy Russian oil — but only for 30 days. Chernyk:

- This is a strategic move to **peel India away** from a potential China–India bloc in the Shanghai
  Cooperation Organisation. India is now the world's most populous country and a massive energy consumer.
- Russia's oil sales are already a zero-sum game: they are at peak extraction, cannot develop new fields,
  and depend on pipelines that Ukraine is destroying (the Druzhba pipeline pumping station has been struck
  again recently in southern Russia).
- For Russia to truly benefit, oil must stay above **$120/barrel for at least 6 months**. Current Brent is
  ~$100; minus discounts, Russia receives ~$80. The 30-day waiver changes nothing strategically.
- Critical nuance: the waiver covers only oil loaded before the date of announcement. New shipments remain
  sanctioned.

## The Hormuz Strait: Why Isn't It Unblocked?

Iran is striking tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. The US has full capability to unblock it — but is
**deliberately delaying**. Chernyk's analysis:

1. **The US is now energy-independent** — producing 14 million barrels/day (shale), it does not need
   Persian Gulf oil. It wants to sell its own oil and LNG to Europe.
2. **China's tankers pass freely** — verified Chinese ships are not stopped. Other tankers hide behind
   Chinese radio signals to slip through. This is a tool of economic pressure on China.
3. **Historical precedent:** In 1987–88, the US ran *Operation Ernest Will* for over a year with light
   corvettes (William Hazard Perry-class), protecting tankers and ultimately winning the naval battle in
   *Operation Praying Mantis*. The capability exists.
4. **The European option:** The US may deliberately leave the unblocking to a European coalition — France
   (carrier *Charles de Gaulle*, 30+ frigates/corvettes) and Italy (carrier *Cavour*, submarines). This
   would force Europe to build military infrastructure in the region and accelerate European rearmament,
   freeing the US to refocus on China.

## The 1973 Parallel: Oil Crisis and the Electric Vehicle Revolution

The 1973 oil crisis — triggered by Arab states after the Yom Kippur War — led to a revolution in engine
efficiency (from 6.5L Chevrolet Impalas to 2–2.5L engines) and the Alaskan pipeline. Chernyk sees the
current Middle East conflict as potentially triggering **the next such revolution**: the full transition to
electric vehicles. Mercedes has already announced abandoning combustion engines. The crisis may be partly
engineered to accelerate this transition — a multi-decade strategic campaign typical of American long-range
planning (e.g., stealth technology programmes planned 70 years ahead with $1.5 trillion allocated).

## Iran's 450 kg of Enriched Uranium

Iran enriched uranium to 60% (above the 20% weapons-grade threshold). The stockpile has been dispersed and
hidden in the Zagros Mountains (northeastern Iran — 1,500 km wide, 300–400 km deep, filled with caves and
gorges). Chernyk expects a **special operations hunt** rather than airstrikes — similar to the decade-long
hunt for Osama bin Laden, concluded in 2011. US special forces have demonstrated the capability to insert
800 km deep by helicopter (Super Stallion). Tomahawks were also used in the bin Laden hunt before the
precise location was established.

## The Bryansk Strike: Ukraine's Most Impactful Precision Hit

The strike on the Bryansk microelectronics factory was described as the **most successful single
destruction of an aggressor's military-technological potential**. What was lost:

- Microelectronics production for Russian missiles (primarily the X-35 series).
- Specifically: the upgraded *Izdeliye-30* variant of the X-35 — enhanced to 1,500 km range and up to
  500–800 kg warhead — was being developed for the Su-34. With ~80–90 operational Su-34s each capable of
  carrying multiple missiles, this would have been a mass-scale, strategically dangerous weapon.
- The strike used **Storm Shadow** missiles — 5 of them penetrated Russia's vaunted air defence (which
  Chernyk believes barely exists around the Bryansk region).
