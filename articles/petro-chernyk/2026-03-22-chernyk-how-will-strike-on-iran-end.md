# Chernyk: How Will the Strike on Iran End? Sensational Forecast

Military expert and Ukrainian Armed Forces Colonel Petro Chernyk appeared on
ZAXID.NET to analyze the ongoing US military operation against Iran, its
strategic consequences for Ukraine, and the shifting geopolitical order.

## Operation Status: Iran's Military Potential Destroyed by 90%

According to Pentagon data, over 15,000 targets have been struck since the
operation began. Iranian missile launch activity has fallen by 90% and drone
usage has dropped by 95%. Iran's attacks on neighboring states have become
isolated incidents.

Chernyk confirms Iran's combat potential has been reduced by roughly 90%.
Before the operation began -- called "Epic Epoch" -- Iran was estimated to
possess up to 2,000 heavy missiles and around 500 launch platforms. Chernyk
estimates that two-thirds of these have been destroyed, though he notes
that Iran is a mountainous country (4/5 of its territory is mountains),
and the Iranians spent decades building tunnel complexes and mountain launch
shafts that are difficult to hunt individually.

He adds that the US withdrawal from Afghanistan was a strategic mistake
precisely because a base there would have made it far easier to systematically
destroy Iran's missile program from the east.

## Kharg Island Strike

US Central Command Admiral Cooper reported a massive precision strike on
Kharg Island, destroying over 90 Iranian military targets including bunkers
for sea mines and missiles, plus other military infrastructure. Over 6,000
combat sorties have been conducted, and more than 100 Iranian naval vessels
have been sunk. Crucially, the oil terminals handling 90% of Iranian oil
exports were deliberately left untouched.

Chernyk explains that securing shipping through the Strait of Hormuz requires
deploying a significant naval force in the strait itself. He rejects the
narrative that the US lacks the capability to do this, referencing Operation
Earnest Will in 1987-88 when the US escorted tankers while Iran's fleet was
still intact. Today, Iran's fleet has been practically annihilated.

## Why the US Is Not Entering the Strait

The US is deliberately leaving the strait security role to European nations.
The rationale: the US now imports only about 2% of its oil from the Persian
Gulf region. China, by contrast, imports roughly 90% of its oil through the
strait. Japan and Europe are also heavily dependent.

Chernyk argues this is by design -- America is pushing its allies to take
responsibility for securing a waterway that benefits them far more than it
benefits the US.

Trump has warned that if Iran attempts to close the strait, oil terminal
strikes will follow. He confirmed that NATO partners are being recruited
to patrol the strait and has received positive responses from six countries:
the UK, France, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, and Japan.

## European Reluctance and Its Dangers

Chernyk is critical of the European tendency to avoid joining US-led
operations. He acknowledges the political toxicity around Trump's name but
insists the analysis should focus on American strategic interests, not the
personality of the current president.

He argues that Europe has long lived under an American security umbrella and
has grown passive. But the global confrontation between democracies and
tyrannies is not over -- it is only beginning. The authoritarian bloc (Russia,
China, Iran, North Korea) remains intact and is preparing for a larger
confrontation.

He points to history: the Islamic Caliphate reached Iberia and occupied it for
720 years before being expelled. The Ottoman Empire reached Vienna in the
second half of the 17th century. Only a unified "Holy League" stopped it.
Democracy has no alternative but to consolidate.

He notes that Europeans still have enormous untapped military potential they
have not shared with Ukraine -- at least 1,500 operational fighters (Gripen,
Rafale, Mirage 2000, Tornado, Eurofighter) and at least 4,000 Leopard 1 and
Leopard 2 platform units. Spain last year scrapped dozens of M113 APCs rather
than providing them to Ukraine.

## The Risk to NATO's Article 5

Chernyk raises the scenario of a Russian provocation against the Baltic states.
He argues Russia does not need advanced equipment -- a 40,000-strong corps
armed with Kalashnikovs (of which millions remain in storage, along with
15 million SKS rifles and 6 million other Soviet-era weapons) could cause
enough chaos to trigger Article 5 proceedings.

He believes Hungary and Slovakia would block Article 5 from activating even
if Russia attacked Estonia tomorrow. He cites British General Richard Shirreff's
book "War with Russia" as essential reading on this scenario.

He draws a parallel to January 2022, when 99.9% of analysts said there would
be no war. The war happened. The principle: always overestimate the enemy;
prepare so that when he attacks, the cost is unbearable.

## Ukraine's "Stockholm Syndrome" Toward Iran

One of Chernyk's most pointed arguments is directed at Ukrainians themselves.
He is alarmed by a growing sympathy for Iran in Ukraine's information space
and attributes it to deliberate Russian information operations. Russia is
seeding the narrative that the US attack on Iran is a "special military
operation" equivalent to Russia's war against Ukraine. Chernyk calls this
a dangerous false equivalence.

His argument: the Russia-Ukraine war and the US-Iran conflict are not two
separate geopolitical vectors -- they are one. Both are democracies fighting
tyrannies. The Iranian regime has directly participated in the genocide of
Ukrainians through its weapons: the Shahed-136 drone alone killed thousands
of Ukrainians, destroyed tens of thousands of homes, and shattered millions
of lives.

He makes a careful distinction between the Iranian regime and the Iranian
people, but notes that ordinary Iranian citizens were manufacturing, packaging,
and transporting the weapons that killed Ukrainians. He invokes the Nuremberg
precedent: after World War II, not just the Nazi party but the entire German
people underwent denazification. Germans living near death camps who "looked
the other way" were held accountable. The same principle applies.

He also invokes the Persian historical record: Persia was an imperial power
that launched the first attack on Europe (the Battle of Thermopylae, 480 BC).

## China's Role and the Strait's Oil Politics

China's economy is 80% export-oriented, directed primarily at the
Euro-Atlantic world. Iran's oil represents a critical input, making China
highly vulnerable to any disruption in the strait.

Chernyk finds it revealing that China has provided no significant military
assistance to Iran despite their partnership. He draws a historical parallel:
before the US began arming the Afghan mujahideen with Stingers in 1986,
90% of all weapons reaching the mujahideen were Chinese. Saudi money funded
the purchases; China provided the weapons. One year alone saw 70,000 tonnes of
ammunition purchased, including MANPADS replicas. Six hundred Chinese
specialists operated from Pakistan.

Yet today China has not sent a convoy of weapons to Iran. He believes this
reflects China's calculation that the US could escalate further and strike
Iran's oil infrastructure -- something America has not yet done.

Trump's invitation for China to join patrols of the Hormuz strait is, in
Chernyk's view, a genuine geopolitical offer: China helped create this
situation; China should help resolve it.

## Dubai, Russian Money, and the War Economy

Chernyk notes a curious side effect of the conflict: large numbers of pet
cats and dogs have appeared on Dubai streets. The explanation, he says, is
that wealthy Russians who had been using the UAE as a gray zone for money
laundering and sanctions evasion are now fleeing. He identifies these
individuals not merely as wealthy expats but as participants in the
microelectronics and dual-use materials supply chain that kept Russia's
military economy running.

The Kh-47M2 Kinzhal missile contains 130 components of Western manufacture.
It was this network -- running through Dubai and other Middle Eastern hubs --
that sourced those components. That network is now collapsing.

Dubai received $140 billion in investment in 2024. Much of that ecosystem
is now being disrupted.

## The Oil Price Argument

Chernyk disputes the idea that oil price spikes will be economically
catastrophic. He notes that during the 1973 Yom Kippur War, Arab monarchies
quadrupled the price of oil and the world did not collapse. Current price
increases of 30-40% are manageable. For a truly painful impact, oil would
need to reach a minimum of $150 per barrel and hold for six months. Chernyk
does not believe this will happen.

He expects the US to conclude the operation by summer. The operation was
announced as lasting "no less than five to six weeks" -- the current point is
roughly the midway mark. Iran has already lost 90% of its missile and drone
capacity, its air force does not exist as a fighting force, and its navy has
been sunk.

## US Amphibious Capability and Kharg Island

Chernyk refutes claims that the US lacks the forces for an amphibious
operation if needed. He details the inventory: approximately 30 universal
amphibious assault ships including the San Antonio class and the new America
class (40,000-tonne displacement, comparable to France's Charles de Gaulle
carrier). Collectively these could land up to 10,000 Marines in a first wave,
supported by AAV-P7 amphibious APCs (capacity 25 troops), V-22 Osprey
tiltrotors (22 troops), CH-46/CH-47 Chinook helicopters (55 troops), and
Super Stallion helicopters (30+ troops).

The last major US amphibious assault was Iwo Jima in 1945 -- successful
despite US casualties of over 4,000 against Japanese losses of over 20,000
defenders. Chernyk notes the US has been in an offensive posture since its
Civil War and has refined amphibious operations extensively.

He also raises the question of the Bushehr nuclear power plant -- whether
that would require a dedicated raid. He references the January 1945 Cabanatuan
raid in the Philippines where 130 US Army Rangers freed 500 POWs held by
1,500 Japanese troops as an example of American raid capability.

## America's Strategic Position and the Future

The US economy is 80% self-sufficient. It imports only 2% of its energy from
the Persian Gulf. Meanwhile 1.5 trillion dollars in net capital flowed into
America last year, including $80 billion in euro-denominated funds from Europe.
The dollar's dominance is a reflection of where global capital sees safety.

Chernyk outlines three scenarios that could threaten America:
- A massive nuclear strike (possible from Russia or China)
- Internal American separatism (Southern and Southwestern states)
- A Yellowstone supervolcano eruption

Absent these, the US is reestablishing its role as global hegemon. Chernyk
quotes Francis Fukuyama's error in the 1990s -- the belief that liberal
capitalism would inevitably spread to China. In reality, only 6-7% of the
world's nations are genuine democracies; the rest are authoritarian in
various forms.

His closing argument: better an American hegemon than a Chinese or Russian one.

## Ukraine's Role in Civilizational History

Chernyk closes with a broader reflection. He believes Ukraine is at a
civilizational inflection point -- for the first time in history, Ukraine's
outcome will determine the direction of world civilization for decades. He
quotes Stepan Bandera's strategic principle: rely on your own strength, keep
emotions for battle, and bring a cold mind to war.

He references Peter Zeihan's book "The End of the World is Just the Beginning"
as essential reading for those seeking deep structural analysis.

He also expresses personal conviction that humanity is approaching an energy
revolution: fossil fuels will be gradually phased out over the coming decades
and replaced by atomic energy based on helium-3 -- a long-range forecast
he offers with appropriate uncertainty.
