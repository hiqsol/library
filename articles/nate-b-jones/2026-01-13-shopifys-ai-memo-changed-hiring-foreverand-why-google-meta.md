## The Memo That Started It All

Eight months ago, Shopify CEO Tobi Lutke posted a memo that most people
dismissed as typical tech CEO posturing: AI is mandatory, prove the
robots cannot do it before hiring a human, and put AI usage into
performance reviews.

They were wrong to dismiss it. Lutke fired the starting gun on a talent
market restructuring that is now accelerating faster than anyone
anticipated. This week, Josh Miller at the Browser Company mentioned
paying premiums for people native to the Claude Code way of building —
one data point, but symptomatic of something much larger.

## The Red Queen Framework

The memo was not new philosophy. It was the application of an existing
framework to AI. Lutke operates under what he calls the Red Queen
framework, from Lewis Carroll's Through the Looking Glass: "It takes
all the running you can do to stay in the same place."

At Shopify, growing 20–40% year-over-year, you must improve by at least
that much every year just to re-qualify for your own role. This applies
to Lutke himself. Stagnation is not just failure — it is slow-motion
termination.

## The Specific Mandates

- Reflexive AI usage is a baseline expectation — not a suggestion
- AI must dominate the prototype phase of all projects
- Performance reviews include AI usage questions — managers and peers
  rate each other on AI nativeness
- Teams must prove AI cannot do the work before requesting headcount
- Everyone means everyone, including executives

As Lutke put it: the earlier call to tinker with AI "was too much of a
suggestion. This is what I want to change today."

## Shopify's Infrastructure: Years in the Making

VP and Head of Engineering Farhan Tawer brought GitHub Copilot to
Shopify pre-alpha, a year before ChatGPT's release. By early 2023,
they had 80% adoption.

The infrastructure that made the mandate possible:

- **Internal LLM proxy** — centralized system for accessing multiple
  AI models through a single interface, handling scaling, tracking,
  and failover
- **Dozens of MCP servers** — connecting to Slack, Salesforce, G Suite,
  making every piece of internal data available for AI interrogation
- **Roast** — an open-sourced AI orchestration framework built with
  Claude Code that reviews code contributions with constructive
  criticism

Unlike companies that gate advanced models for technical teams, Shopify
gives everybody access to everything with no spending quotas. Tawer
tracks who spends the most on Cursor tokens as a proxy for employee
value — the CTO recently topped the list.

**The fastest-growing user groups were not in engineering — they were
in support and revenue teams.**

## How AI-Augmented Work Actually Looks

A sales engineer brought MCPs of his most frequently used tools (G Suite,
Slack, Salesforce) into a Cursor-built dashboard. He asks "what do I do
today?" and the system looks at Salesforce opportunities, notices missing
email responses, and prompts action. He works in Cursor as his homepage.

The revenue tooling team deployed an RFP agent that answers RFP questions
while learning from winning responses — improving through accumulated
knowledge of what wins deals.

Shopify's internal AI chat tool originated as a senior engineer's
prototype and became so widely adopted that a dedicated engineering
team now runs it. This pattern — individual experimentation becoming
organizational infrastructure — is how AI-native workflows propagate
in organizations that allow wide experimentation.

## The Headcount Story Is Complicated

| Period | Headcount | Context |
|---|---|---|
| End of 2022 | ~11,600 | Peak of zero interest rate era |
| After May 2023 layoffs | ~10,000 | Interest rate normalization |
| December 2024 | ~8,100 | Before AI memo |

Headcount declined primarily from interest rate normalization, not AI.
You can see roughly flat headcount on a normalized basis while still
seeing skyrocketing AI-driven productivity.

## The Surprising Internship Expansion

Months after the "prove AI can't do it" memo, Shopify announced
expansion of their internship program from 75 to over 1,000 engineering
interns — more than 10x increase.

The logic: Shopify is looking for what Tawer calls "AI centaurs" —
people naturally comfortable with AI tools because they grew up with
them. They push existing talent to stay current. The program yields
a huge candidate pool for full-time roles, and interns leave with
real skills for the broader ecosystem.

This reveals a **U-shaped talent market**: very senior people who are
incredibly leveraged by AI, plus a passion for AI-native juniors.

## The Copycat Wave

Box, Fiverr, and Duolingo all jumped on the bandwagon. Results varied:

**Duolingo (cautionary tale):** CEO Luis von Ahn shared a memo in April
2025 about phasing out contractors if AI can do the work. Response was
a disaster — subscription cancellations, "AI first means people last."
He walked it back, admitting backlash dampened customer growth.

**Box (more promising):** CEO Aaron Levie's approach — teams that
automate get to keep the savings for strategic projects. AI savings
return to the team, not the CFO. This converts AI adoption from a
threat into an opportunity for entrepreneurial teams.

**Key lesson:** Shopify's memo worked because they had years of
infrastructure and culture to support it. Copycats without that
foundation stumbled.

## Jensen Huang's Response

In November 2025, Jensen Huang responded to reports of managers telling
employees to use less AI: "I want every task that is possible to be
automated with artificial intelligence to be automated." He called
resistance "insane." Nvidia grew from 29,000 to 36,000 employees in
one year, and Jensen says they are still about 10,000 employees short.

## The Job Market Data

- Job postings requiring AI skills: 5% (2024) to 9% (2025)
- Workers in occupations requiring AI fluency: 1M (2023) to 7M (2025)
- 66% of enterprises reducing entry-level hiring with AI adoption
- 91% report automation-driven role changes
- 84% of companies report significant skill gaps
- AI/ML roles take 89 days to fill (longer than average)
- 90% of organizations use AI in operations; only 9% grade themselves
  as AI mature

## Where This Is Heading in 2026

**AI fluency becomes baseline.** By end of 2026, expect AI fluency
requirements on the majority of knowledge work postings — like email
or spreadsheets.

**Role boundaries continue dissolving.** Designers submitting PRs,
non-engineers prototyping, engineers running side experiments. The cost
of crossing into adjacent domains is dropping rapidly, making
traditional org chart assumptions obsolete.

**New coordination roles emerge.** When everyone can build, someone
must ensure coherence. Expect roles like "design producer" — synthesis
and curation roles for a world with dramatically more output per person.

**Compensation polarizes.** If one AI-fluent worker does what previously
required 2–3, premiums for genuine AI leverage grow while workers whose
productivity does not scale face wage pressure even if absolute output
is constant.

**Entry-level squeeze intensifies.** Traditional entry-level investment
is harder to justify, yet companies want AI-native early-career talent.
Expectations for junior employees are being rewritten with no consensus.

**Training gap becomes strategic liability.** Companies that invested
early (like Shopify's MCP servers and LLM proxy) have compounding
advantages. Late adopters face a chicken-and-egg problem: cannot hire
AI-fluent workers without infrastructure, cannot build infrastructure
without the workers.

## The Bottom Line

The Red Queen memo is about selection pressure. Companies face
extinction-level risk from AI if they do not act. Tailwind (foundational
CSS framework) is struggling to stay afloat despite more usage than ever
— the business model has not stayed competitive.

As Lutke wrote: stagnation is almost certain if we do nothing, and
stagnation is slow-motion failure. If you are not climbing, you are
sliding. This is the biggest training event in all of our lifetimes.
