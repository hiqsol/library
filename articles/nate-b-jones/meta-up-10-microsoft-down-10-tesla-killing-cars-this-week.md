## The Market Cannot Decide What AI Spending Means

Meta and Microsoft reported earnings within hours of each other. Both
beat expectations. Both announced massive AI infrastructure spending.
One stock jumped 10%, the other dropped 11%.

**Meta** posted $8.88 EPS against expectations of $8.16, with revenue
clearing $59.9 billion. Their forward-looking capex guidance was $115
to $135 billion, up from $72 billion in 2025 — nearly doubling infra
spend in a single year. Investors largely cheered because Meta can
point to where the money goes. Its recommendation algorithms already
run on AI. Its advertising engine improves measurably with every model
generation. When Zuckerberg spends $130 billion, shareholders can
trace a line from capital to revenue.

Zuckerberg has also spent billions on the metaverse without a clear
revenue line, but investors were patient because the ad revenue model
kept ticking. The AI investment is easier to support: "If you have a
wallet, AI is going to make it possible for you to spend on Meta."

**Microsoft** told a different story. Azure grew 39%, which sounds
great until you remember it grew 40% last quarter. A single
percentage point mattered in context. Microsoft also disclosed that
45% of its $625 billion commercial backlog is tied up in OpenAI
commitments — nearly half of contracted future revenue depends on a
company it does not control. Deutsche Bank described this as a
"make-or-break moment" with deferred commitments coming due.

The market's message: spending on AI is fine. Spending on AI you do
not own is a different story. Meta chose to build proprietary AI
capabilities — expensive, slow, but defensible. Microsoft chose to
rent frontier models from a lab — and is now structurally exposed to
OpenAI's execution. Even as Meta's Reality Labs burns $6 billion a
quarter on the metaverse.

Markets have started pricing AI strategies differently depending on
**who controls the underlying asset.**

### Apple's Strategic Choice

Apple reportedly passed on investing in Anthropic because Anthropic
was "nickel-and-diming" them on pricing for powering Siri with Claude.
Apple went with longtime partner Google instead, who was willing to
partner more expansively with Gemini. Investors may react differently
than they did with Microsoft, because the street does not believe
Apple can leapfrog in AI on its own — they are looking for an AI deal
to support continued iPhone dominance.

Google is the only hyperscaler where AI is an affordable bet relative
to search revenue. Ironically, a year ago the narrative was that
Google would be disrupted by AI search competitors.

## The Waymo Robo-Taxi Incident

On January 23rd, a Waymo robo-taxi struck a child in Santa Monica
during drop-off hours. The NHTSA opened a preliminary evaluation, and
the NTSB had already opened a separate inquiry into Waymo's Austin
operations, where vehicles illegally passed stopped school buses at
least 19 times since the school year began.

Waymo's response was defensive and tone-deaf — citing a peer-reviewed
model suggesting a human driver would have struck the child harder.
This misses the point entirely. By most statistical measures, Waymo
vehicles are safer than human-operated cars, but data does not matter
when there is a compelling counter-narrative about an unaccountable
autonomous vehicle striking a child.

The key issue: **accountability.** If autonomous vehicles are to gain
societal acceptance, companies must take direct accountability when
things go wrong rather than deflecting with statistics.

## Infrastructure Buildout Continues

**Nvidia invested $2 billion in CoreWeave** at $87.20 per share,
accelerating CoreWeave's buildout of AI-optimized data centers with a
target of 5 gigawatts of capacity by 2030 — roughly the power
consumption of a mid-sized city. Jensen Huang called it "the largest
infrastructure buildout in human history."

**Microsoft signed a $750 million three-year deal with Perplexity**,
giving the AI search company access to OpenAI, Anthropic, and xAI
models through Microsoft's Foundry. Perplexity was careful to note
that AWS remains its primary cloud provider — hedging against
over-reliance on a single hyperscaler.

Both deals signal a shift from the first wave of AI spending (model
development) to the second wave: building the physical and logical
layers that make model deployment possible at scale.

The ultimate unanswered question: is the infrastructure buildout sized
correctly? Is 5 gigawatts too small, too big, or just right? None of
the hyperscalers want to risk leaving cash on the sidelines.

## Tesla Is Now an AI Company That Sells Cars

Tesla's earnings call was not really about cars. It was about robots,
AI investments, and dismantling product lines that no longer fit.

Key announcements:

- **Discontinuing the Model S and Model X** — factory lines will
  convert to Optimus robot production, targeting one million robots
  per year at the Fremont facility
- Optimus production slated to begin late 2026
- **$2 billion investment in xAI** — Grok is now deployed across the
  Tesla vehicle fleet
- More than **one million active Full Self-Driving subscriptions**
- Robo-taxi service expanding to **seven new metro areas** in H1:
  Dallas, Houston, Phoenix, Miami, Orlando, Tampa, Las Vegas
- **$20 billion capital expenditure plan** covering AI training
  infrastructure, robotic manufacturing, and autonomous vehicle
  deployment

Tesla has decided its future lies in AI and robotics, not premium
sedans. The Model S and Model X were always low-volume products
consuming disproportionate factory capacity. Robotics offers higher
margins. It is significant that Tesla would rather be in the robotics
industry at scale than the car industry at scale.

## Anthropic Valued at $350 Billion

Anthropic closed a funding round valuing the company at $350 billion,
nearly double its September valuation. The round was led by
Singapore's GIC and Coatue, with potential for $10 to $15 billion
more if Microsoft and Nvidia join the tail end.

At roughly half the market cap of Walmart, the valuation reflects:

- Claude models gaining enormous enterprise traction, especially in
  safety-critical domains leading to sticky revenue
- Constitutional AI approach clearly differentiated from competitors
- Intensely competitive funding market with investors paying steep
  premiums for perceived winners

Anthropic is openly building AI to build AI — using internal models to
scale their model-building efforts. If that bet pays off, $350
billion may look cheap in retrospect.

## OpenClaw Crosses 100,000 GitHub Stars

Peter Steinberger's weekend project — formerly Clawbot, then Moltbot
after Anthropic raised trademark concerns — has crossed 100,000
GitHub stars. OpenClaw is an AI agent that connects to messaging
platforms (WhatsApp, Slack, Discord, Telegram, iMessage) and enables
autonomous task execution: email management, calendar updates, file
operations, web browsing, cross-platform automation.

The appeal is simplicity: instead of using AI through a chat
interface, you just text it and it does things. The risks are equally
obvious — it requires broad system access, creating a combination of
private data access, exposure to untrusted content, and the ability to
take action.

The growth is hyper-exponential and may hit a million GitHub stars
within months. The democratization of agent AI means more autonomous
systems operating on the internet, with escalating security
implications.

## The Through-Line: Irreversible Commitment

The thread connecting all these stories is commitment:

- Microsoft is committed to OpenAI at a scale that shapes its entire
  financial structure
- Meta is committed to infrastructure spending that assumes AI will
  transform its business
- Waymo is committed to autonomous deployment despite regulatory
  scrutiny
- Tesla is committed to a pivot into robotics
- Anthropic is committed to scaling enterprise revenue

None of these commitments are cheap or easily reversible. The phase
where companies could talk about AI potential is ending. What remains
is execution against bets that are too far in to walk back. The chips
are on the table.
