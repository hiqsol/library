## The Broken Hiring System

LinkedIn is dead for job seekers. The success rate on applications has
dropped to roughly 0.4%. Both sides are in an arms race:

- Candidates use AI to pass interviews, get hired at big salaries, then
  get fired within a week because they cannot explain their own work
- Companies use AI to screen resumes and then penalize candidates whose
  answers sound too much like AI
- 88% of employers admit their own systems cause them to miss qualified
  people

The arms race only exists because everyone accepted the same premise:
as a candidate, you are a supplicant. The employer has all the gates.
Your job is to squeeze through.

## The Alternative: Build Your Own Interface

What if instead of submitting yourself for filtering, you created the
point of contact? A surface where people encounter you on your own
terms — where they can query your experience, explore in depth, and
discover what you actually do instead of scanning claims in a tiny
resume.

Three years ago, building a custom interface required engineering skills
or real money. That gap is closed. You can build working prototypes,

## Why This Works: The Economics of Attention

The scarce resource in hiring is not talent — it is attention. Human
attention. The strategic question shifts from "how do I present my
qualifications" to "how do I capture attention in a way that leads to
genuine evaluation?"

**The cognitive frame shift is critical:**

| Standard Resume | Interactive Interface |
|---|---|
| Recipient is in filtering mode | Recipient is in investigating mode |
| Goal: find reasons to say no | Goal: understand what this person can do |
| 6 seconds of scanning | 5 minutes of actual engagement |

If you can earn five minutes of human attention in this market, you are
gold.

## The Epistemology of Evaluation

When you hand someone a resume, you are making claims. They must choose
whether to believe your assertions with very little basis for that
decision beyond pattern matching.

When anyone can generate a perfect, polished, keyword-optimized resume
in 30 seconds, the signal value of polish collapses to zero. A well-
formatted document with strong action verbs proves nothing in 2026
except that you have access to ChatGPT.

An AI interface changes the epistemology of evaluation. Instead of
asserting claims, you create a tool that demonstrates capability through
use:

- Ask a question, get a detailed answer grounded in real projects
- Ask a follow-up, get the same depth
- Probe an edge case, get a substantive answer or honest acknowledgment
  of a gap

This quality of interaction is difficult to fake at scale. You can write
a resume claiming deep expertise in distributed systems. It is much
harder to train an AI to conduct a convincing multi-turn conversation
about distributed systems architecture if you do not actually understand
it.

## The Discovery Effect

Research on persuasion consistently shows that people believe conclusions
they reach themselves far more than conclusions they are told. When
someone explores your AI interface, they feel like they investigated and
formed their own judgment. But you architected that discovery — you
decided what context the AI has, which questions it handles well, and
wrote the system prompt.

They feel like they discovered the truth. You designed what they would
find.

## The Fit Assessment: Inverting the Power Dynamic

The most powerful feature: a tool where employers can paste a job
description and your AI analyzes it against your experience, telling
them honestly whether you are right for the role.

**Strong fit:** "Let's talk. I have platform API experience, cross-
functional leadership, and I'm comfortable with ambiguity."

**Weak fit:** "This role needs deep consumer product experience. My
career has been in B2B. For this specific position, I'm probably not
your person. But if you have roles that match, let's talk."

This completely inverts the traditional power dynamic. Instead of
"please look at my resume among hundreds and decide if I'm worthy,"
you are saying "let's figure out together whether this makes sense."

This positioning changes how you are perceived. It is also genuinely
useful — hiring managers waste enormous time on mismatched candidates.

## Objections Addressed

**"Nobody will find this."** Correct — this is not a replacement for
distribution. It is a replacement for what people find when they arrive.
Think conversion optimization, not lead generation.

**"Seems like a lot of work."** Consider the alternative: dozens of
hours tailoring applications for a 0.4% success rate.

**"Won't this seem gimmicky?"** Know your audience. For tech roles,
demonstrating AI fluency is a signal you need to send. For very
traditional industries, it may not fit.

**"What if everybody does this?"** Then quality becomes the
differentiator — competing on substance rather than keyword gaming.
That is a healthier equilibrium.

**"I don't have enough experience."** True if you are early career. An
AI trained on two internships will not sustain deep interrogation. For
early career, a portfolio site showing learning journeys and projects
is probably stronger.
