# 4,000 People Lost Their Jobs At Block. Dorsey Blamed AI. Here's What Actually Happened.

[summary](../../summary/nate-b-jones/2026-03-12-4000-people-lost-jobs-block-dorsey-blamed-ai.md)

## The Dorsey Framing Was Wrong

When Jack Dorsey announced Block's layoffs, he used the now-standard narrative: AI is letting us
do more with less. Nate Jones argues this framing is not just incomplete — it produces a
**catastrophically wrong** model of what AI is actually doing to organizations.

The standard forecast goes: AI automates automatable tasks, leaves judgment-heavy work untouched,
net result is a smaller but otherwise similar org. That forecast understates the real impact by a
factor of two or three.

---

## The Coordination Tax

Most knowledge work is not value creation. It is the cost of coordinating humans who cannot share
a brain.

**What knowledge workers actually spend their time on:**
- Writing specs so people not in the room can act on decisions
- Sitting in meetings so eight people can synchronize their state
- Preparing decks so executives can make decisions without reading primary sources
- Filing tickets so work can be tracked across people who can't see each other's progress
- PRDs, sprint planning, status updates, cross-functional syncs, design handoffs, backlog grooming,
  postmortems, retros

The value is the code, the shipped product, the solved customer problem. Everything between
*we understand what to build* and *the thing exists and it works* is process. Process exists
because the execution layer is made of people.

**Research backing:**
- Microsoft 2025 Work Trend Index: average employee spends 57% of time communicating, 43% creating
- Sana Anatomy of Work study: 60% of work is "work about work"
- Average knowledge worker: 11.3 hours of meetings per week (tripled since 2020)
- Typical knowledge worker ratio: ~60% coordination, ~40% actual work

Nate calls this the **coordination tax**. It is not a productivity failure. It is the structural
cost of running an organization with a human execution layer.

---

## What Actually Happens When You Add AI Harnesses

The standard AI forecast assumes: AI automates some tasks inside the existing org chart. The org
chart shape stays the same.

The correct model: when agentic coding harnesses can go directly from insight to code in one
continuous loop, you do not automate tasks inside the existing org. You **delete the need for
the org to be structured that way at all**.

The coordination roles disappeared not because they were automated but because the handoffs they
managed no longer happen. There is no PRD when the person with customer insight can work directly
with an agent to ship the thing. There is no sprint planning when there are no eight engineers
who need to synchronize. There is no status meeting when the status is the commit history.

**Anthropic's own example:** Anthropic no longer uses PRDs. The spec and the implementation
converge into one artifact.

---

## The Org Is Moving to Code

"The org is moving to code" does not mean everyone becomes a software engineer. It means:

- Code is now readable and writable in natural language
- The artifacts of work are becoming machine-inspectable and testable
- The translation layers between people — the roles that existed to convert one person's knowledge
  into a form another person could act on — are being deleted

When translation layers disappear, everybody gets closer to the product. The PM isn't writing a
document an engineer will interpret. They're shaping the actual artifact. The marketing leader
isn't writing a brief for someone else to execute. They're building and testing the landing page
in the same session.

---

## The Flywheel

The structural argument that Nate describes as one of the most important he'll make this year:

1. Remove humans from execution layer → introduce agents
2. Fewer coordination roles are needed
3. Remaining work becomes more verifiable (because it's closer to code)
4. Agents can do more of it
5. Even less coordination is needed
6. Each turn of the loop accelerates the next

This is a compounding effect. The standard forecast that counts automatable tasks misses it
entirely because it counts tasks, not the structural conditions that create tasks.

---

## What Survives

Human work concentrating toward its highest-value form — not disappearing:

- **Product vision** — upstream conviction about what the product is and who it's for
- **Brand as deep thought work** — what the company means, not the guidelines document
- **Genuine customer relationships** — reading between the lines of what a customer says
- **Engineering architecture** — systems thinking under deep uncertainty at the frontier
- **Designing agentic systems** — building, tuning, evolving harnesses; teaching the skill;
  prompting, debugging failure modes, building internal tools

The coordination tax was not just wasting time. It was suppressing the highest-value work:
- Typical product leader: 5–10% of week on actual product vision
- Senior engineer: ~10% of time on genuine architecture thinking

Removing coordination work does not diminish human roles in organizations. It concentrates human
effort on the work that was always most important.

## Two Qualities That Predict Adaptation

Across people from music, development, product, customer success — two traits consistently
separate people who are catching the wave from those getting drowned:

- **Agency** — "This is a skill issue. I can learn this. I can figure this out."
- **Ramp** — The ability to learn quickly, driven by deep curiosity, willing to just go dive in

These two qualities together appear in every person Nate knows who is jumping in and thriving.

---

## The Dorsey Story Revisited

Dorsey used the automation narrative to explain Block's cuts. Nate's read: Block was correcting
for overhiring. The automation framing was a convenient label for a different decision.

The deeper point: the standard "AI automates tasks, some people go" framing is a cover story for
something more structural. Organizations built around coordination overhead will shed that overhead
whether or not they frame it as AI automation.

The bitter pill: AI is revealing that the job was never the coordination. The coordination was the
cost of doing the job with humans. That is — once the coordination tax is gone — actually good news.
