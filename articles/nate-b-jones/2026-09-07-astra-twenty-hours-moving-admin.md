# There Are Jobs You Could Never Give AI. I Gave GPT-6 Astra 20 Hours Of Admin.

[summary](../../summary/nate-b-jones/2026-09-07-astra-twenty-hours-moving-admin.md)

 ## From individual tasks to an entire job

Nate B Jones describes GPT-6 Astra as a change in how much work he can delegate. He delayed his
review to try handing it substantial jobs. His central example is a simulated household move,
which his research puts at more than 20 hours of administrative work. Finding housing, comparing
schools, locating doctors, preparing government appointments, transferring utilities, and
gathering documents create a network of dependencies.

Older models can already perform bounded pieces of that work: verify nearby doctors against
practice websites, compare options, or prepare an appointment checklist. Jones says Astra's
advantage is its ability to connect those pieces across applications, continue after obstacles,
and retain the context of a changing plan. He compares this shift in knowledge work to the
moment Claude Code became capable of carrying a software assignment instead of merely writing
fragments.

## Why a manager agent matters

The difficulty moves from composing individual prompts to managing the overall job. A move can
involve changing dates, missing records, competing neighborhood criteria, and dependent tasks.
Writing a complete specification in advance can become another burdensome project.

Jones proposes a manager loop. A manager agent interviews the user, turns the answers into
assignments, delegates execution, monitors progress, and remains the user's single contact.
Questions cover the destination and deadline, household members, budget, housing preferences,
schools, doctors, vehicles, pets, available documents, and decisions already made.

Independent work can proceed together. Housing research can begin alongside school research.
Doctor searches can use neighborhoods that pass an initial housing review. DMV preparation can
start once destination, vehicle details, and timing are known. A blocked task should not stop
unrelated work.

Jones reports trying this with a simulated move. He describes the agent navigating maps and
practice websites, checking official DMV requirements, and carrying dates into a calendar. This
is a demonstration of preparation and coordination, not evidence that an actual household move
was completed autonomously.

## Supervision and human responsibility

The manager loop is intended to make complex delegation manageable for the person as well as for
the agents. Jones cites a separate demonstration by Matt Shumer involving manager and execution
agents constructing a detailed Manhattan model street by street.

He argues that requiring humans to inspect every intermediate action will not scale. Agents can
perform much of the checking, while the person retains important choices and accountability. An
agent may research homes and explain trade-offs; the person chooses the home. The useful
questions are whether the work is done, whether actions match the assignment, and whether the
agent respected approval boundaries.

Jones expects this to increase the number of decisions people can make by expanding the options
prepared for them. He describes Fable 5.1 as especially useful for discussing an ambiguous
problem before a goal is clear, and Astra as strong at carrying an established goal through a
large, changing body of information. These are his qualitative impressions from use.

## Recipe cards as a practical handoff

Jones calls his proposed job format a recipe card. It names a real outcome, sketches the jobs
inside it, identifies information and access the agent needs, and explains when to return to the
user. A useful card also specifies approval boundaries, expected outputs, and recovery behavior
when a login fails, a document is missing, sources disagree, or a website blocks access.

The initial request can be short: describe the move, ask the manager to interview the household,
delegate research and preparation, continue unblocked work, and bring back decisions requiring
the user. The manager develops the fuller assignment through that conversation.

Jones promotes a paid companion containing 23 detailed cards for household and business work.
The transferable principle is to start with an outcome large enough to matter and let an agent
help define and manage it. Before delegating, ask whether the job can be handed over directly,
whether its complexity warrants a manager agent, and which decisions remain yours.
