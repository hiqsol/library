# Why the Smartest AI Bet Right Now Has Nothing to Do With AI

## The Abundance Narrative vs. Reality

At Davos, Switzerland, Elon Musk told the World Economic Forum that
we are approaching "abundance for all" — ubiquitous AI, ubiquitous
robotics, an explosion in the global economy "truly beyond all
precedent." He recommended people not save for retirement. Meanwhile,
Dario Amodei predicted half of white-collar jobs would disappear, but
that abundance would compensate.

The abundance narrative was everywhere at Davos — echoed through every
panel, every fireside chat, every apres-ski conversation. But the
abundance economy is probably the wrong frame for thinking about the
next few years. Instead, we should think about the **bottleneck
economy**. It is much more practical, much more likely to get you
employed, and much more likely to help you succeed as a builder or
company leader.

## The Bottleneck Principle

Cognizant released research claiming AI could unlock $4.5 trillion in
US labor productivity. But there was a massive caveat: the value will
only materialize if "businesses can implement it effectively." That is
the biggest asterisk imaginable.

Most businesses, according to Cognizant CEO Ravi Kumar, have not yet
done the hard work. That is the gap between the abundance narrative
that sounds so good in Switzerland and the reality. It is not about
model capability — it is about implementation and value capture.

A bottleneck is the binding constraint in a system. It is the
high-leverage constraint that determines actual throughput. If you
improve anything else, you accomplish nothing. But if you improve the
bottleneck even a little bit, everything moves.

This is basic systems thinking, and most people ignore it. They
optimize for whatever is visible, whatever is comfortable, whatever
they are already good at. They work harder instead of differently.

## Historical Pattern: Bottlenecks Create Empires

Every dominant organizational form emerged to dissolve a specific
bottleneck:

- **Dutch East India Company** — solved the capital lockup problem of
  multi-year oceanic voyages
- **Railroads** — cracked the energy constraint on overland transport
- **Banks** — emerged to allocate capital across time
- **Stock exchanges** — aggregated capital at scales exceeding any
  private fortune
- **Walmart** — solved the information bottleneck in retail supply
  chains

The pattern is consistent: whoever solves the binding constraint
captures disproportionate value. Everybody else participates in the
abundance that is created.

## AI Era Bottleneck 1: Atoms, Not Bits

The binding constraint on AI capability is increasingly physical, not
digital. Jensen Huang told Davos that AI needs more energy, more land,
more power, and more trade-skilled workers.

- Contemporary hyperscale data centers consume 100+ megawatts
- Training a single frontier model can require sustained exaFLOPS of
  compute for weeks
- Electricity demands approach those of small nations
- Google recently disclosed they are bottlenecked on grid connections
- DRAM prices are skyrocketing due to memory scarcity

Physical infrastructure operates on very different timelines than
software. You can ship a new model in months if you have the compute,
but building a data center takes years. Permitting alone can take
years. Expanding grid capacity is even harder.

The companies that understand this are securing power purchase
agreements, advanced memory purchase agreements, locking up
construction capacity, and building utility relationships years in
advance. The companies that do not are assuming compute will magically
appear.

TSMC and a handful of other fabs control advanced semiconductor
production. Nvidia's market position is not really about better
chips — it is about having chips at all when everyone else is capacity
constrained.

Jensen Huang calls the resulting demand for facilities workers
"high-quality jobs" — trade craft salaries in this space have nearly
doubled.

## AI Era Bottleneck 2: The Trust Deficit

When Demis Hassabis spoke at Davos, his biggest concern was not
technical. It was "the loss of meaning and purpose in a world where
productivity is no longer the priority." He also worried that we lack
"institutional reflection" about AI. What he is really saying: these
are coordination problems, and coordination runs on trust.

When anyone can generate sophisticated AI content at the touch of a
button — text, images, video, code — the cost of generation
collapses. But the cost of trust does not get cheaper. If anything,
trust gets harder because synthetic and authentic become
indistinguishable.

Trust is the infrastructure of coordination. It reduces transaction
costs across the economy. When trust degrades:

- Deals take longer
- Verification layers multiply
- Everything gets harder

Who captures value here? Whoever can mediate trust — institutions that
verify, authenticate, and certify. Platforms that develop reputations
for signal in a world of noise. Networks where track records are
visible and accountability exists.

## AI Era Bottleneck 3: The Integration Gap

Cognizant's research points to something specific: $4.5 trillion in
value is conditional on implementation. This is the integration
bottleneck.

AI has general capacity but no specific context:

- A general AI can write code, but it does not know your codebase
- A general AI can draft strategy, but it does not know your
  competitive dynamics
- It can discuss board politics generally, but it does not know your
  board

The gap between "AI can do this" and "AI does this usefully right
here" is $4.5 trillion. Bridging it requires context that is often
tacit — embedded in practices, relationships, not just documents. The
person who has been at the company for 20 years knows things that are
not written down anywhere. That knowledge is not promptable.

Some companies will figure out how to solve this integration problem
and unlock massive productivity gains. Others will deploy AI tools
that sit unused or, worse, generate outputs that look deceptively
productive but connect to nothing that matters.

## AI Era Bottleneck 4: The Coordination Problem

AI does not dissolve the challenge of getting humans to agree or
align. It might make coordination even harder — when anyone can
generate sophisticated arguments for any position, groups have even
more trouble reaching consensus.

Larry Fink's warning at Davos was pointed: if AI does to white-collar
workers what globalization did to blue-collar workers, we need to
confront that reality directly. The IMF managing director said a
"tsunami" was hitting the labor market, with 40% of jobs globally
affected, adding: "We don't know how to make it inclusive."

The people closest to knowing how to put AI and jobs together are not
at Davos. They are building workflows where AI and people work
together.

## Individual Bottlenecks: The Fractal Principle

The bottleneck principle is fractal — it applies to individuals too.
Your output, impact, and leverage are functions of which bottleneck
you are solving.

The old individual bottlenecks are dissolving. Access to information
is abundant. Access to tools is cheap. Skill acquisition is rapidly
getting easier. It used to take five or more years to become a
proficient programmer. AI compresses or eliminates those runways.
Amodei noted that his own engineers no longer program from scratch —
they supervise and edit the work of models.

### Taste and Judgment

When generation is cheap, curation of what is good becomes expensive.
Knowing what to make, when to stop, what is good enough versus what
is actually good — these capacities still take time to learn. The AI
can generate a hundred options, but knowing which option is right is
still human terrain.

The challenge: taste develops slowly while AI devalues output. People
who are thriving are narrowing their focus earlier, pushing rapidly to
the frontier past where "AI good enough" is acceptable.

### Problem Finding Over Problem Solving

AI solves well-specified problems with increasing fluency. But
specifying the right problem remains very human. The analyst who knows
which questions to ask vastly outpaces the analyst who can answer any
question. The skill increasingly is direction-setting, not execution.

### Context as a Moat

Context and institutional knowledge are becoming moats for individuals
the way data is becoming a moat for companies. The person who
understands why the organization really operates the way it does, what
stakeholders actually want beneath what they are saying — that tacit
knowledge is very hard to replicate and increasingly valuable.

This creates a strange dynamic: juniors who would historically
accumulate context through years of apprenticeship now face a
compressed path. The grunt work was where context got absorbed, and
the implicit knowledge that made senior people valuable came from
thousands of small exposures that never happen if AI handles all
tasks.

### Execution and Follow-Through

AI can generate plans, but turning them into reality requires a human
who decides, commits, persists, navigates politics, holds people
accountable, and keeps going when things get hard.

Steve Jobs did not just have a brilliant mind — he had relentless
execution. He called Corning to get them to produce Gorilla Glass. He
called Google to say the yellow in the "O" looked terrible on the
iPhone and his engineers would be there to fix it.

### Tolerance for Ambiguity

The environment is shifting fast. Best practices change constantly.
The constraint is your ability to metabolize change — how much
uncertainty you can hold without freezing while continuing to execute
on a longer-term perspective.

## The Leverage Shift

The old model of talent development was linear: acquire skills, trade
time for money, let it accumulate slowly. The new model has a
different shape. Some individuals are discovering 10x leverage through
AI augmentation — not by working harder, but by identifying their
bottleneck and dissolving it.

The diagnostic question is deeply personal: **What is constraining my
output right now?** Not what you wish was constraining you. Not what
was constraining you three years ago. Not the constraint you built
your identity around solving. The actual binding constraint today.

## The Only Question That Matters

Intelligence is getting cheaper. The promise of abundance is real. AI
will keep getting smarter. Cognitive output will keep getting easier
to produce every month.

But abundance does not create value directly. Abundance shifts where
scarcity lives. The question is not whether to believe in coming
abundance as an article of faith. The question is: **where are the
bottlenecks, and are you positioning yourself and your business to
solve them?**
