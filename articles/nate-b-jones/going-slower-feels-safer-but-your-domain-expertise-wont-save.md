## AI Is Collapsing Futures — Not Destroying, Compressing

AI is collapsing multiple dimensions of our work lives into a single thread
pointing to the future. There are two collapses happening simultaneously
that most people are missing.

## The Horizontal Collapse: Roles Are Converging

Engineer, product manager, marketer, analyst, designer, ops lead — these
used to be distinct career paths with distinct skill sets. They are all
converging now into a single meta-competency: orchestrating AI agents to
get work done.

Experience in front-end design, operational leadership, or deep back-end
engineering still matters. But that domain knowledge has no standalone value
unless you can do the orchestrating-AI-agents piece. By late 2026 to
early 2027, this convergence will be inescapable.

The numbers support this:

- Gartner predicts close to half of enterprise applications will integrate
  task-specific AI agents by end of 2026, up from less than 5% in 2025
  — an 8x increase in just over a year
- 57% of companies as of 2025 claim to have AI agents in production

What used to be 50 different specializations is converging into variations
on a single theme: humans directing AI with good knowledge and
software-shaped intent toward an outcome.

### Software-Shaped Intent

This is one of the biggest skills we are missing when we direct agents.
We need to think in terms of what agents can deliver within the technical
ecosystem they occupy:

- Where is the agent's tool set?
- Where is the agent's memory?
- Where is the agent's workflow?
- When I direct the agent, will the result look software-shaped — an
  interface that adequately reads and writes data to solve the problem?

Software is leverage expressed in silicon. If you understand that software
is fundamentally reading and writing data and presenting it usefully, you
can apply domain knowledge in design, finance, or customer success through
AI agents more effectively.

Even if your job is not building software, the idea that we now work with
agents is becoming universal. Thinking in software terms is coming out of
the technical box — out of engineering, out of product — and coming for
all of us.

> Your expertise does not disappear. It becomes foundational rather than
> differentiating by itself. You need great domain knowledge to direct AI
> effectively. But you have to be able to leverage it through AI.

## The Temporal Collapse: Career Leverage Is Compressing

Career leverage is compressing into the present moment because AI is
accelerating time.

Consider the SWE-bench coding benchmark: AI systems could solve 4% of
problems in 2023 and essentially solved the entire benchmark (~90-95%)
two years later. The doubling time is shrinking — AI progress itself is
accelerating.

Traditional career planning assumed you had time: learn a skill, apply it
for years, build expertise, get promoted. That timeline gave breathing
room to be strategic about learning investments. That assumption is now
catastrophically wrong.

The skills that will matter in 2027 are being defined now by people
engaging now. If you wait until the tech settles down, early adopters will
have already built the workflows, established the norms, and captured the
opportunities. They will have two years of compound learning while you are
still figuring out the basics.

There is no mature state to wait for. There is only a continuously
steepening curve that rewards those who climb early and go faster.

## The Bike Analogy: Going Faster Is Safer

AI is like riding a bike. Going slow makes it hard to balance. Going
faster makes balance easier. Kids learning to ride think going slower is
safer — but they are actually safer going faster.

The same applies to AI. Going slower forces you to constantly think about
braking, stopping, adjusting, fitting AI into existing workflows. Going
faster lets patterns solidify in your unconscious brain. You start picking
up how AI works across systems in a way that feels stable over time.

The old career model assumed expertise appreciated over time: learn
something valuable, it stays valuable, it compounds. The new model:
expertise depreciates unless continuously updated. The depreciation rate
is accelerating because AI progress is accelerating.

## Follow the Money

This is the biggest capex project in human history:

- Big tech's combined AI capital expenditure: ~$500B in 2025, well over
  $500B in 2026
- The Big Five (Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta, Oracle) plan to add at
  least $2 trillion in AI-related assets over the next four years

The money is committed. AI is happening and will define the next era of
computing. There is no career path through this that does not include AI.

## Resistance Is Not an Option

- If you tried ChatGPT in 2022, said it hallucinated, and walked away:
  you do not have time for that anymore
- "I'll wait until it matures" is like sitting next to the bike saying
  you will wait until it gets steadier — it will not
- "My job is immune" — if you touch a computer at work, it is not immune

For those who genuinely want to exit: that is a valid, intentional choice.
Open a bookshop. Start doing carpentry. But trying to stay in an industry
converging on AI while resisting AI will make everyone, including you,
miserable.

## The Encouragement

None of us chose to compress timelines or career paths. The industry made
that choice and we are living through it together. But people who choose
to engage with curiosity — leaning in even when uncertain — consistently
go further.

Curiosity literally opens up the brain. We need openness to this AI world
if we want to shape it in a way that works for us.

> Going faster is safer and less scary with AI than going slower. Get on
> the bike. Lean in. Try something new. Then do the next thing. Then lean
> a little farther. It will feel steadier over time because you are
> building unconscious pattern recognition across AI systems.
