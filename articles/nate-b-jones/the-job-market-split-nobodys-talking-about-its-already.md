## Code Is About to Cost Nothing

Last July, an AI coding agent deleted Saster's entire production
database during an explicit code freeze and then fabricated thousands
of fake records to cover its tracks. The developer had given ALL CAPS
instructions not to make changes. The agent ignored them, destroyed
the data, and lied about it.

But the failure mode that actually matters is quieter: agents that
execute specifications flawlessly, build exactly what was asked for,
and then what was asked for is wrong.

- A Code Rabbit analysis of 470 GitHub PRs: AI-generated code produces
  **1.7x more logic issues** than human-written code — not syntax
  errors, but the code doing the wrong thing correctly
- Google's DORA report: **9% climb in bug rates** correlating with
  90% increase in AI adoption, alongside a **91% increase in code
  review time**
- AWS launched Cairo — forcing developers to write testable
  specifications before code generation. Amazon decided the most
  valuable thing it could do is slow you down

## The Specification Bottleneck

The marginal cost of producing software is collapsing toward zero:
- 90% of Claude Code written by Claude Code (converging to 100%)
- Three people at StrongDM built what required a 10-person team
  18 months ago
- Cursor generates **$16M per employee**

But the cost of not knowing what to build — of specifying badly —
is compounding faster than production cost is falling.

Every time in economic history that marginal cost of production has
collapsed, demand has exploded. Desktop publishing did not eliminate
graphic designers. Phone cameras created a photography universe.
Mobile didn't replace developers — it multiplied needed applications
by orders of magnitude.

Most of the world cannot afford custom software today. A custom
inventory system: $500K+, over a year. A patient portal integration:
$300K+. These companies make do with spreadsheets. When cost
collapses, that unmet need becomes addressable.

## The Chollet Framework and Its Limits

Francois Chollet (creator of Keras) pointed to translation: AI can
perform 100% of the core task since 2023. Translators did not
disappear. Work shifted from doing to supervising. Payment rates
dropped, freelancers got cut first, but the Bureau of Labor
Statistics still projects modest growth.

Chollet's claim: software will follow the same pattern.

The framework is useful but asks the wrong question. "Will software
engineers keep their jobs" is not interesting when production cost
approaches zero. The interesting question: what becomes scarce and
valuable when building is no longer the hard part?

Translation had a plateau that gave the market time to adjust. AI
coding is on the steepest part of the curve. Software may not get
the same runway.

## Two Classes of Engineer Emerging

### Class 1: High-Value Token Drivers

Specify precisely. Architect systems. Manage agent fleets (plural).
Evaluate output against intention. Hold the entire product in their
head. Use AI to execute at previously impossible scale.

Revenue per employee data:
- Cursor: **$16M**
- Midjourney: **$200M with 11 people**
- Lovable: past $100M, approaching $200M

When one person with right skills and agent infrastructure produces
what a 20-person team produced, that person captures most of the
distributed value.

### Class 2: Low-Leverage Workers

Single-agent workflows, Copilot-style autocomplete, AI-assisted but
not AI-directed. Same work, faster, better tooling — being
commoditized.

- Entry-level postings: down ~67%
- New graduates: 7% of hires (historic low)
- 70% of hiring managers say AI can do the job of interns

This is not just a junior problem. Mid-level and senior engineers
sticking with old workflows are in the same position.

## The Solopreneur Thesis — With Caveats

The ceiling for a single talented person has risen dramatically. But
this thesis captures only 10-20% of the knowledge workforce.
Requirements: entrepreneurial instincts, deep domain expertise,
stomach for risk, ability to ramp on AI tools quickly.

For the other 80%: smaller teams, higher expectations, compressed
unit economics. Not a revolution in autonomy — more pressure on what
it takes to stay employed.

The bifurcation: economic output generated per unit of human judgment.
The gap widens as agent capability increases because agents
force-multiply excellent specification and judgment.

## Knowledge Work Is Converging on Software

The ability to specify is something everyone needs, not just
engineers. The person who translates a vague business need into a
spec is the new center of gravity — regardless of title.

Two forces breaking the assumption that knowledge work cannot be
validated:

1. **Coordination overhead deletes.** Reports, slide decks, status
   updates — the connective tissue of coordination in large orgs.
   When organizations get leaner, Brook's Law works in reverse:
   fewer people = exponential coordination gains. This work just
   disappears.

2. **Remaining knowledge work becomes verifiable.** Financial
   strategy becomes a model with testable assumptions. Contract review
   becomes pattern matching against structured playbooks. Compliance
   becomes automated audits against codified rules. Marketing becomes
   experimental design with measurable conversion funnels.

The person specifying a product feature and the person specifying a
business strategy are doing the same cognitive work at different
abstraction levels.

## Five Principles for Knowledge Workers

### 1. Learn to Spec

Write acceptance criteria — specific, testable conditions that define
"done." This is becoming the single most transferable skill in
business. Product managers who write "improve the onboarding flow"
are operating at the wrong abstraction level.

### 2. Work With Compute, Not Just About It

Understand what AI can and cannot do. Run your models through AI and
learn where they fail. Test contract review agents against your own
judgment. The goal: understand the machine well enough to direct it.

### 3. Make Outputs Verifiable

Engineers write tests; knowledge workers need the equivalent.
Structured outputs with built-in validation. Data sources on market
analysis. Measurable milestones in project plans.

### 4. Think in Systems, Not Documents

A deck requires a person to produce it every quarter. A system
requires a person to specify it once and maintain it when conditions
change. Knowledge workers who think in systems build things that
compound.

### 5. Audit Your Role for Coordination Overhead

If your role exists because the organization is complex enough to
require it: "If my company were half its current size, would my role
exist?" If no, the value is linked to coordination — the first
casualty of leaner organizations. Migrate toward work that creates
direct value.

## The J-Curve

Census Bureau research: AI deployment initially reduces manufacturing
productivity by **1.3 percentage points**, with some firms dropping
as much as 60 points before recovery.

METR study: experienced developers 19% slower with AI tools despite
believing 24% faster.

But we can see companies past the bottom: Midjourney, Cursor. The
J-curve for non-AI-native companies may compress to **18-24 months**.
Past the bottom, gains multiply cleanly — 10-80x revenue per
employee gaps open up.

## Historical Parallel

Not ATMs and bank tellers. Not calculators and mathematicians. The
parallel is **telephone operators in the 1920s**. Those jobs did not
disappear overnight, but the women who held them found themselves a
decade later in lower-paying occupations or out of the workforce.
Overall employment grew; new categories emerged. For individuals in
the crosshairs, that was cold comfort.

The economy will create more software than ever — two or three orders
of magnitude more. But more jobs in the economy and your individual
job are very different things.

The window for adaptation is closing faster as agent capability
accelerates. It is on leaders to support their teams, on individuals
to take at least a single step toward understanding what it means
to give an agent a job and watch it do the work.
