# Beyond One-Shot Prompting: Workflows, Product Judgment, and Career Resilience

[article](../../articles/andrew-ng-laurence-moroney/2026-09-05-ai-workflows-product-judgment.md)

The repost's headline is false: Andrew Ng never says prompting will die in six months.
He never says graphs replace prompts, and “graph(s)” does not appear in the transcript.
His six-month remark is about how often his favorite AI coding tool may change.
The philia upload is dated September 5, 2026, but the recording is Stanford's
CS230 Autumn 2025 Lecture 9, recorded November 18, 2025.
The repost is 1:43:02, which is 126 seconds shorter than Stanford's 1:45:08 original.
Automatic captions may contain errors.

Ng speaks only to 16:31; Laurence Moroney delivers most of the lecture and Q&A.
Ng argues that faster coding shifts the bottleneck toward product judgment:
decide what to build, specify it, show users, gather feedback, and iterate.
His observed shift from 4–8 engineers per PM toward 2:1 or 1:1 is anecdotal.
He encourages projects and hard work but allows for health, care, and life constraints.

Moroney's three pillars are technical depth, business focus, and delivery.
His dividing line is production: useful systems require UX, evaluation, review,
documentation, maintenance, risk control, and measurable value.
Code may be cheap to generate; finished, engineered code is not.
Technical debt is unavoidable, so teams should ask whether value justifies it.
SwiftUI is a UI framework, not a language; Swift is the language.

His practical agent loop is: understand intent, plan, use tools, reflect, and repeat.
A private sales case allegedly shifted work from an 80/20 research/selling split
and recovered 10–15% of wasted time, but no company, sample, or method is disclosed.
Prompts remain inside this workflow rather than disappearing.

Video demos suggest that story context and tool-aware planning can improve output.
Yet Moroney admits the comparison was not like for like and rested on his gut.
The generated acting remained “wooden”; reported $2–$3 costs and 2–3 tries are anecdotal.

Several market claims need correction.
McKinsey did not report an 85% AI-project failure rate; the statistic is misattributed.
The “80% of YC companies” claim came from a16z and implies about 16–24% of applicants.
AI venture capital was not broadly drying up: 2025 private investment rose 127.5%.

Moroney forecasts hosted “big AI” and self-hosted “small AI” diverging over five years.
Privacy, latency, customization, and cost can favor local or open-weight models.
Exact 7B-to-50B or 300B capability equivalences remain unsupported.
Arm SME2 accelerates matrix work through CPU instructions; Apple's Neural Engine
is a distinct accelerator, not SME2.

Labor pressure is real but causation is unsettled: software-developer employment
among ages 22–25 fell nearly 20% from 2024, while recent graduates had 5.6%
unemployment and 42% underemployment in 2026 Q2.
The durable role is trusted adviser: filter hype, start with the problem, manage risk,
and connect deep engineering, user needs, production quality, and business value.
