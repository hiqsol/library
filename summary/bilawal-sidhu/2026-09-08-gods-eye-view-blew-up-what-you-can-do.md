# Summary

[article](../../articles/bilawal-sidhu/2026-09-08-gods-eye-view-blew-up-what-you-can-do.md)

God's Eye View combines a 2D or photorealistic 3D globe with live and near-live public data,
AI voice control, and extensible map layers. Its launch video passed 500,000 views, and the
open-source repository reached number one on GitHub Trending.

## Setup and Interface

- Pinokio 8.2+ offers non-coders a one-click installer for Windows, macOS, or Linux.
- Coders can give the GitHub repository and setup prompt to a local coding agent such as
  Codex, Claude Code, Cursor, or Antigravity.
- The keyless version uses Esri 2D; Google or Cesium access enables photorealistic 3D tiles.
- Optional credentials are managed locally through the Power Up panel.

## What It Can Do

- Track commercial and military aircraft using a global volunteer receiver network; contacts
  show speed, altitude, nearby traffic, and a 250-kilometer context radius.
- Follow a flight in cockpit view, step between nearby tracks, and listen to available police,
  weather, and ordinary radio stations.
- Use GPT Realtime voice control to enable layers, fly to places, mark locations, orbit the
  camera, draw routes, animate journeys, and explain visible infrastructure.
- Map vessels, traffic congestion, and public cameras; Austin camera images refresh every
  five to ten minutes to limit individual-vehicle tracking.
- Build and replay camera-shot sequences for screen-recorded tours and film projects.
- Explore earthquakes, NASA FIRMS fire detections, data centers, cables, and infrastructure.

## Data Limits

- Public cameras can provide current but deliberately low-frame-rate images for privacy.
- Free terrestrial AIS vessel data is strong in Europe and the United States but weak near
  the Strait of Hormuz because receivers need coastal proximity.
- Satellite radio-frequency vessel data can cost thousands or tens of thousands of dollars,
  sometimes with surge pricing in high-interest regions.
- Public satellite imagery in war zones can be delayed by about three days because governments
  restrict open-source intelligence that adversaries could exploit.
- New terrain needs time to load or low-flying aircraft may temporarily clip through the map.

## The Larger Idea

- The project is a foundation, not a fixed dashboard: coding agents can fetch new open data,
  plot it, and create specialized layers through natural-language instructions.
- Users reconstructed a Nepal earthquake, landslide, and flood by combining official reports,
  the flood path, and geolocated social-media footage.
- Community proposals already include automatic license-plate readers and inexpensive
  software-defined radios costing tens of dollars.
- Personal, non-commercial use can be nearly free without voice, but a hosted service would
  require commercial data licenses and may need a paid voice tier.
- Historical data is possible but substantially more expensive.

The goal is an extensible window for monitoring, reporting, geopolitics, and infrastructure.
