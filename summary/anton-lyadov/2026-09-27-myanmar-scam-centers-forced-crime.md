# Inside Myanmar's Forced-Scam Economy
> **Content note:** Trafficking, confinement, violence, financial loss, and death.
*The source documentary uses Russian automatic captions; some details may be mistranscribed.*
Myanmar's scam compounds harm two groups at once: trafficked people forced into online
fraud, and people whose trust and savings are stolen through that coerced work.
OHCHR, UNODC, and INTERPOL document deceptive recruitment, transit through Thailand,
passport seizure, confinement, long shifts, quotas, violence, ransom, and resale.[1][2][3]
Mae Sot is the Thai border city; Myawaddy is the area across the Moei River in Myanmar.
KK Park, south of Myawaddy, and Shwe Kokko, to its north, are separate hubs.
US Treasury findings allege that armed and commercial networks supplied land, security,
utilities, and protection, but sanctions determinations are not criminal convictions.[4][5]
In Anton Lyadov's documentary, Saken says a plausible IT offer took him through Thailand
to KK Park, where he was confined, exhausted, forced into fraud, and abused for months.
Artyom says he suspected the proposed job involved fraud, then faced guarded confinement,
withheld documents, punishment around him, and a threatened transfer to another compound.
Their accounts fit regional findings, but their case-specific details remain testimony.
Initial awareness of dubious work is not consent to detention, violence, or forced labour.
Authorities should use individualized, trauma-informed screening and the non-punishment
principle for unlawful acts people were compelled to commit through trafficking.[1][2]
Willing perpetrators may also be present; status cannot be assigned to a group wholesale.
The fraud uses fabricated relationships and fake investment platforms showing invented
returns, then blocks withdrawals or demands more money. AI-assisted faces, voices, and
profiles make identity checks harder, so even a live call is not proof of identity.[2][3]
The film's alleged KK Park executions, bodies in the river, organ harvesting,
Belarusian death-and-ashes story, and “American psychologists” are not verified.
Nor are a universal $300,000 quota, 200 accounts per worker, a 10,000–15,000 population,
or 500 kilometres of jungle. The film also understates $3,000 in rubles by a factor of ten.
The October 2025 KK Park raid was a major disruption, not complete destruction.[6][7]
China sentenced 11 Ming-family members and associates in September 2025 and executed them
on 29 January 2026; their network was in Laukkaing, not KK Park.[8]
Before travel, independently verify the employer and obtain a written contract and visa.
Leave passport copies and an itinerary with family; do not surrender a passport or phone.
Set a discreet code phrase. If danger is suspected, contact national police and the relevant
embassy or consulate. Do not advise confrontation or an improvised escape.
