# AI Is Automating More AI Research, but the Recursive Loop Is Still Open

[article](../../articles/pourya-kordi/2026-11-21-google-deepmind-recursive-self-improvement-race.md)

Google's AI-assisted R&D is accelerating. Sergey Brin called it an “RSI treadmill,” Sundar Pichai
expects frontier models to help build successors, and Demis Hassabis warned that removing human
review could create a dangerous closed loop. None said that loop is closed today.

Google released Gemini 4 Argon, Carbon, Hydrogen, and Neon. Barium was reported as an internal
checkpoint; Krypton and Oxygen remain unverified. “Seven months of silence” also ignores several
Gemini 3.x releases.

Dream-RSI rewrites the search policy around a fixed coding agent and tests candidates by replaying
stored search trees. It cut model calls about 42% in specific Lasso runs and achieved a 2.09× result
on one GPU-kernel task. It did not modify the base model or prove open-ended improvement.

The video's claimed 72%-to-81% Dream-RSI coding gain was not found in the paper. Replay covers only
previously explored branches and may select policies that overfit the recorded search history.

DeepSeek automates task and environment synthesis, but also changed its architecture, pretraining,
and systems. Its benchmark gains therefore cannot be credited solely to AI-generated tasks.

Kimi K3 builds persistent multi-application environments and recursively expands a knowledge graph.
Its reported 1.000 score was for one camera-repair-system replication task, not a general success
rate. Recursive graph expansion is not autonomous modification of Kimi's own weights.

Levent Alpöge and an internal Claude model produced a huge Lean proof of the Riemann Hypothesis.
Independent experts verified the formal artifact. Human steering was substantial, and the proof is
not yet a conventional readable argument, so “Anthropic solved it” is imprecise.

AlphaGo Zero improved under fixed human-designed rules, objectives, architecture, and training.
Go's cheap self-play and exact outcomes do not establish that open-ended science supports the same
loop. The Lee Sedol match involved the earlier AlphaGo, not AlphaGo Zero.

Nuclear fission illustrates positive feedback, but reactors routinely control chain reactions.
AI has no measured neutron-multiplication equivalent; compute, experiments, verification,
institutions, and diminishing returns can interrupt the cycle.

Research on expert intuition supports learning from regular patterns and reliable feedback. It does
not show that research taste is fictional. TypewriterLM and historical materials embeddings provide
ways to study temporal prediction, but historical back-training remains an untested proposal.

Current systems automate more coding, curriculum generation, evaluation, and search. A closed loop
would additionally require reliable verification, transferable gains, sufficient resources, secure
operation, short iteration cycles, and repeated progress despite diminishing returns.

The evidence establishes consequential AI-assisted R&D—not an autonomous intelligence
explosion. The Cardboard segment is sponsored marketing rather than supporting evidence.
