# AI Is Automating More AI Research, but the Recursive Loop Is Still Open

[article](../../articles/pourya-kordi/2026-11-21-google-deepmind-recursive-self-improvement-race.md)
Google's release cadence and AI-assisted R&D have accelerated. Sergey Brin called this an “RSI
treadmill,” Sundar Pichai expects frontier models to help build successors, and Demis Hassabis
warns that removing human review could create a dangerous closed loop. None said that loop is
closed today.
Google officially released Gemini 4 Argon, Carbon, Hydrogen, and Neon. Barium was reported as an
internal checkpoint; Krypton and Oxygen remain unverified. “Seven months of silence” ignores
several Gemini 3.x releases, while claims about Neon's personality are subjective.
Dream-RSI rewrites the exploration policy around a fixed coding agent and evaluates candidates
by replaying stored search trees. It reduced calls by about 42% in specific Lasso runs and
produced a 2.09× result on one GPU-kernel task. It did not alter the base model or prove
open-ended improvement.
The video's 72%-to-81% Dream-RSI coding result was not found in the paper. Replay also evaluates
only previously explored branches and can select policies that overfit the recorded search
history.
DeepSeek automates large-scale task and environment synthesis, but also changed architecture,
pretraining, and systems. Its reported 54.4-to-74.2 and 82.7-to-90.6 benchmark gains therefore
cannot be credited solely to AI-generated tasks.
Kimi K3 builds persistent multi-application training environments and recursively expands a
knowledge graph. Its reported 1.000 result was one camera-repair-system replication task, not a
general success rate. Recursive graph expansion is not autonomous modification of its own
weights.
Mathematician Levent Alpöge and an internal Claude model produced a huge Lean proof of the
Riemann Hypothesis. Independent experts verified the formal artifact. Human steering was
substantial, and the proof has not yet become a conventional readable argument, so “Anthropic
solved it” is imprecise.
AlphaGo Zero improved under fixed, human-designed rules, objectives, architecture, and learning
algorithm. Go's cheap self-play and exact outcome do not establish that open-ended science can
use the same loop. The Lee Sedol match involved the earlier AlphaGo, not AlphaGo Zero.
Nuclear fission is a useful positive-feedback analogy, but reactors routinely control chain
reactions. AI development has no measured equivalent of a neutron multiplication factor;
compute, experiments, verification, institutions, and diminishing returns can interrupt the
cycle.
Kahneman and Klein link reliable intuition to learnable regularities and adequate learning
opportunities. Tetlock and therapist studies caution against assuming tenure creates judgment,
but do not show that research taste is fictional or slightly-better-than-random selection is
sufficient.
Materials-science embeddings found historical signals associated with later research.
TypewriterLM is a real 7.24B-parameter historical model, but its 1913 cutoff leaks and it has
not demonstrated scientific forecasting. Historical back-training remains an untested proposal
for research taste.
Current systems automate more coding, curriculum generation, evaluation, and search. A closed
loop would also need reliable verification, transferable gains, short iteration cycles,
sufficient resources, operational autonomy, security, and repeated progress despite diminishing
returns.

The evidence establishes a consequential AI-assisted R&D feedback loop, not an autonomous
intelligence explosion. The Cardboard segment is sponsored product marketing rather than
supporting evidence.
