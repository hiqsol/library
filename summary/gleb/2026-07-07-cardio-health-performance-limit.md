# When More Cardio Stops Meaning More Health

[article](../../articles/gleb/2026-07-07-cardio-health-performance-limit.md)

## The thesis

Gleb argues for a dose-response curve, not an anti-cardio message.
Inactivity is common and harmful; regular moderate activity can yield large gains.
Health may later plateau while endurance and race performance keep improving.
At very high chronic volumes, recovery costs and some risks may rise.

## What the video claims

Small runner and triathlete comparisons reportedly found lower testosterone.
Gleb links heavy endurance work with bone injury and a U-shaped pattern for knees.
He reports poorer fertility biomarkers, but not proven reductions in conception.
He also cites depression, anxiety, cognition, immune, and respiratory concerns.
Arrhythmia, gut stress, cancer, and mortality are mentioned as well.
These are not one proven testosterone-driven causal chain.
Biomarkers, symptoms, diagnoses, fertility, cancer, and death are distinct endpoints.

## MET-hours and individual load

A MET expresses intensity relative to rest; MET-hours equal MET value times hours.
They are not simply exercise hours and require an assumed intensity for conversion.
The captions say "50 MIT hours"; the likely term is 50 MET-hours per week.
Gleb loosely equates this to about an hour of running daily, with little other work.
That is an uncertain illustration, not a universal threshold or prescription.
Fitness, pace, adaptation, heat, pollution, terrain, and health alter the true strain.

## Recovery and an illustrative mix

Daily calorie balance can hide low energy availability during and after long sessions.
Gleb stresses timely fueling, adequate total intake, sleep, and adaptation.
Stable weight and feeling energized do not prove recovery is adequate.
For a prepared person, he illustrates two hours each of easy running and strength,
plus 10 to 20 minutes of brief high-intensity work per week, not as a personal plan.

## Evidence limits and measured use

The description has no study citations, so the claims cannot be checked here.
Small samples, cross-sectional data, elite selection, and confounding all matter.
Acute biomarkers may not predict clinical outcomes; captions may also contain errors.
The sound takeaway is diminishing returns, not a precise cliff at one weekly dose.
Seek medical or sports-medicine assessment for concerning symptoms and before major
training changes, especially with persistent fatigue, injury, illness, or heart symptoms.
