# What BYD Teardowns Reveal—and What They Cannot Predict

The video's central manufacturing thesis is well supported. UBS's 2023 Seal teardown estimated a
roughly 25% sustainable BYD cost advantage, a 16% modelled gross margin, and a 5% EBIT margin. It
attributed the result to vertical integration in batteries, powertrain, electronics, and vehicle
design. Reporting on the analysis put BYD's in-house share near 75%, not “almost the entire car.”

BYD's 8-in-1 drive system genuinely combines eight major functions, while its Cell-to-Body Blade
Battery uses cobalt-free LFP cells in a structural pack. Those choices can reduce factory cost,
weight, and interfaces. Public sources do not prove that a failed sensor requires replacing the
whole drive unit or that ordinary floor damage will write off a Seal. These are open repair risks.

Nikkei BP published a Seal teardown in September 2023, but its bibliographic record says 494 pages,
not 350. The US$6,400 price comes from secondary reporting. The claim that Toyota, Honda, and
Nissan engineers bought it was not verified. Caresoft did dismantle a Seagull and called it a
“clarion call” and “an exercise in efficiency.”

Euro NCAP gave the Seal five stars: 89% adult, 87% child, 82% vulnerable-road-user, and 76% safety-
assist scores. Original UK models sat in insurance groups 48D and 50D; revised 2026 cars fell to
46D and 48D. Insurance groups include multiple risk factors and do not isolate battery repair cost.

UK data recorded 51,422 BYDs versus 45,513 Teslas in 2025. That marque comparison mixes BYD BEVs
and plug-in hybrids, while Tesla is BEV-only. Globally, BYD did lead pure-electric sales in 2025,
with 2.26 million BEVs against Tesla's 1.64 million, about 38% more.

The £45,730 Seal list price is correct. Used listings near £28,500 were plausible in October 2026,
but an asking-price gap is not a controlled depreciation rate. Auto Express forecast about 48%
retained value after three years, competitive with several named EV rivals.

BYD ended 2025 with 125 franchised dealership sites, not necessarily 125 service workshops. Its
October 2026 locator showed 123 service entries, including Inverness and Truro. Rural gaps can
still matter, but the video's Highlands and Cornwall warning was too broad.

The Seal-specific warranty table supports six years or 93,750 miles for the car and eight years or
155,350 miles for the battery, with a 70% state-of-health threshold. BYD's generic text instead
says 125,000 miles, so buyers should check their exact model-year document. Transfer applies “in
most cases,” not automatically.

BYD began as a roughly 20-person rechargeable-battery company in 1994–95, entered cars in 2003,
and received a roughly 10% investment from Berkshire-controlled MidAmerican in 2008. Its ten-
million milestone combines BEVs and plug-in hybrids. It sold about 4.6 million NEVs in 2025.

Toyota evaluated BYD cars and formed an R&D venture to learn from its battery and low-cost
engineering, but the claim that it dismantled several BYD models was not verified. Benchmarking is
not evidence that Japanese engineers were “terrified.”

The video is persuasive about BYD's manufacturing discipline but too certain about repairability,
write-offs, depreciation, and future parts. A teardown can explain how a car was built; it cannot
supply ten years of British ownership data. The title is sensational, while the competitive threat
and the need for careful long-term ownership checks are real.
