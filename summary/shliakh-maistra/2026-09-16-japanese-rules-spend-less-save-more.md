# Eight Japanese Rules for Spending Less and Saving More

[article](../../articles/shliakh-maistra/2026-09-16-japanese-rules-spend-less-save-more.md)

## Core idea
- More income need not mean more freedom when each raise becomes a higher spending baseline.
- Ask what deserves your money and space, not merely what you can afford.

## 1. Kakeibo: make spending conscious
- Log each expense and ask, “Would I seek this without the discount?”
- A UAH 2,000 item cut to UAH 1,200 is UAH 1,200 spent, not UAH 800 saved, if unplanned.

## 2. Mottainai: use value already bought
- Before upgrading, ask what your current phone, car, or tool can no longer do.
- Replace unsafe, broken, or inadequate items; do not pay only for short-lived novelty.

## 3. Ma: leave financial space
- Unspent money needs no immediate assignment; keep an accessible reserve.
- Let it cover a car repair, treatment, an unexpected bill, or a difficult month.

## 4. Wabi-sabi: age is not lost value
- Separate cosmetic wear from failures of function, safety, or reliability.
- A marked table, scratched car, or ten-year-old possession may still serve well.

## 5. Shibui: let quality be quiet
- Test a status purchase by asking, “Would I choose it if nobody saw it?”
- A car or renovation is visible; cash reserves and freedom from debt usually are not.

## 6. Danshari: stop excess at the door
- Ask what the item changes, what it replaces, and whether one you own does the same job.
- Count its second price: space, attention, care, repair, and eventual disposal.

## 7. Kaizen: improve one repeated habit
- Change one recurring cost, such as monthly services you barely use, not your whole life.
- Review another category next month and save part of the next income increase.

## 8. Taru o Shiru: define enough
- Set your own sufficient car, home, quality, and price before ads or neighbors do it.
- More is not always better; admire an upgrade without replacing what already works.

## One-month test
- Choose one rule rather than imposing all eight at once.
- Track declined purchases and what, if anything, your life actually lost.
- Keep the rule if more money remains with little or no loss in daily life.
