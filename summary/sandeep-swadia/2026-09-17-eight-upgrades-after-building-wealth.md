## Eight upgrades

1. Sleep: Swadia says recovery supports health, judgment, and the ability to earn.

2. Health habits: a better gym or visiting trainer may buy accountability and time.

3. Bottlenecks: solve a frequent constraint instead of buying generic premium gear.
Swadia's standing desk and noise-cancelling headphones target sitting and noise.
4. Education: fund finance, communication, negotiation, and time-management skills.
He cites repeated modeling practice and a mentor who had built a $60 billion company.

5. Diversification and reserves: protect wealth from concentration and forced selling.
He cites a stock moving from $19 to $140 and a leveraged fund's rapid collapse.
Swadia advises hedges and emergency cash; these can reduce upside and add costs.
6. A BATNA fund: savings can preserve the option to reject a poor job or deal.
Swadia says his fund supported six to nine months of job searching.
7. Expert safeguards: complexity may justify tax, planning, legal, and estate help.
A friend's insured fire loss illustrates planning for risks that could return one to zero.

8. Soul: fund personal passions, close relationships, shared memories, and generosity.
His examples include music study, dinners, walks, family trips, causes, and volunteering.

## Priority and cautions

First address health, sleep, and daily constraints; then close a costly skill gap.
Next protect gains with reserves, options, diversification, and proportionate expertise.
Then reserve money and time for passions, relationships, and service to others.
Productive spending adds capacity or choice; lifestyle inflation adds recurring overhead.
Status spending signals wealth; meaningful spending serves a specific person or value.
Every upgrade has opportunity costs, and financial choices need advice fitted to the person.
