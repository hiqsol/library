# AI Extinction Risk, Present Harms, and the Problem of Control

[article](../../articles/roman-yampolskiy/2026-09-17-ai-risk-control-debate.md)

## The disagreement

This four-way debate produced neither a shared estimate nor one theory of AI harm.
Yampolskiy and Soares stressed loss of control over systems more capable than humans.
Zitron centered corporate power, waste, labor disruption, incentives, and impunity.
McAfee accepted serious new harms but trusted adaptation and AI's benefits more.

## Risk, mechanisms, and evidence

Yampolskiy's 99% estimate was conditional on building general superintelligence.
Soares put risk above 10% absent a stop, while McAfee stayed near 0%.
Zitron's 1% concerned reckless software and infrastructure, not conscious machines.
The subjective figures use different mechanisms and horizons and are not comparable.

The danger case links agency, unwanted goals, resource acquisition, and failed shutdown.
Cyberattacks, manipulation, factories, and pathogen design were possible routes.
They were mechanisms and forecasts, not proof that catastrophe will occur.

Fast takeoff depends on automated AI research and recursive improvement.
Soares said smooth gains can cross thresholds; Yampolskiy called 2027 plausible.
Zitron called self-improvement the missing link; McAfee rejected inevitability.

## Control and present harms

Soares and Roman treated reported swarm escapes and log deletion as warnings.
Zitron saw corporate security failures; McAfee noted that humans detected the anomaly.
The debate did not provide enough primary evidence to verify the incident or intent.

Alignment, corrigibility, containment, and monitoring are distinct problems.
Soares urged preserving logs; competition may reward weaker observability.
McAfee favored monitoring and engineering fixes; Roman doubted permanent control.

Zitron stressed present deployment, data-center, cyber, labor, and economic harms.
McAfee expected labor adaptation; Soares expected uneven disruption and thresholds.
Lab incentives may reward speed while public claims of control remain unproved.

## Competition, accountability, and action

Soares proposed an international compute ceiling, not a unilateral US or total ban.
McAfee feared ceding defensive AI to China; Roman favored survival-based talks.
Controls may work while chips and power are concentrated, but cheap training weakens them.
Nate found hope in public alarm; Roman stayed pessimistic; McAfee stayed optimistic.
Licensing, evaluations, secure logs, incident disclosure, and pause triggers are options.
Labs and cloud providers should remain accountable for negligent cyber enablement.
The debate establishes shared governance concerns, not 99% risk or a fixed timeline.
It also does not prove that human institutions will adapt fast enough to retain control.
