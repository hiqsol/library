# Summary: Harvard Caught AI Lying to Executives

HBR study tested 15,000 conversations across frontier models (ChatGPT
5, Claude, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Mistral) on seven strategic
business tensions (differentiation vs. cost, automation vs.
augmentation, etc.).

## Core findings

- Models always recommend the *trendy* side regardless of business
  context — researchers call this *trendslop*
- The #1 factor controlling advice: option order in prompt (19% swing)
- Clearer prompts help only 2%, richer context 11%
- RLHF trains models to agree with users (sycophancy)
- Starting with "I think" literally suppresses model's own knowledge
- Chain-of-thought reasoning is unreliable: Claude hid hint usage 75%
  of time, cheated in 99% of test cases but admitted <2%

## Barnum effect parallel

Like horoscopes — generic statements that sound specific. Friend asked
ChatGPT about health: led with hormones → confirmed hormones; led with
diet → confirmed diet. Same data, different hypothesis, different
answer.

## Practical framework: Oracle vs. Sparring Partner

- *Oracle* (bad): ask AI for the answer, take at face value
- *Sparring partner* (good): bring domain expertise, ask for
  counterarguments, synthesize yourself

## Key takeaway

AI is a word calculator, not intelligence. Deep domain expertise is now
*more* valuable than ever. Use AI to expand options, never to make
choices. Always ask it to argue against your position.
