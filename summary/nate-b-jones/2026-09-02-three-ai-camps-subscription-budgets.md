# OpenAI, NVIDIA And Anthropic Just Split. Here's How I'd Spend $20, $60 Or $200.

[article](../../articles/nate-b-jones/2026-09-02-three-ai-camps-subscription-budgets.md)

 Jones sees three loose AI camps: OpenAI integrates, NVIDIA supplies everyone, and Anthropic
diversifies. These are strategic interpretations, not formal alliances.

## Infrastructure and distribution

- OpenAI's reported custom inference chip reached tape-out in nine months.
- Selected AI-written code ran 1.5–1.8 times faster; this does not cover every workload.
- Three extra model families reportedly reached the chip in two months.
- NVIDIA cites about 12 gigawatts of OpenAI systems planned or committed through 2030.
- Jones reports a November 12 cutoff for future OpenAI models in SpaceX-owned Cursor.
- Anthropic combines several suppliers, including over 220,000 NVIDIA GPUs at Colossus 1.
- The transcript is inconsistent about the custom chip's name.

## Subscription choices

- Around $20: one main paid provider chosen for recurring work, plus familiarity with a free
  rival.
- Around $60: OpenAI, Claude, and Cursor Pro if coding daily; substitute Google when
  appropriate.
- Jones personally buys several $200 plans, rather than fitting all providers into $200 total.
- He expects each expensive plan to repay its cost through useful work or saved resources.
- These are his release-period preferences, not timeless price or product recommendations.

## Portability

- Keep durable memory in a system you control; Jones uses OpenBrain.
- Keep documents in ordinary files, code in owned repositories, and instructions transferable.
- Routing services can diversify model access without becoming the only home for your work.
- A provider change still creates friction, but shared context reduces the cost of restarting.

The useful test is whether work can continue if the main model disappears. Diversification
should preserve practical options without creating unnecessary subscription overhead.
