# The People Getting Promoted All Have This One Thing in Common — Summary

Entry-level tech hiring has collapsed 50%+ since 2019. Entry-level
postings across the US dropped 29 percentage points (Jan 2024–Jan 2026).
The traditional career ladder is being disassembled as AI automates the
routine tasks that once trained newcomers.

The answer is extreme high agency — an internal locus of control where
everything is perceived as within your influence. Psychologist Julian
Rotter's concept from the 1950s. High agency people respond to perceived
limitations with "That's a skill issue."

Research supports this: internal locus of control correlates with 20–30%
higher academic performance, greater persistence, better professional
outcomes, stronger entrepreneurial activity, and more effective leadership.

AI is the greatest equalizer for agency. Barriers that required years of
education or network access can now be overcome with a laptop and
determination. AI does not care about pedigree.

The forcing function: AI accelerates divergence between high and low
agency people. Gaps that took 20 years to emerge now happen in months.
Career stagnation that took a decade becomes apparent in 1–2 years.

The say-do ratio matters enormously. Collapse the distance between
intention and action. Ship halfway done rather than wait for perfection.

Solo founders rose from 22% (2015) to 38% (2024) of startups. AI
unicorns reaching $1B valuations with ~20 employees. Ma'or Shlomo built
Base44 to $80M acquisition by Wix in 6 months — solo, no VC.

Dario Amodei predicts first one-person $1B company in 2026. Sam Altman
says 2028.

High agency people are obsessed with value creation. They believe the
world is bendable through sufficient contribution.

Passive AI use (answer engine) produces bad outcomes. High agency AI use
(exploration, learning, prototyping) produces extraordinary results —
same tools, radically different outcomes.

Action steps: identify a goal outside your perceived control, determine
what it takes to bring it inside, use AI to map and accelerate the path,
then act today — not after more research.
