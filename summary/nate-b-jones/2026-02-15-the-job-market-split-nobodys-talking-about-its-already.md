# The Job Market Split Nobody's Talking About — Summary

**Published:** 2026-02-15 19:00

The marginal cost of producing software is collapsing to zero (90%
of Claude Code self-written, 3-person teams replacing 10-person
teams, Cursor at $16M/employee). But the cost of specifying badly
compounds faster than production cost falls. AWS launched Cairo to
force specs before code generation — Amazon decided slowing developers
down is more valuable than speeding up.

AI code produces 1.7x more logic issues than human code (Code Rabbit,
470 PRs). Google DORA: 9% bug rate climb correlating with 90% AI
adoption increase, plus 91% more code review time.

Two classes of knowledge worker are emerging:
1. **High-value token drivers:** specify precisely, manage agent
   fleets, hold entire products in their heads. Revenue/employee:
   Cursor $16M, Midjourney $200M/11 people.
2. **Low-leverage workers:** same work + better tools, being
   commoditized. Entry-level postings down 67%, 70% of hiring
   managers say AI can do intern work.

Chollet's translation analogy (AI performs 100% of task, translators
persist) is useful but incomplete. Software is on a steeper curve
with less adjustment time.

The solopreneur thesis applies to 10-20% of knowledge workers
(entrepreneurial instincts + domain expertise + risk tolerance).
For the other 80%: smaller teams, higher expectations, more pressure.

Knowledge work is converging on software. Two forces: (1) coordination
overhead deletes as orgs get leaner (Brook's Law in reverse), (2)
remaining work becomes verifiable (financial strategy = testable
model, compliance = automated audits).

Five principles: learn to write specs with testable acceptance
criteria; work with compute hands-on; make outputs verifiable; think
in systems not documents; audit your role for coordination overhead
(would it exist if the company were half its size?).

The J-curve is real: Census Bureau finds AI initially reduces
manufacturing productivity by 1.3 percentage points. But companies
past the bottom see 10-80x revenue/employee gaps. Non-AI-native
companies may compress the J-curve to 18-24 months.

Historical parallel: telephone operators in the 1920s, not ATMs.
Overall employment grew, but individuals in the crosshairs ended up
in lower-paying work. The economy will produce orders of magnitude
more software — but more jobs in aggregate and your specific job
are different things.
