OpenClaw is the most consequential ChatGPT-level moment in AI since ChatGPT itself. The media
covers it as either a horse race or a security disaster. Both miss the real story: every major
company responding to OpenClaw has made a *different strategic bet* based on their position.

*Three axes define every agent product in the OpenClaw ecosystem:*
- Where does the agent run? (local / cloud / hybrid) — determines privacy and security posture
- Who orchestrates the intelligence? (one model / multi-model harness / model-agnostic)
- What is the interface contract? (messaging app, desktop, phone)

*The competitors and their bets:*
- OpenClaw: sovereignty play. Local, modular, any LLM, any messaging platform. 250,000 GitHub
  stars. 30,000+ publicly exposed instances with weak auth. 800+ skills compromised in supply
  chain attack. For developers who want maximum control and will manage the security surface.
- Perplexity Computer: delegation play. Fully cloud. Describe outcome, system decomposes into
  subtasks and executes. Claims support for tasks lasting months. $200/month. Also planning a
  local version to capture OpenClaw-adjacent users. For knowledge workers and enterprise teams
  who want outcome-level work without infrastructure responsibility.
- Manus (Meta, $2 billion acquisition): distribution play. Not an enterprise-quality argument —
  a scale argument. Meta wants agent eyeballs inside the Meta ecosystem. Uses a mix of Meta
  models and outside models. The data question: do you trust Meta? For consumers and small
  businesses nervous about the terminal and already in the Meta ecosystem.
- Anthropic Dispatch: safety play. Message into Claude Code from your phone. Single-threaded,
  one instance, currently primitive. Also a Telegram option for developers. No multi-model
  routing. Anthropic sees disintermediation as brand reinforcement: Claude is the safe option.
  For non-technical professionals invested in the Claude ecosystem.

*The Lovable irony:* Lovable surpassed $300 million ARR and was the most imitated AI product of
2025. On March 19, 2026, it announced expansion beyond website building into general-purpose
agent execution. The most copied product is now copying. Lovable's entire interface assumed a
human types a prompt. That assumption is dissolving as users want autonomous multi-step agents.
Walking the tightrope from human-mediated to agent-first is hard when millions love it as-is.

*2026 thesis — relentless simplification:* Agents are compressing the interface layer. Every
vertical tool is under pressure to collapse into a single conversational agent. Survivors will
either go deep enough to have unique capability (Lovable's potential path) or broad enough to
become a default delegation layer (OpenClaw, Perplexity). The middle — good but not best-in-
class, not general enough for general-purpose — is a recipe for product death.

*Positioning map:*
- OpenClaw: upper right — maximum technical complexity/risk AND maximum user control
- Perplexity Computer: lower left — minimum risk, minimum user control
- Manus: middle, with data privacy as its own axis
- Dispatch (Anthropic): low complexity, moderate control, more options coming
- Lovable: low technical complexity, very high user control — almost its own island

*The overarching question of 2026:* how we delegate agentic trust. Which niches in the OpenClaw
ecosystem will other players carve out by serving users who want OpenClaw-style experiences but
trade down on sovereignty for delegation, safety, or simplicity? That lens is more useful than
horse-race hype or security panic. The 250,000 GitHub stars signal this ecosystem is stable and
not going anywhere. Agents are how commerce will be conducted.

Nvidia built NeMo Claw and Jensen compared OpenClaw to Linux. OpenAI acqui-hired Peter
Steinberger. Forks include ZeroClaw (Rust rewrite), Moltbot (enterprise), OpenFang (agent OS),
Nanobot/Hong Kong (4,000 lines). Every weakness in the original becomes a startup thesis.
