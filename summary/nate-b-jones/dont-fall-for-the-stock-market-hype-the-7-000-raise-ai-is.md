# Don't Fall for the Stock Market Hype: The $7,000 Raise AI Is Giving You — Summary

A fictional Substack memo from 2028 wiped $100B+ in market cap. IBM
cratered 13% from an Anthropic blog about COBOL. Seven major AI sell-offs
driven by economic fear, not technology failure.

**Doom case (Catrini Research):** White-collar workers = 50% of US
employment, 75% of discretionary spending. Top 20% = 65% of consumer
spending. AI displacement spiral: AI improves -> companies cut payroll ->
savings fund more AI. Fictional S&P drop: 38%, unemployment: 10.2%.
Private credit ($2.5T) rolled up SaaS at valuations assuming perpetual
growth — those assumptions are dying.

**Bull case:** Alex Emis (U Chicago Booth) modeled Catrini's scenario —
it requires no policy response, which is historically untenable. Michael
Bloke: AI agents compress services costs 40-70%, returning $4,000-$7,000
annually per median US household, tax-free. Census Bureau: 532,000 new
business applications in Jan 2026 alone (+7% MoM), trend accelerating
since 2021.

**The missing variable: Capability-Dissipation Gap.** Two curves: AI
capability (exponential) vs. societal dissipation (flat). The gap explains
why capabilities are stunning but disruption modest, why both narratives
sound compelling, why a blog post crashes stocks.

**Four forces of inertia:** (1) Regulatory — COBOL runs 95% of US ATMs,
hundreds of billions of lines. (2) Organizational — gap between "Claude
can do parts of this job" and "we've reorganized" is enormous; RAG pilot
programs abandoned because AI moved past them. (3) Cultural — Toby Lutke
(tech-fluent Shopify CEO) had to mandate AI in performance reviews; mid-
market firms won't replicate this. (4) Trust — building verification
systems requires capital and cultural transformation most lack.

**The opportunity:** The gap creates asymmetric returns for early adopters.
Large firms have capital but carry inertia (18 months from "saves $10M" to
savings). Small firms/individuals have speed. AI-native people think in
hours, not quarters.

**Lutke's playbook:** Mandate AI use, personal eval folder per model
release, require AI in every prototype phase (failure = eval for next
model), build organizational muscle memory. His insight: tools are
instruments to be played, not replacements for the player.

**Three takeaways:** (1) Stock volatility creates mispriced assets — buy
side not priced at all. (2) Doom narrative is 10-50x more viral than
counter-evidence; useful as policy warning, not career planning. (3) Map
where you sit on the two curves. The career move: become the person who
says with authority "I've tested this, here's what AI does in our
workflow, here's the plan."
