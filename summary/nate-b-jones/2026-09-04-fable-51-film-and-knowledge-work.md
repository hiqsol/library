# Everyone's Testing Claude Fable 5.1 On Code. It Made Me A 37-Second Film.

[article](../../articles/nate-b-jones/2026-09-04-fable-51-film-and-knowledge-work.md)

 Jones compares Fable 5.1, Fable 5, and GPT-5.6 Soul on practical knowledge work. These are
personal work samples rather than controlled benchmarks.

## Financial analysis

- Fable 5.1 low: seven workbook sheets, 13 slides, working formulas, and a $1.15 base-case
  estimate.
- Missing sources and checks sheets made assumptions harder to inspect.
- Fable 5.1 extra: nine sheets, 15 slides, 26 sources, and more explicit deal uncertainty.
- Extra separated businesses and closing timing, modeled funding, and added valuation checks.
- Soul: 10 sheets and 10 slides, with dedicated sources and checks sheets.
- Missing financial history limits valuation claims; unclear transcript prices are not
  normalized.

## Writing and visual work

- A 100-word Toyota test showed clearer causality and less decoration with Fable 5.1.
- Soul traded some factual density for smoother prose suited to a general audience.
- Fable 5.1 built a 37-second Blender walkthrough from a Seattle property address and a loose
  brief.
- It created geometry, lighting, landscaping, and camera motion, then inspected and revised
  renders.
- Stylized trees and simple glass limited realism; the result served concept communication.

## Cost and workflow

- Reported API rates: $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens.
- Cached reads fell from $1 to $0.25 per million tokens.
- Anthropic's cited estimates: about 25% lower typical cost and 45% lower cache-heavy agentic
  cost.
- API economics do not directly translate to subscription limits, which Jones still finds tight.
- Use low effort for drafts, another model for inspection, and extra effort for consequential
  checks.

The main distinction is between a finished file and inspectable reasoning. Knowledge work
benefits from iteration, explicit assumptions, and a handoff another person can audit.
