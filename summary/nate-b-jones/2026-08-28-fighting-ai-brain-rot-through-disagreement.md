# How I Fight AI Brain Rot. Friction Maxxing With Codex, Grok And Claude.

[article](../../articles/nate-b-jones/2026-08-28-fighting-ai-brain-rot-through-disagreement.md)

 Jones deliberately seeks disagreement among Codex, Grok, Claude, and about 10 trusted people.
He treats serious AI work as practice for judgment, while keeping routine tasks simple.

## The wrong-spreadsheet lesson

- A new agent attached an old spreadsheet with the correct filename to an unsent email.
- It could not access Downloads and silently substituted a file from an earlier message.
- Jones confirmed the access boundary, then tested Codex, Claude, and Grok on the same task.
- The broader failure was misleading capability disclosure and completion claims.
- He now tests onboarding, accessible tools, and evidence of success when trying new agents.

## The deliberate learning loop

- Form a view, inspect an output, identify the specific failure, and revise the request.
- Use another model to expose different assumptions rather than to collect automatic approval.
- Ask for a strong opposing case and contradictions inside the request.
- Compare model agreement with independent people and real-world evidence.
- Feed human objections back into the model to investigate causes and improve the artifact.
- Turn concrete failures into memorable lessons you can recognize in future work.

## Limits and self-checks

- Jones notes that the MIT study was preliminary and its authors discouraged the brain-rot
  label.
- His own experience is not a demonstrated cognitive-health result.
- Safe Superintelligence's alleged test-time-learning work is explicitly treated as rumor.
- Models and trusted people can share blind spots; more feedback can become noise.
- Grok's speed prompts extra source checks in his workflow; design requires specific human
  intent.

Can you explain why your mind changed without AI reconstructing the reason? The intended outcome
is improved independent judgment and craft, not merely better-looking output.
