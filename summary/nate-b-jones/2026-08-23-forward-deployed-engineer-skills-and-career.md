# OpenAI Pays $280,000 For This Job. You Don't Have To Be An Engineer.

 Forward-deployed engineering translates general AI capability into a working customer
deployment. Jones identifies three parts: workflow judgment, technical delivery, and post-launch
ownership.

## Compensation and requirements

- Jones cites OpenAI base pay up to $280,000 plus equity and a $300,000 Handshake posting.
- He contrasts a tens-of-thousands training ambition with 86 trained in the initiative
  discussed.
- These are time-specific examples, not guaranteed pay or proof that coding is unnecessary.
- OpenAI's cited role requires front-end and back-end engineering.
- Adjacent implementation, solutions, operations, and product titles have different technical
  balances.

## The insurance example

- Maya finds incomplete claim packets waiting several days before anyone notices missing
  documents.
- Hundreds of delayed claims can represent roughly 1,800–2,000 aggregate claim-days each month.
- That is waiting time across cases, not a measured total of employee labor hours saved.
- A narrow intake checker removes a bottleneck while leaving consequential claim decisions to
  people.
- Evaluate detection time, false positives, missed documents, and review burden.
- About 50 labeled examples can support a bounded evaluation set.

## Four-week portfolio project

- Week one: inspect 10–20 completed cases and reconstruct recurring delays.
- Week two: observe actual users, select an intervention, estimate impact, and define access
  limits.
- Week three: build a small system and test clean and difficult historical cases.
- Week four: watch two or three users, fix failures, and document the measured result.
- Experienced practitioners may complete this faster; the month is a suggested first-project
  pace.

Engineers need customer judgment; operators need technical depth; implementers need ongoing
ownership. Use existing industry knowledge and prove a specific deployment outcome instead of
waiting for a title.
