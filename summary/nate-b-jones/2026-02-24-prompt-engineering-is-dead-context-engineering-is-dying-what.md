# Prompt Engineering Is Dead, Context Engineering Is Dying — Summary

**Klarna case:** AI agent did the work of 853 FTEs, saved $60M, resolved
2.3M conversations in month one across 23 markets/35 languages. Resolution
time: 11 min -> 2 min. Then customers complained — generic answers, no
judgment. CEO admitted "lower quality." Klarna rehired human agents. The
AI succeeded brilliantly at the wrong objective.

**Three disciplines of AI input (cumulative):**

1. **Prompt Engineering** — Individual, synchronous, session-based. The era
   of "how to write the perfect prompt." Now a warm-up act.

2. **Context Engineering** — Curating the information environment agents
   operate within. RAG pipelines, MCP servers, organizational knowledge
   structuring. Necessary but not sufficient.

3. **Intent Engineering** — Encoding organizational purpose (goals, values,
   trade-off hierarchies, decision boundaries) so agents optimize for what
   the company actually needs, not just what's measurable. The discipline
   almost nobody is building for yet.

**Scale of the problem:** 84% of companies haven't redesigned jobs around
AI (Deloitte, 3,000+ leaders, 24 countries). Only 21% have mature agent
governance. 74% report no tangible AI value. Yet 57% put 21-50% of digital
transformation budgets into AI (~$700M avg for $13B revenue company).

**Microsoft Copilot:** 85% of Fortune 500 adopted. Only 5% moved beyond
pilot (Gartner). ~3% of M365 users became paid Copilot users. Microsoft
slashed internal sales targets. Deploying AI without intent alignment =
hiring 40,000 people without telling them what the company does.

**Three-layer intent gap:**

1. **Unified context infrastructure** — Every team rolls its own stack.
   Shadow agents mirror shadow IT but stakes higher (agents act on data).
   MCP is de facto standard (~100M monthly SDK downloads) but protocol
   adoption != organizational implementation.

2. **Coherent AI worker toolkit** — Individual AI use vs. organizational
   leverage = 30% gains vs. 300% gains. Fluency scales through shared
   infrastructure, not training alone.

3. **Intent engineering proper** — OKRs assume human judgment. Agents need
   machine-readable goal structures, delegation frameworks with resolution
   hierarchies, and feedback loops. Google's Agent Development Kit and
   DeepMind's 5-level autonomy framework are early sketches.

**Why it hasn't been built:** (1) Genuinely new — humans were the intent
layer until long-running agents broke that model. (2) Two-cultures problem:
strategy people don't build agents, builders don't understand strategy.
(3) Most orgs have never made goals explicit and structured.

**The race that matters:** Not intelligence but intent. Mediocre model +
extraordinary intent infrastructure beats frontier model + fragmented
knowledge. If OKRs aligned thousands of humans (1970s), intent engineering
aligns thousands of agents (2026). The most important 2026 AI investment
is organizational intent architecture, not model subscriptions.
