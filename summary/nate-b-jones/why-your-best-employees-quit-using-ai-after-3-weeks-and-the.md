# Why Your Best Employees Quit Using AI After 3 Weeks — Summary

Microsoft tracked 300,000 employees using Copilot. Excitement peaked
at week 3, then cratered. Most quietly stopped. Result: ~20% monthly
active users, ~80% dormant seats. The survivors figured out that AI
is a management skill, not a tool skill.

**The missing middle:** AI training bifurcated into 101 (prompting
basics) and 401 (technical implementation), skipping the 201 level
where most productivity gains live. The 201 question: "Where does
this tool fit in my workflow, and when is output trustworthy?"

**Ethan Mollick's insight:** Best AI users are good managers. Token
consumption leaderboards are dominated by senior execs and
distinguished engineers — not for technical skill, but management
skill + domain knowledge.

**BCG/Harvard jagged frontier study:** Inside AI's capability
boundary, consultants finished 12% more tasks, 25% faster. Outside
it, they were 19 percentage points less likely to reach correctness
than those working without AI. People apply a single mental model
and miss where AI hurts rather than helps.

**Two successful patterns:** Centaurs (clean human/AI division, best
for high-stakes) and Cyborgs (fluid integration, best for creative
work). The 201 skill is switching between them.

**Six 201-level skills (none are prompting):**
1. Context assembly — curating the right inputs
2. Quality judgment — knowing when to trust vs. verify
3. Task decomposition — breaking work into AI-appropriate chunks
4. Iterative refinement — treating first output as starting point
5. Workflow integration — embedding AI into how work gets done
6. Frontier recognition — knowing where AI fails for your work

**Adoption blockers:**
- Fear of doing it wrong (permission gap, not skill gap)
- IT solving the wrong problem (infrastructure vs. capability)
- Generic tools not retaining feedback at enterprise scale
- Apprenticeship collapse (juniors lose judgment-building work)

**Organizational moves:** AI labs with non-technical users, systematic
discovery across functions (Trek Bicycle: 40+ use cases), visible
success/social proof, 5+ hours formal training, explicit positive
guardrails, systematic failure case sharing.

**Key takeaway:** Getting the 80% from 101 to 201 is the difference
between companies humming on AI and companies where it stalled. It
is a judgment investment, not a technology investment.
