# The App Is Disappearing From View, Not From the Stack

[article](../../articles/nate-b-jones/2026-10-04-post-app-ai-agent-interface.md)

Nate B. Jones's “end of the app era” is better read as a forecast about interfaces.
Apps are not ending; software value is separating from mandatory direct interface use.
The emerging stack has four parts: interface, governed workflow,
data/service/execution, and an agent layer that orchestrates the rest.
DoorDash shows how a company can surrender the screen but retain the transaction.
Its September 2026 text-ordering launch was a U.S. beta with a waitlist,
and its MCP corporate-ordering connector was a limited beta with sign-in and consent.
The merchant network, account, checkout, and fulfillment remain DoorDash's value.
Meta's Muse for Small Business connected services including QuickBooks and Shopify,
but Jones's prediction that consumer agents will become business hubs is a 2027 forecast.
Salesforce's Koa, built on NVIDIA Nemotron, was in selected customer pilots.
Claudeforce was also in pilot or open-beta territory, not proof of broad interoperability.
Salesforce popularized SaaS and cloud CRM; it did not invent SaaS.
Microsoft's persistent Autopilot agent was expanding to private preview.
These launches establish strategic direction, not adoption or business outcomes.
Crustdata's recruiting example is a vendor case study, not an independent evaluation.
It shows a customer keeping structured data while replacing a packaged interface.
Workday's Payroll Agent shows the opposite boundary: payroll rules, approvals,
permissions, audit, and dependable execution remain valuable under an agent surface.
MCP connects AI clients to tools and data; A2A coordinates agents with other agents.
Neither guarantees portable memory, semantics, policy, payments, or reliable completion.
Agents also need distinct identities, sponsors, narrow scopes, expiry, and revocation.
Prompt injection remains unresolved when untrusted emails or documents can steer actions.
Payments require explicit intent, limits, confirmation, dispute handling, and liability.

Reliability keeps visual review and exception surfaces alive.
Stanford reported 66.3% on OSWorld in 2025, leaving substantial failure risk.
Each model call and connector can add latency, stale data, or partial completion.
The right metric is cost per successful, governed outcome—not AI volume or speed.

Contrary market evidence matters: 2025 mobile downloads, spending, and time set records.
Stanford found broad organizational AI adoption but single-digit agent deployment
across nearly all business functions. Apps remain healthy while agents remain early.

The hidden switching cost is the employee workflow: prompts, skills, memory,
data access, review habits, and tacit knowledge accumulated around one agent.
Leaders should map these dependencies before consolidating vendors on token price.
Workers should document inputs, approvals, exceptions, checks, and measurable outcomes.
Portability requires tested exports, migration exercises, and vendor-neutral evaluations.

The likely future is hybrid: agents reduce navigation and app switching,
while structured interfaces remain for comparison, monitoring, editing, and trust.
The app is not disappearing from the stack; it is becoming one optional doorway.
