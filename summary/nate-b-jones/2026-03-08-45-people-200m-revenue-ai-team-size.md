# Summary: 45 People, $200M Revenue — AI and Team Size

Nate argues the real problem behind meeting overload is team size — teams are
3–10x too big and AI is making it worse by amplifying output through broken
coordination structures. AI note-taking apps are "barnacles" that miss the root
cause.

**The math of five:** Communication pathways grow as n(n-1)/2. Five people = 10
pathways (manageable). Twenty people = 190 (unworkable). Dunbar's 1992 research,
military structure (US fire team = 4 + leader), Bezos's two-pizza rule, and Fred
Brooks's 1975 software engineering findings all converge: the human brain
sustains deep coordination with about five people.

**AI changed the stakes, not the number:** AI-native companies (Lovable,
Midjourney, ElevenLabs) run 5–10x the SaaS benchmark of ~$500K revenue per
employee. When each person produces $2–3M/year, the coordination cost of a 6th
person is measured in millions of lost productivity — not a minor tax but a
catastrophe.

**Correctness over volume:** Harvard Business School study (2025, 776
professionals at P&G) found AI teams were 3x more likely to produce top-10%
quality ideas. AI also broke functional silos, extending each person into
adjacent domains. Volume is free; correctness is scarce.

**Two archetypes:**
- **Scouts** — solo operators exploring (Peter Steinberger built OpenClaw in ~60
  days with 4–10 agents). Great for exploration, insufficient for production
  correctness.
- **Strike teams** — five people with AI, optimized for correctness. Minimum
  surface area for a complete decision. Below five: blind spots. Above five:
  silos.

**Ambition expansion, not cost cutting:** 500 people at 5–10x capacity = force
of 2,500–5,000. 400 engineers → 80 strike teams could build 10 products instead
of maintaining one. Lovable (45 people, unicorn) and Midjourney used small teams
to think big, not to shrink.

**Composing at scale:** Dunbar ratios — 3–4 strike teams per domain, 3–4 domains
per strategic objective. Management thins; taste layer (Shopify's "constitution")
becomes most important.

**The AI slop tax:** Mediocre contributors on a five-person team don't just
underperform — their amplified bad judgment creates verification burdens on
everyone else.

**Testing readiness:** Give someone a real scout mission (one week, full AI
tools, no committee). Test for: defining problems without specs, architectural
judgment, holding full systems in their head, defaulting to action over
permission. Best strike team candidates are often the "frustrating" builders who
skip meetings and ship unrequested brilliant work.

**Shopify mandate:** Toby Lutke requires AI prototyping before any real build,
AI fluency in performance reviews, and proof AI can't do a task before approving
headcount — building a systematic evaluation pipeline across the org.
