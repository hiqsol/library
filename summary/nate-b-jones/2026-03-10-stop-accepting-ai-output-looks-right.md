# Summary: Stop Accepting AI Output That "Looks Right"

- **Source:** [YouTube — Nate B Jones](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-FhtPUkXKO4)
- **Author:** Nate B Jones
- **Published:** 2026-03-10

---

Nate argues that rejection — saying "no" to AI output — is the most
undervalued AI skill and the largest structural gap in the AI tool ecosystem.
Your rejections are more valuable than your prompts.

**Generation is commoditized.** OpenAI's GDPval shows frontier models beat
or tie professionals with 14 years of experience 70% of the time, 100x
faster, at less than 1% of the cost. The bottleneck is no longer production.
The question is: what happens to the 70% that looks right but isn't, and
the 30% where AI completely whiffs? Someone has to look and know.

**Three dimensions of rejection as a skill:**
1. **Recognition** — detecting something is wrong. Depends on domain
   experience; hard to shortcut. The person who's reviewed 2,000 deals and
   can feel when something is off is becoming the most important person in
   the building, not despite AI, but because of it.
2. **Articulation** — explaining *why* it's wrong in a way that produces a
   usable constraint. "This isn't right" is a rejection; "this isn't right
   because you're treating all requirements identically" is a constraint.
   Learnable but almost nobody teaches it.
3. **Encoding** — making constraints persist. Currently almost every
   rejection evaporates in email threads, chat windows, Slack messages.
   The same fight with AI repeats tomorrow.

**The compounding flywheel.** Properly encoded rejections scale the residue
of expert judgment, not experts themselves. A consulting firm encoding
partner rejections across thousands of engagements builds an institutional
quality bar no competitor can replicate via the same AI APIs.

**Precedents:** Epic Systems won healthcare by encoding clinical workflows
from thousands of hospitals (300M+ patient records, structural switching
costs). Bloomberg did the same in financial data. The moat is not software
— it's encoded judgment about what the software must get right.

**Karpathy's corollary:** "What you can verify, you can automate." The
frontier of AI value is identical to the frontier of your organization's
taste. Where verification capacity extends, AI creates value. Where it
doesn't, AI generates compounding silent risk — more output, less
understanding, a forgotten quality bar.

**Practical fix:** Jones built a constraint library via MCP server — logging
rejections inside the chat tool as a side effect of work already being done.
No context-switching. Accessible to juniors who can then learn senior-level
taste faster, addressing the growing junior-senior knowledge gap.

**The competitive moat** is not which AI vendor you choose. It's the depth
and durability of your organization's encoded taste.
