# Why Every AI Skill You Learned 6 Months Ago Is Already Wrong — Summary

**Date:** 2026-03-01 19:00

The AI capability boundary is an expanding bubble. Tasks migrate inside as
agents improve, but the surface area (the frontier) grows — more seams, more
judgment calls, more verification challenges. Unlike every prior workforce
skill, this one has no fixed destination. It expires quarterly.

**Frontier Operations** is the name for this practice. It has five components:

1. **Boundary Sensing** — Maintaining current intuition about where the
   human-agent boundary sits. Opus 4.5 couldn't retrieve from deep in long
   docs; Opus 4.6 scores 93% at 256K tokens. Calibrated in November but
   not updated? Both over-trusting and under-using are expensive errors.

2. **Seam Design** — Structuring work so human-agent transitions are clean,
   verifiable, recoverable. Which phases are fully agent-executable? What
   artifacts pass between phases? The right seam location changes quarterly.

3. **Failure Model Maintenance** — Current frontier models fail subtly:
   correct-sounding analysis on misunderstood premises, 98% accurate
   summaries with 2% confidently fabricated. The skill is a differentiated
   failure model per task type, not generic skepticism.

4. **Capability Forecasting** — Sensible 6-12 month bets about where the
   boundary moves next. Probabilistic positioning, not linear prediction.
   Invest in skills that will matter at the next frontier, not the current.

5. **Leverage Calibration** — Deciding where to spend human attention.
   McKinsey framework: 2-5 humans supervising 50-100 agents. Triage your
   own attention in real time. Recalibrate thresholds monthly.

**Structural properties:** The skill gap compounds — 6 months earlier means
6 months of calibration the peer lacks, and the distance widens with each
model release. This explains AI-native company leverage (Cursor, Lovable,
Anthropic shipping at outsized rates with small teams).

**Team structures emerging:**
- Team of one: high-leverage operator running multiple agent workflows
- Team of five: one frontier operator + developing practitioners + domain
  specialists, shipping at 20-person team pace

**For leaders:** Build practice environments (not courseware). Measure
calibration (not knowledge). Maximize feedback density (not training hours).
Create explicit frontier operations roles. Hire for differentiated failure
models and forecasting track records, not "good at prompting."

**The determining factor** for which businesses and economies win this
decade: not who builds models (portable), not who has compute (rentable),
but who can field workers excellent at operating at the AI-human frontier.
Models are commoditizing. Compute is being built out. Human frontier
operations capacity remains scarce.
