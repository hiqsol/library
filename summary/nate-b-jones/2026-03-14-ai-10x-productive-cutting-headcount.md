# Summary: AI 10x Productivity — Companies Cutting Headcount Are Wrong

[article](../../articles/nate-b-jones/2026-03-14-ai-10x-productive-cutting-headcount.md)

Nate argues that companies cutting headcount in response to AI
productivity gains are making the wrong bet. The right question isn't
"how many fewer people do we need?" but "what can we do now that was
previously impossible?"

**The signal:** Whoop is hiring 600+ people, nearly doubling its
800-person workforce. CEO Will Ahmed: "We are doing both" — AI and
hiring. The smartest operators are asking how people can work differently,
not how to eliminate them.

**Jevons' paradox:** When efficiency increases, consumption goes up.
Steel → skyscrapers/railroads/cars. Computing → internet/mobile/cloud.
Distribution → new media categories. There are more software engineers
now than 1-2 years ago. If the paradox applies (and every structural
indicator says it does), demand for insight, judgment, creativity, and
domain expertise is about to explode.

**Six structural unlocks (all available today, none require AGI):**

1. **Speed.** Compress product iteration from months to days. Cursor's
   Feb 2026 update: 20 parallel agents on cloud VMs, ~1/3 of code/PRs
   written by agents. 200 learning cycles/year instead of 2-4.

2. **Domain experts as builders.** 35M developers vs. hundreds of
   millions of domain experts locked out by the translation layer.
   Lovable, Bolt, Replit put production development in non-coder hands.
   Total surface area of problems addressed increases by 1-2 orders of
   magnitude.

3. **Quality as default.** Testing, security, docs, accessibility,
   polish — all agent-verifiable. Baseline quality rises dramatically.
   Differentiation shifts to product/customer experience.

4. **Every company = platform.** Agents will access your system whether
   you plan for it or not. Build integrations cheaply and proactively.
   Platform strategy literacy must move from VP-level to IC-level.

5. **Market for ambition.** A $10M market that needed a $3M/year team
   is now viable. An R&D experiment with 20% success odds becomes
   rational when you can run 5 of them. CFOs need new mental models.

6. **Speed of insight.** "Default to get it into code." When you get a
   reliable customer insight, act on it — don't get stuck in process.

**The unsolved frontier:** personalized education, clinical decision
support for individual patients, financial planning for 2 billion
adults with a bank account and no adviser. These are unsolved economic
problems, not unsolved technical problems. The cost of building was
just too high — until now.

**Core thesis:** The pie was artificially constrained by execution cost.
We weren't limited by ideas or ambition — we were limited by the cost
of turning ideas into products. The hardest work ahead is figuring out
upskilling when the job isn't "do the same thing faster" but "do
something you've never been asked to do before."
