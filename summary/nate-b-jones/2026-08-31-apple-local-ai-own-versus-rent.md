# Apple's New Mac Line is Built Around Local AI. The Bet Is You'd Rather Own Than Rent.

 Jones interprets Apple's Mac refresh as a bet on owning useful local intelligence. It does not
imply that a desktop can replace every frontier cloud model.

## Reported product ladder

- M6 Mac mini: 16–32 GB unified memory.
- M5 Pro mini: up to 64 GB and 307 GB/s bandwidth.
- M5 Max Studio: up to 128 GB, starting at $2,500.
- M5 Ultra Studio: up to 512 GB and 1.2 TB/s, starting at $5,500 before upgrades.
- Shipping is described as September 22, with 512 GB arriving in late October.
- Jones would choose 128 GB for his intended local-model and multi-agent workload.

## Competing economics

- Useful models keep getting cheaper and smaller while frontier agents demand more compute.
- Local hardware offers control and predictable ownership costs, but installation remains
  difficult.
- Cloud agents can retain sessions and work after the user's laptop closes.
- Providers can add compute and upgrade models without replacing the customer's device.
- Jones cites over $10 billion quarterly Mac revenue and roughly 40% product gross margin.
- His 5–10% local-adoption estimate is speculation, not a measured market share.

## The unresolved middle

- The missing product is simple installation and routing between local and frontier models.
- Apple's risk is a cloud agent becoming the permanent home of users' files and memory.
- Jones expects both local ownership and paid frontier capability to coexist.
- His 80–90% local-work split and 80% open-weight OpenRouter share by December are forecasts.
- The product specifications and prices reflect Jones's August 31 account.

The strategic choice concerns where ongoing work, memory, and control live, as much as raw
compute. Making local and hosted intelligence cooperate is the opportunity he considers
underdeveloped.
