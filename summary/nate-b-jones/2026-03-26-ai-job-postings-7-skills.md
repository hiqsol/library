# Summary

[article](../../articles/nate-b-jones/2026-03-26-ai-job-postings-7-skills.md)

The AI job market is K-shaped: traditional knowledge-work roles are
flat or falling while AI-specific roles are growing explosively. The
ratio of AI jobs to qualified talent is 3.2-to-1, with ~1.6M open
positions against ~500K qualified applicants. Average time to fill:
142 days.

Seven skills recur across hundreds of AI job postings:

1. **Specification precision** — writing machine-literal intent, not
   vague requests. The 2026 prompting bar is highly detailed: define
   task scope, escalation rules, and measurement criteria up front.

2. **Evaluation and quality judgment** — the most cited skill. Build
   evals, resist AI fluency-as-competence bias, detect edge cases,
   insist on functional (not just semantic) correctness.

3. **Task decomposition and delegation** — the managerial skill
   behind multi-agent systems. Size work for the agentic harness;
   define subtasks and logical relationships clearly enough for a
   planner agent to coordinate.

4. **Failure pattern recognition** — diagnose six failure types:
   context degradation, specification drift, sycophantic
   confirmation, tool selection errors, cascading failure, and silent
   failure (the most dangerous — plausible output that is wrong).

5. **Trust and security design** — decide where agents vs. humans
   act; evaluate blast radius, reversibility, frequency, and
   verifiability of every agentic action.

6. **Context architecture** — build the information layer agents
   search at scale. Persistent vs. per-run context, clean vs. dirty
   data, easy traversal. A massive unlock: get it right and you can
   build dozens of agentic systems, not just one.

7. **Cost and token economics** — calculate per-task ROI, pick the
   right model mix, compute blended cost, and prove value before
   deploying agents. High-school math applied in a fast-moving
   pricing landscape.

These skills span engineering, ops, product, and architecture roles.
They are tied to how AI fundamentally works and will remain relevant
even as models improve.
