# Model Scores Are Multidimensional, but Different Kinds of Intelligence Remain Unproven

[article](../../articles/nate-b-jones/2026-11-23-gpt-astra-gemini-neon-review-gap.md)

GPT-6.2 Astra received negative reactions from some writers and developers, while Gemini 4 Neon
was praised for speed and tone. Both reactions can describe real but different workflow slices.

Two dates in the video are wrong. Astra launched November 17, 2026, not November 10. Neon launched
November 18, not November 11. Claude Fable 5.2 launched November 9.

Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0.1 scored Fable 5.2 at 65, Astra at 62, and Neon at 61
under different configurations. These are composite index points, not percentages.

“Zero improvement” in coding is too strong. Codex with Astra Max scored 53.47 on Coding Agent Index
v2.1, versus 53.10 for GPT-6 Astra Max: essentially flat there, but not literally zero.

Agent scores measure systems, not bare models. Prompts, tools, context handling, effort settings,
token budgets, fallbacks, and verification can materially change results.

Jones found Astra detail-obsessed and weak for broad writing or ambiguous builds, but useful for
explicit, verifiable work. His product-analysis example suggests strength in tracing contradictions
and dependencies. This is firsthand experience, not a controlled evaluation.

Every's accessible test was reported by Katie Parrott and led by Mike Taylor. It supports the
narrow claim that OpenAI's models handled bounded tasks but left evaluators wanting more judgment
and follow-through. It does not verify that Astra repeatedly stopped early.

Google positions Neon between Carbon's long-horizon depth and Hydrogen's everyday speed. Its model
card says Neon is adapted from Carbon. Parameter count and pretraining scale are not public, so
calling it a smaller or “lighter pre-train” is unsupported.

The video's “sea urchin” replaces one intelligence score with many capability spikes. A workflow
sees only a shadow of that shape, so local regression and gains elsewhere can coexist.

The metaphor is a useful selection lens, not established psychometrics. No cited ablation shows
that Astra's reported science gains caused its alleged writing weakness.

Different business models may influence portfolios, but public evidence does not show that revenue
mix causes model style. Google, Anthropic, and OpenAI all make overlapping claims across coding,
science, agents, and enterprise work.

The practical recommendation is to audit each task: scope, ambiguity, quality criteria, interaction
mode, verification, economics, and risk. Test multiple model-plus-scaffold systems on repeated,
representative work rather than selecting from one leaderboard.

A two-model handoff can use Astra to extract contradictions and edge conditions, then Neon to frame
a readable memo. Preserve source identifiers and verify the final memo so unresolved evidence is not
smoothed away.

The evidence supports different model portfolios, operating points, and measured profiles. It does
not yet prove that the labs are building fundamentally different kinds of intelligence.
