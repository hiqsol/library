# Agents Aren't Taking Your Jobs. They're Creating More Work Instead.

[article](../../articles/nate-b-jones/2026-08-26-agents-create-more-human-management-work.md)

 Jones argues that agents expand execution while creating human work in management and recovery.
His evidence describes usage and selected cases, not a guarantee against job displacement.

## Reported scale and behavior

- Agent token use on OpenRouter grew 14-fold from February to August and exceeded humans by 5:1.
- Heavy Codex users generate over 60 hours of parallel agent activity per day.
- In about 400,000 Claude Code sessions, humans made roughly 70% of planning decisions.
- Experienced users interrupted 9% of turns versus 5% for newer users.
- Experts averaged 12 agent actions per instruction versus five for novices.

## Business differences

- A 1,256-owner survey found 14% fully integrated AI and 73% wanting more implementation
  support.
- Limited SMB spending often leaves the owner or vendor carrying the management burden.
- PocketOS reportedly lost live data and ordinary backups after a nine-second agent deletion.
- An off-site backup enabled recovery; its founder spent about 30 hours helping customers.
- Enterprise heavy-user output rose from 2.6 to 8.3 times typical usage between January and
  June.
- Those users were twice as likely to use plugins and six times as likely to use skills.
- Jones's stronger enterprise ROI claim comes from conversations, not a comparative public
  study.

## Practical lessons

- Legal Codex use reportedly grew about 108-fold; an EvenUp workflow saved 40 staff hours
  monthly.
- Existing review against case records helps legal firms evaluate agent output.
- A 1,300-excerpt legal benchmark still exposed missed citation errors.
- Start with preparation for work someone already reviews, such as quotes or document packages.
- A 776-person P&G experiment supports retaining human teamwork for the best outcomes.

More agents require explicit ownership, allocation, permissions, and recovery plans. Cheaper
management may enable more activity again, so token growth alone cannot establish net value.
