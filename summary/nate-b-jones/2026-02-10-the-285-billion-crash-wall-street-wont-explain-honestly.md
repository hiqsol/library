# The $285 Billion Crash Wall Street Won't Explain Honestly — Summary

**Published:** 2026-02-10 15:00

On January 30th, Anthropic released open-source plugins for Claude Co-work.
A ~200-line markdown prompt for legal contract review triggered a $285B
market cap wipeout in 48 hours: Thomson Reuters -16%, RELX -14%, LegalZoom
-20%, PE firms (Ares, KKR, TPG) ~-10%.

The real story is not the plugin — it is the per-seat SaaS pricing model
breaking. Software industry forward P/E compressed from ~8x to ~2x in the
four months prior, the worst since the 2002 dot-com bust.

Jensen Huang's counterargument: AI runs on software, so software demand
increases. He is right about the product, wrong about the pricing model.
The world needs software but no longer needs to pay per-seat for it.

Parallel to print media: newspapers' content survived but the access model
(buy the whole paper for one section) died. Software's proprietary data
survives; the per-seat access model does not.

Bank of America's Vivek Arya called the sell-off "internally inconsistent."
Markets simultaneously priced in AI infrastructure collapse (thesis 1) and
AI so powerful it kills legacy SaaS (thesis 2). Both cannot be true.

The KPMG story matters more than the stock crash. KPMG pressured Grant
Thornton to cut audit fees, citing AI cost savings. Fees dropped from
$416K (2024) to $357K (2025) — 14% reduction. This is an operating event,
not a market event. The playbook: use AI's existence as negotiating
leverage across all professional services.

What survived: proprietary data, vendor accountability ("single ringable
neck"), SLAs. What died: per-seat pricing that scales with headcount. One
AI agent replacing 10 paralegals means 9 lost seats of revenue.

Build vs. buy is flipping as software creation cost approaches zero.
Cursor: 1,000 commits/hour with no humans. StrongDM: "code must not be
written or reviewed by humans." The caveat: the articulation problem —
translating vague human needs into precise specs — remains unsolved for
most enterprise use cases.

For individuals: using AI to proofread emails or summarize docs is
"bolting AI on top." The real move is rethinking workflows from the ground
up, just as SaaS companies must rethink architecture, not just add
chatbots.

The transition everyone expected to take 5 years compressed into a 48-hour
repricing event that has not stopped.
