# Why $650B in AI Spending Isn't Enough — Summary

Nate B Jones argues that the $650–700B in AI infrastructure spending
by the five largest tech companies in 2026 is not a bubble — it's
probably insufficient for the agentic era now arriving.

## Key Numbers

- Google 2026 CapEx: $175–185B (double 2025's $91B, 50% above
  analyst expectations of ~$120B). Stock dropped 7%.
- Alphabet Q4 revenue: exceeded $400B for the first time.
- Combined big-five AI CapEx 2026: ~$700B. Goldman projects $1T+
  by 2025–2027.
- Microsoft capital intensity: 45% of revenue.
- OpenAI ARR: $20B in 2025 — ~3% of infra investment on its behalf.
- Anthropic: from <1,000 to 300,000+ business customers in 2 years;
  44% enterprise penetration by January 2026.
- SaaS Apocalypse: $285B erased from enterprise software market cap
  after a markdown file (Claude legal plugin) wiped 16% off Thomson
  Reuters.

## Core Argument

The narrative flipped from "AI is a bubble" to "we're underbuilt"
in weeks, not months. The trigger: agents deployed into production
(contract triage, coding, compliance) consuming 1,000x the inference
tokens of a human user. You can't believe agents are powerful enough
to crash SaaS and simultaneously think the infra is excessive.

## Why This Isn't a Dumb Pipe

Railroads and fiber were dumb pipes — overbuilt, crashed, then
justified by later applications. AI infrastructure is vertically
integrated: the model is the product, every inference call sells
cognitive capability. Model makers capture value from applications
built on top, not just hosting fees.

## Training → Inference Phase Shift

First wave (2023–mid 2025): training — bursty, front-loaded. Current
wave: inference — continuous, never stops. Agents generate ~1,000x
human inference load. Google's 60/40 split (servers vs. data centers)
signals they're building for inference-dominant demand.

## Infrastructure Window (~18 months)

Historical pattern: build during the window → become a platform;
wait → become a tenant (see: AWS 2003–2006). Current cycle is
compressed because agent-driven demand arrives fast. Google would
rather overspend than underbuild.

## Four Skills That Survive

1. **Taste** — distinguishing competent from extraordinary, technically
   correct from strategically right. A filter, not a checklist.
2. **Domain judgment** — hard-to-articulate, experience-encoded
   understanding of how a specific domain works. Not in training data.
3. **Phenomenal ramp** — absorbing change at speed, operating at a
   frontier that moves weekly. A meta-skill that multiplies all others.
4. **Relentless honesty** — inventorying your own work to identify
   what's still valuable vs. what agents handle better. Reallocate
   before the market forces you to.
