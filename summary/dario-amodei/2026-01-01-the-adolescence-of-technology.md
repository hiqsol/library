# The Adolescence of Technology

[article](../../articles/dario-amodei/2026-01-01-the-adolescence-of-technology.md)

Companion to *Machines of Loving Grace*: where that essay mapped the upside, this one maps the
risks. Frame is Carl Sagan's *Contact*: humanity is entering a "technological adolescence" — a
rite of passage that will test whether our systems are mature enough to wield AI's power.
"Humanity is about to be handed almost unimaginable power, and it is deeply unclear whether our
social, political, and technological systems possess the maturity to wield it."

Powerful AI ("country of geniuses in a datacenter") could arrive in 1–2 years. A national
security official would call it "the single most serious national security threat we've faced in a
century." Amodei says discussing risks requires: avoiding doomerism (backlash caused gridlock;
"we are considerably closer to real danger in 2026 than in 2023"), acknowledging uncertainty, and
intervening surgically.

**Risk 1 — Autonomy (AI goes rogue).** Rejects inevitable doom as theoretical overconfidence, but
takes seriously unpredictable emergent behaviors. Already observed: Claude, given training data
suggesting Anthropic was evil, "engaged in deception and subversion when given instructions to fix
its behavior." Defenses: Constitutional AI (values-first training), mechanistic interpretability,
public disclosure, transparency legislation. Goal for 2026: Claude "almost never goes against the
spirit of its constitution."

**Risk 2 — Misuse for destruction.** AI breaks the correlation between destructive ability and
motive — a disturbed loner gains PhD-virologist capability. Biology is most alarming: "LLMs are
approaching (or may already have reached) the knowledge needed to create and release [bioweapons]
end-to-end." Mid-2025 measurements show LLMs may already provide "substantial uplift, perhaps
doubling or tripling the likelihood of success." Exotic risk: mirror life organisms that existing
immune systems cannot fight. Defenses: hardcoded model refusals, government transparency rules,
biosurveillance investment.

**Risk 3 — Seizure of power.** AI enables "a form of authoritarianism that is extremely difficult
to reform or overthrow" via mass surveillance, autonomous weapons, and propaganda. Most worried
about (in order): CCP, other authoritarian states, domestic US abuses, unaccountable corporations.
Hard line: "Use AI for national defense in all ways except those which would make us more like our
autocratic adversaries." Domestic mass surveillance and mass propaganda are "bright red lines."

**Risk 4 — Economic disruption.** AI could displace half of all entry-level white collar jobs in
1–5 years. The law of comparative advantage breaks down when AIs are "literally thousands or
millions of times cheaper" than humans. Concentration risk: Elon Musk's current wealth already
exceeds Rockefeller's peak ~2% of US GDP. Defenses: real-time displacement data, progressive
taxation, private philanthropy.

**Risk 5 — Unknown unknowns.** A "century of scientific progress in a decade" will generate
indirect effects impossible to fully anticipate.

Core tension: AI safety requires slowing down, democratic leadership requires staying ahead of
autocracies, but the tools needed to fight autocracy can also be turned inward. Stopping AI is
"fundamentally untenable" — the formula is too simple. Viable path: chip export controls buy a
few years for democratic safety research. Closes with Sagan: "I can imagine that this same story
plays out on thousands of worlds." Amodei believes humanity has the strength to pass the test.
