# AI broke the one thing we can't fix — Summary

[article](../../articles/mo-bitar/2026-03-24-ai-broke-the-one-thing-we-cant-fix.md)

Mo Bitar argues that AI-powered spam and content generation have
permanently broken the internet's trust model, with no fix in sight.

## The catalyst: OpenClaw

- Nikita Bier warned that iMessage, phone calls, and Gmail will be
  unusable within 90 days due to AI-driven spam and automation.
- He purged 1.7 million bot accounts off X; they respawned the next day.
- OpenClaw — the highest-starred GitHub project ever, surpassing React
  — lets anyone with a laptop build AI agents that send emails, texts,
  browse the web, make phone calls, and impersonate people.
- The barrier to becoming a spammer dropped from technical skill to
  just owning a laptop.

## Spam as information warfare

- The Chinese government floods X search results with porn during
  political unrest to drown out protest information — not censorship
  by blocking, but by overwhelming with more engaging content.
- AI-generated spam on X now links AI-made profiles to AI-generated
  crypto scams — a fully automated deception pipeline.

## Why AI content wins the attention war

- RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback) optimizes AI
  output to be more compelling than anything humans can write.
- Every AI-generated piece of content is engineered at the neural
  network level to maximize engagement.

## The collapsing economic model

- Google traffic to publishers dropped 38% last year in the US.
- The internet's deal — humans write genuine content, get ad revenue
  — is dead because bots generate more clicks than humans.
- No one in the creation-to-monetization chain has incentive to care
  whether content is real.
- Nearly 10 of YouTube's 100 fastest-growing channels last year were
  fully AI-generated.
- "Slop" was Merriam-Webster's word of the year for 2025.

## The only thing left: trust in known people

- There is no technical solution. This is what the internet is now.
- AI-generated emails will be so personalized that the only way to
  verify authenticity is already knowing the sender.
- The spam is now smarter than the average person — built from the
  ground up to be better at fooling you than you are at detecting it.
- The only remaining signal of authenticity: specific humans who have
  earned trust over time. Not platforms, not algorithms — people.
