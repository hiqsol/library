# China's Rare-Earth Magnet Strategy Beyond Oil

## Thesis and limits
Maxinomics' strongest thesis is narrower than "better than oil."
China turned rare-earth processing and magnet manufacturing into strategic leverage.
The source does not show that rare earths replace oil as fuel, trade, or state revenue.
It barely treats solar, and does not analyze wind, batteries, emissions, or fossil demand.
EVs appear through motors; solar, aluminum, steel, and ships are asserted parallels.
English automatic captions may mishear names, terms, dates, and numbers.

## Why magnets matter
A live copper coil pushes a permanent magnet, making compact motors and actuators.
The video counts six magnets in AirPods, 40 in an iPhone, and at least 12 in a laptop.
It says an EV motor has 12 lb of magnets and each US missile at least 12 magnets.
Neodymium-iron-boron, unveiled by GM and future Hitachi Metals and dated 1983,
was smaller, stronger, and three times cheaper; GM halved starter-motor size.
The source says 96 cents per rare-earth dollar reaches magnets and China controls 94%.

## How China built scale
Deposits are widespread; costly chemical separation, waste handling, and know-how are not.
A 200 lb Mountain Pass scoop could make nine 1.5-inch magnets or about 250 AirPods sets.
An eight-inch, 14-mile pipe ruptured over 60 times in 12 years, spilling nearly
one million gallons of thorium-bearing toxic water and helping end US processing.
Maxinomics says China's one-mile-by-one-mile waste pool faced looser rules and no suits.
GM sold Magnequench in 1995 after two years of bids; captions garble the buyer's name.
Two Chinese firms allegedly held 62%; their owners were Deng Xiaoping's sons-in-law.
Deng, China's ruler for 12 years, said Saudi Arabia had oil; China had rare earths.

## Industrial policy and leverage
Maxinomics' four steps are cheap state loans and land, export limits, waiting, then reopening.
Late-2010 controls roughly quadrupled foreign prices while Chinese inputs stayed cheap.
Foreign firms shifted orders, drawings, recipes, and Detroit or Tokyo know-how to China.
An iPhone's roughly $9 of materials becomes a product selling for about 120 times more.
Trade cases take three to five years; reopening can underprice new US, Canadian,
and Australian mines while two state champions consolidate dozens or hundreds of firms.
China Northern Rare Earth Group and China Rare Earth Group are the named champions.
On April 4, 2025, Maxinomics says China crossed from market power into coercion.
He says US chip curbs began around 2016, tightened in 2019 and 2020, then came 2025 tariffs.

## Security, response, and risk
Samarium allegedly cuts magnet heat loss by 95%; China controls 99% of its processing.
It takes one-third more steps, costs 12 times more than neodymium, and is scarcer.
One hundred Tomahawks flew in the Gulf War's first 24 hours; each needs at least 12 magnets.
China's 2025 cutoff disrupted supply chains and shut US factories, the source says.
The Pentagon now guarantees $110/kg and volume for 10 years under "take or pay."
Above $110, government gets 30% of the difference on other sales.
Mountain Pass is mining, refining, and building a Texas plant; success remains uncertain.
Pollution, coercion, subsidy exposure, substitution, and rival capacity are core risks.
