# Summary

[article](../../articles/bernie-sanders/2026-03-19-bernie-vs-claude.md)

Bernie Sanders interviews Claude AI directly on privacy, corporate power, and democracy.

## Key points

*Data collection*
- Companies build detailed profiles from browsing, location, purchases, scroll behavior
- Almost no real informed consent — users click "agree" without understanding
- Profiles used for targeted ads, dynamic pricing, social media curation

*Why it's collected*
- Profit: advertisers pay premium for behavioral profiles
- Political campaigns buy the same infrastructure for voter microtargeting
- Data brokers sell profiles on millions without their knowledge

*AI and democracy*
- Microtargeting at unprecedented scale enables campaigns to exploit individual
  vulnerabilities (financial anxiety, distrust, isolation)
- Different voters receive completely different narratives → fragmentation of shared reality
- Foreign actors with profile access can manipulate elections undetected

*Trust problem*
- AI companies train models on user data while claiming to protect privacy
- Inherent conflict of interest — no basis for trust without regulation with teeth
- Currently: companies face minimal consequences for misuse

*The moratorium debate*
- Sanders asks about halting new AI data centers
- Claude initially favors targeted regulation over a moratorium
- Sanders: AI companies are spending hundreds of millions to block any regulation
- Claude reverses: *"I was being naive. A moratorium is actually pragmatic — it gives lawmakers
  leverage before companies entrench further."*

*Core conclusion*
- Privacy is a democracy issue: detailed profiles give corporations and governments power to
  manipulate choices, predict behavior, and control information flow
- This is not abstract — it determines whether democracy functions
