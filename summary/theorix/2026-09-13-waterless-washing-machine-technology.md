# Waterless Laundry with Liquid Carbon Dioxide

## Claim and context
Theorix says ordinary washing uses about 100 liters per load plus detergent and drying.
It inconsistently compares that water with one teaspoon, then one tablespoon, of dirt.
China's household laundry is said to create about 10 billion tons of wastewater yearly.
These figures and broad pollution claims are narrated, not linked to studies in the video.

## How the proposed cycle works

Compressed, refrigerated CO2 becomes liquid and is held under high pressure.
Theorix says its low surface tension penetrates fibers and dissolves oils and grime.
Depressurization returns it to gas; dirt stays in a chamber and clothes emerge dry.
Captured gas is recompressed and reused, eliminating water, detergent, rinse, and dryer.
That describes an intended closed loop; no recovery, leakage, cycle, or residue data appear.
Liquid CO2 is distinct from the supercritical CO2 and perc dry cleaning also discussed.

## LG, history, and alternatives

LG reportedly won a South Korean regulatory-sandbox approval on December 30, 2021.
It committed to a two-year research-center safety trial, then possible mall laundries.
No completed trial result, authorization, installation, launch date, or price is shown.
US patent 12,359,363 was reportedly granted in July 2025 from late-2020 Korean filings.
A patent supports engineering activity, but not safety, performance, production, or sales.
A captioned “Maf” patent dates to 1977; NASA supercritical-CO2 work is dated 1979.
Liquid-CO2 dry cleaners, including Electrolux systems, were sold in the 1990s.
They allegedly lost on cost: one CO2 system equaled three conventional units in the 2000s.
Xeros beads claim 99.95% recovery, hundreds of uses, and one-tenth normal water use.
They were reportedly sent to drought-hit Cape Town; India's 80 Wash claims 80 seconds.
A March 2026 Chinese coating claims detergent-free cleaning for over 100 washes.
Samsung's separate microfiber filter is said to capture fibers down to 65 micrometers.

## Benefits claimed, evidence missing

No process water means no detergent wastewater and potentially less aquatic microfiber loss.
No hot water or dryer may save energy, but CO2 refrigeration and compression consume power.
No comparative energy-per-load result or life-cycle assessment is provided.
Gentler fabric care and CO2 sanitization are implied, not shown in controlled LG tests.
The video gives no stain-spectrum, hygiene, wear, shedding, rewash, or throughput results.
Reuse is not net-zero proof: CO2 origin, losses, electricity, and manufacture are uncounted.

## Barriers and conclusion
High pressure requires a heavy, sealed, expensive vessel plus reliable pumps and service.
Leak response, ventilation, interlocks, fatigue, certification, and infrastructure are omitted.
Commercial sites may spread costs, but homes face price, space, safety, and habit barriers.
Automatic captions contain errors, including “Maf,” “Soul,” and garbled laundry terms.
The source is a secondary narration, not a demonstration or independent validation.
It supports plausibility and continued LG interest, not a market-ready replacement.
