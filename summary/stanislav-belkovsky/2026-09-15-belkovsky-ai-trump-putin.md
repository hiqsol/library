# AI, Power, Trump, and the Future of Putin’s System

## Core argument
- Stanislav Belkovsky treats AI fear as political theology, not a machine uprising.
- He says secular societies replace God with dictators, aliens, weapons, or AI.
- In his view, AI remains a human tool through which some people control others.
- Nuclear weapons are his analogy: their danger depends on who holds and uses them.

## AI, resources, and ownership
- Belkovsky says AI owners amplify fear, then claim only they can keep AI safe.
- He compares this pitch to a protection racket that demands a 30% payment.
- He names Altman, Musk, and likely Dario and Daniela Amodei as key magnates.
- Data centers require water, energy, land, and other shared physical resources.
- He claims AI firms also privatized humanity’s accumulated “General Intellect.”
- He predicts revenue must rise by tens of times within five years to avert a bubble.
- He attributes AI’s slowdown to weak economics and scarcity, not moral restraint.
- He predicts Sanders- and Mamdani-style politics will revive ownership questions.

## Religion, immortality, and progress
- Belkovsky says human numinous experience distinguishes people from AI.
- He concedes that this argument collapses if God and the immortal soul do not exist.
- Tech elites promise life to 120, then mind-uploading into non-biological bodies.
- He asks who would control such immortality and whether it would still count as life.
- Chernobyl and Mars support his warning that technical progress can destroy habitats.

## Putin’s system
- Belkovsky rejects the claim that Boris Berezovsky single-handedly created Putin.
- He credits Yeltsin’s family, especially Valentin Yumashev and Tatyana Dyachenko.
- He alleges that their circle enabled the Chechen war behind Putin’s rise.
- That grave allegation is Belkovsky’s and is not verified by the interview.
- Berezovsky sold assets in 2000; three months before Putin’s ascent, success looked remote.
- Gusinsky, Berezovsky, and Khodorkovsky suffered after challenging system rules.
- Major oligarchs largely kept wealth, making Putinism a continuation, not a rupture.
- Belkovsky links this structure to AI’s privatization of a collective inheritance.

## Trump and the next order
- Belkovsky calls Trump a transitional figure ending the American-centered order.
- He says Trump can revive religion while vanity and pettiness expose his own model.
- Anecdotes about freebies, Witkoff, and Kremlev remain unverified or caption-uncertain.
- Claims about Trump, Iran, Netanyahu, Hamas, and October 7 require outside checking.
- His broader charge is that elites demand all resources but accept no responsibility.
- He argues that visible irresponsibility revives a modern, non-Soviet “communism.”
- The future turns on ownership and accountability, not on AI becoming autonomous.
