# New Order for Trump and Putin — Summary

[article](../../articles/vitaly-portnikov/2026-03-22-new-order-for-trump-and-putin.md)

Recorded at King Danylo University, Portnikov addresses students on professional
purpose, Ukrainian statehood, and the geopolitics of the ongoing war.

- Professional success comes from orienting around goals for society, not personal
  career advancement. Portnikov did not want to be a famous journalist — he wanted
  Ukraine independent, Jews free to emigrate, and communism ended. Journalism was
  the instrument.

- Ukraine has a long history as a state-forming people (Kyivan Rus, Grand Duchy of
  Lithuania, Hetmanate), but the loss of the Hetmanate bred disrespect for institutions
  that persisted until 2014 and partially beyond.

- The key failures of 35 years of independence: disrespect for institutions; treating
  regional diversity as weakness rather than strength; multi-vectorism between East and
  West. Ukraine is now in a binary: Europe or Moscow.

- Ukraine has produced the world's most advanced practitioners of drone warfare.
  Operations like "Spider Web" represent tomorrow's military reality. Western allies
  have money and F-16s — yesterday's tools. Ukraine's existential pressure makes it
  more inventive than Russia, which relies on mass and legacy Soviet defense industry.

- Russia's collapse is not a reliable bet. Putin can bomb his own minorities into
  submission, as in Chechnya. 25 years of intensified Russification means the data
  underpinning collapse predictions is outdated. And the same logic that fractures
  Russia can be used against Ukraine.

- Trump's worldview places three "serious men who run serious states" at the top —
  Trump, Xi, and Putin. Everyone else (Zelensky, Macron, Merz) is subordinate.
  Trump assumed he could call Putin as an equal and offer a rational deal. Putin
  wants everything, not a rational deal. Trump blames Ukraine and Europe.

- The conspiracy theories (Epstein as Soviet agent, Moscow hotel tapes, crypto flows)
  are irrelevant even if partially true. The structural explanation is sufficient.

- MAGA is a social trend, not just Trump. A portion of America wants to return to a
  demographic and economic past that cannot be bought back. They will ultimately fail,
  but cause damage in the attempt.

- Giving up Donbas would not produce peace. Putin's goal is the elimination of
  Ukrainian statehood, not a territorial settlement. Territorial demands are tools of
  destabilization. The "land for peace" framing is a trap.

- EU membership is primarily about ensuring European nations do not go to war with
  each other — not prosperity. The alternative for Ukraine is not neutrality but
  the Eurasian Union, which is designed to erase Ukraine. Ukraine will join a different
  EU than the one of 2014; that is still infinitely better than the alternative.

- On "good Russians": collective responsibility is rejected as leading to atrocity.
  The relevant distinction is between those who recognize Ukraine's right to statehood
  and those who do not — regardless of passport. Russian cultural figures who
  weaponize their platform for war propaganda (Gergiev, Netrebko) should be opposed;
  those who defend Ukraine (Kasparov, Arkhipova) should be recognized.

- Closing message to students: the preservation of Ukrainian statehood is the
  foundation of every individual future. Without the state, no Ukrainian is counted
  for anything — a lesson drawn from the history of stateless peoples who discovered
  in the worst possible circumstances what it means to have no government of one's own.
