# How Russia May Test NATO Below the Threshold of War

## Core thesis

Journalist Ivan Yakovina says Putin uses denial to preserve ambiguity and escape routes.
Denials may accompany preparation, he argues, but they do not prove that an operation is planned.
Moscow may prefer calibrated pressure that tests NATO without clearly starting a war.

## Political targets and hybrid probes

Distant or less committed EU states may be easier to intimidate than front-line members.
Pressure could empower Russia-friendly parties and obstruct collective European aid to Ukraine.
Drone incursions, brief border crossings, and sabotage could serve as tests of NATO red lines.
The interview cited incidents in Romania and Poland but did not independently verify them.
Attacks on power sites, NATO bases, or Baltic land were Yakovina's scenarios, not known plans.

## Readiness, Kaliningrad, and Washington

Ukraine's war shows how skilled drone and electronic-warfare units can immobilize larger forces.
Yakovina believes Europe underrates Russia's combat experience and its elite units.
His cited exercise results were not documented, and they do not prove NATO is unprepared.
He portrays Kaliningrad as an asset Putin might sacrifice for greater gains elsewhere.
That is speculation, not evidence of Russian intent or an inevitable NATO response.
He predicts Trump might leave a European clash to Europe, yet calls Trump unpredictable.
The discussion does not establish that the United States would refuse treaty-based assistance.

## Infrastructure and the city-versus-city idea

The interview describes pressure on communications, power, providers, and data centers.
Ukraine's distributed internet improves resilience, though attacks and repairs continue.
Power and data networks remain linked, so local damage could still disrupt civilian life.
The city-versus-city frame contrasts attacks on urban and industrial systems on both sides.
Yakovina sees Ukraine seeking resilience, secure supply, and damage to Russian war industry.
Claims about named refineries, schools, and data centers were mentioned but not verified there.

## Institutions, escalation, and AI

Yakovina values the UN as a public stage, meeting place, and technical standards forum.
He calls it a failure at preventing wars, but the interview does not test that judgment.
A probe could escalate through deaths, captured troops, cascading outages, or clear attribution.
Repeated violations or false assumptions about US restraint could widen a conflict.
Kaliningrad and nuclear signaling would make direct exchanges especially hard to contain.
Yakovina says battlefield AI creates an arms race because unilateral restraint may mean defeat.
His robot warning is a forecast; nearer risks include errors, opacity, and faster escalation.

## Limits of the argument

The thesis proves no attack plan, certain US inaction, Russian victory, or NATO failure.
