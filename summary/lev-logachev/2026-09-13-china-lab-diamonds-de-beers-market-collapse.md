# How China and Lab-Grown Diamonds Broke the Scarcity Model

*This summary follows Lev Logachev's unverified claims and causal interpretations.*
*Russian automatic captions may contain errors; corrupt or conflicting figures are flagged.*

## Manufactured scarcity and demand
Logachev says De Beers once controlled almost 90% of global rough-diamond supply.
It withheld millions of carats and offered fixed-price boxes to selected sightholders.
Surplus purchases and selective releases allegedly kept supply and rivals in check.
Diamond engagement rings were not an ancient norm; advertising supplied the demand.
A 1938 campaign—agency captioned “NWI,” likely N. W. Ayer—reshaped proposals.
“A Diamond Is Forever” followed in 1947, linking stones to love and discouraging resale.
Later salary rules and campaigns exported the norm to Japan, China, India, and beyond.

## An industrial substitute
Logachev dates machine-made diamond to Tracy Hall at GE on December 16, 1954.
HPHT compresses graphite, catalyst, and a seed at tens of thousands of atmospheres.
Its captioned temperature, “1лу°,” is corrupt and cannot support an exact figure.
CVD uses hydrogen, methane, microwaves, and plasma to grow a seed over several weeks.
The result is diamond, not glass or cubic zirconia; instruments can identify its origin.
China first synthesized it in 1963, under Project 121, according to Logachev.
Zhecheng's cluster grew after its first small press factory opened in 1983.
China allegedly made 22 million gem-quality carats in 2023; nearly half came from Zhecheng.
Henan grows rough stones; Surat's hundreds of thousands of workers cut and polish them.

## The price and demand break
Lab wholesale prices allegedly fell from $1,800 in 2018 to $80 per carat in 2025: 96%.
A conflicting series says $4,000 in 2015 versus $300–$500 today, over 90% lower.
A third claim puts uncut stones near $300 per carat; qualities and datasets are unclear.
A $3,000 old lab stone competes with cheaper new output, so some jewelers refuse resale.
Natural 1-carat prices are $6,000 in 2021, later $4,200, and a corrupt “2…”.
Those claims conflict or omit quality assumptions and cannot form one price index.
Logachev says lab share rose from 1% globally in 2015 to 20% in 2024.
He also claims roughly half of U.S. diamond sales and new engagement rings are lab-grown.
De Beers launched Lightbox near $800 per carat in 2018, then closed it in 2025.
Its effort to reserve “serious” luxury for natural stones instead exposed substitution.
Chinese demand also weakened amid fewer marriages, a property crisis, and thriftier youth.

## Beyond jewelry and exposed economies
Diamond allegedly moves heat 5× better than copper and 13× better than silicon.
Logachev sees heat spreaders and power electronics as uses for China's existing capacity.
Botswana allegedly gets one-third of state revenue and three-quarters of FX from diamonds.
He says S&P cut its rating in March 2026; captions incorrectly render the name as “SNP.”
ALROSA allegedly mined 30 million carats in 2025, with 25 million planned for 2026.
Over ten years it paid Yakutia and districts 630 billion rubles, or 35% of own revenue.
Yakutia and eight mining uluses own 33% of ALROSA, exposing public budgets to the slump.
Logachev links Russia's September 1, 2026 labeling rules to defending natural scarcity.
Every new press expands supply, eroding the rarity supporting Botswana, ALROSA, and Yakutia.
