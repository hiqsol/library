# Stonehenge AI Claims: A Fabricated Study Built from Real Archaeology

[article](../../articles/past-unsealed/2026-08-16-stonehenge-ai-claims.md)

The video claims a peer-verified 2026 AI system analyzed every Stonehenge record and discovered a
lunar computer, trance-inducing acoustics, a pentatonic instrument, a geometric master plan, and
knowledge inherited from an older civilization. No identifiable study supports that narrative.

The video names no paper, authors, journal, DOI, institution, model, dataset, code, or method.
Crossref, OpenAlex, arXiv, and official research pages contain no matching study. The quoted
validators Jennifer Walsh, Marcus Chen, and Sarah Bradford could not be linked to this research.

Stonehenge does have 56 Aubrey Holes, but a 56-hole lunar calculator was proposed in the 1960s.
It remains disputed. No publication reports millimetre-scale sinusoidal spacing, a near-zero chance
probability, Kepler-level mechanics, or nine astronomical alignments within 0.3 degrees.

The solstitial axis is established. Possible lunar-standstill orientation is a legitimate but
unresolved hypothesis. Observing horizon cycles is not equivalent to encoding orbital equations,
and post-hoc lines between many features create a severe multiple-comparisons problem.

A real 2020 scale-model study found 0.64 seconds of reverberation and about 4.3 dB speech
amplification. It did not find a consciousness machine. Different bluestone layouts produced
inaudible changes, leading the authors to doubt that sound motivated their arrangement.

The phrase “95–120 Hz infrasound” is technically wrong: infrasound is below about 20 Hz. No credible
study links standing-wave nodes to cremations or shows reliable hallucinations from Stonehenge's
acoustics. Many cremations also predate the main sarsen architecture by centuries.

Some Preseli rocks and bluestones make distinctive tones when struck. That supports a tentative
ritual-selection hypothesis, not a tuned pentatonic scale, pitch-based quarry selection, harmonic
placement, or nervous-system targeting.

LiDAR and geophysics reveal a complex ceremonial landscape, but not one proven geometric figure.
Published chronology describes episodic rebuilding over roughly 1,500 years, not one blueprint
transmitted unchanged across 20–25 generations.

No source supports the video's claimed decline from 3.2 cm to 12.7 cm placement error or two-
millimetre stone tolerances. The argument assumes a perfect acoustic design and then treats later
deviations from that imagined target as evidence of forgotten knowledge.

Stonehenge was not carefully “shut down” around 1600 BCE. Activity continued to roughly 1520 BCE,
and the Y and Z holes accumulated natural sediment. Documented stone falls occurred in 1797, 1900,
and 1963, contradicting the story that ancient custodians gently laid them down.

The genuine engineering remains exceptional. Most sarsens came from West Woods; many bluestones
came from west Wales; and the Altar Stone came from northeast Scotland. Neolithic ropes, sledges,
timber structures, ramps, levers, stone tools, and coordinated labour make transport and shaping
formidable but feasible, rather than inexplicable.

The verdict is severe: the alleged AI study is unsupported and probably fabricated or
misattributed. The video combines authentic archaeology with old speculation, false terminology,
invented precision, and untraceable experts. Stonehenge is remarkable without a fictional paper.
