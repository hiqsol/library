# How Computer Science Students Can Build Engineering Skill Beyond Coursework

[article](../../articles/the-coding-gopher/2026-11-06-computer-science-beyond-the-degree.md)

The Coding Gopher argues that a computer science degree is useful but insufficient preparation for
software work when students focus only on grades and narrowly scoped assignments.

## Core argument
- Autograded exercises teach bounded concepts but often omit architecture, deployment, failure
  diagnosis, changing requirements, real users, and long-term maintenance.
- AI performs well on many structured coding tasks, increasing the value of defining problems,
  validating results, integrating systems, and taking responsibility for outcomes.
- Theory and practical engineering should complement each other rather than compete.

## Recommended skills
- Learn shells, editors, Git internals, debuggers, profilers, build systems, and containers.
- Take operating-systems, compiler, networking, and database-internals courses where possible.
- Build one technically substantial project with tests, benchmarks, documentation, and explicit
  trade-offs instead of relying only on tutorial clones.
- Seek internships, open-source work, research, or campus projects that involve ownership and users.
- Maintain old code after its original context has faded.

The array-versus-linked-list example correctly shows why Big-O notation alone does not predict
performance. Cache locality and pointer chasing matter, but the uncited 20-fold slowdown is
benchmark-dependent.

MIT's *Missing Semester* supports the broad claim that curricula can underemphasize tool fluency.
The video's student anecdotes are not established by the official course page.

## Important corrections
The attribution to Erik Brynjolfsson is unsupported: his cited AI field study concerned 5,172
customer-support agents, not computer-science assignments. Claims about 90 of 100 résumés, 90% of
computer science being hidden, and "a million times" more value are rhetoric, not measured findings.

Grades can still matter for scholarships, internships, and graduate admissions. Framework apps can
demonstrate deep engineering through users, security, observability, deployment, and maintenance.
Internship prestige matters less than mentorship, ownership, and feedback.

The durable lesson is to combine academic foundations with systems knowledge and sustained practice.
AI makes judging generated work more important; these skills are not automation-proof.
