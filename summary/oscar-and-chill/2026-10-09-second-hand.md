intermittent, but still deliberately heard.

The narrator says the car and keys could be transferred, yet part of the absent person remains in

memory. Selling the car would therefore mean relinquishing an environment where the relationship
still feels present.

A prospective buyer checks under the hood and asks why the car has been kept so long. The narrator
nearly explains the places and history attached to it, but gives the safer answer that the engine is
fine. The scene contrasts measurable mechanical condition with private emotional value.

When the buyer asks whether the narrator is ready, the answer is “Not today.” This is postponement,
not closure. The final return to the unreliable radio leaves the machine and the grief unresolved.

The title links a used object changing hands, keys passed during a sale, the two hands on the cover,
and memory as indirect contact. The narrator can reach the missing person only second hand—through
the car, radio, remembered habits, and song.

The track's strength is its concrete emotional object. Its limitation is a sparse, archetypal story
with little biographical context. The subdued electronic arrangement and repetition sustain the
mood, while the absence of verified lyrics prevents confident analysis of obscured wording or exact
